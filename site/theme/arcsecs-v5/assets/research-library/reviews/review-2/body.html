Abstract

ArcSecs develops a physical-process cosmology on nonmaterial location coordinates and a constant ordering parameter. Its twelve investigator-defined foundations require positive photon mass, post-emission slowing, relational physical motion and a proposed reduction in inferred astronomical distances and sizes. The central C-01 hypothesis asks whether the collective matter distribution of a cosmic domain establishes the maximum initial speed of light, potentially with different constitutive conditions in other domains. This edition integrates the original investigations, the previous consolidated review, and six curated independent research branches into a common forward-model specification. We distinguish characteristic, group, phase, causal and separation speeds; specify a retarded matter-sourced regulator and impedance-matched electromagnetic response; formulate a positive-gap transport control with a physical reservoir; and define operational clock, spectral, pulse, angular and luminosity observables. The proposed moving material regulator is a research candidate, not a rescaled conventional distance curve. New analytical results establish the minimum energy loss required to cross into a higher-ceiling environment without reacceleration for fixed-gap radiation. In a linear-wall control, constant momentum drag sufficient for monotone slowing cannot carry a near-ceiling packet through a sufficiently large ceiling increase. Four independent trajectory integrations, four energy-accounting checks, four reachability checks and sixteen endpoint identities verify the restricted benchmark. The result motivates a dynamically coupled gap and reservoir or an alternative causal history mechanism, not a prescribed speed clamp. Existing distance anchors, time-dilation and lensing constraints remain mandatory. The manuscript supplies a unified parameter and observable interface, corrected extreme-object sensitivity relations, a prioritized validation plan and an evidence ledger. No common parameter set has yet been demonstrated to reproduce all relevant observations.

Keywords: ArcSecs; C-01; Machian inertia; massive photons; photon history; constitutive fields; causal propagation; physical clocks; recession; optical transport; cosmic domains.

Research charter and scope

The project is organized around the following twelve working foundations, supplied by the investigator. They are retained as research premises, not restated as experimental findings. Historical source reports use an eleven-item charter in places; those sources are preserved verbatim in the archive rather than silently renumbered.

  1. Photons have mass.
  2. Time is constant.
  3. Light slows down over time.
  4. Time is not physical; it is the “When.”
  5. Space is not physical; it is the “Where.”
  6. Everything physically existing at any moment occupies area or extent and has mass.
  7. Spacetime is not physical.
  8. Light is not the fastest in the universe.
  9. Objects can separate faster than causality; nonphysical time, space and spacetime cannot physically stretch.
  10. The size of the universe is unknown.
  11. The age of the universe is unknown.
  12. Distant objects are smaller and closer than they appear.

These requirements govern the search for candidate mechanisms. They do not exempt any model from empirical testing. In particular, Foundation 9 is interpreted operationally as permitting separation rates exceeding a specified signal-speed ceiling; it does not establish locally transmitted information outside that ceiling. Foundation 12 requires comparison with independently specified conventional distance estimates and a joint fit to angles, brightness, spectra and timing. Its scope cannot be established by assuming the desired distance correction in advance. Foundation 8 is compatible with a distinction between old photons and a faster field mode below a local causal maximum, but an all-sector limit and mode ordering must be derived. “Mass” requires an explicit distinction between invariant rest mass and energy, momentum or gravitational response. A localized quantum state is not necessarily a rigid body with a classical radius.

What, where and when

Physical entities and their interactions comprise the proposed ontology; position labels 𝐱\mathbf{x} and ordering parameter TT describe where and when events occur. A clock is a physical system with measurable phase or transition count. The choice not to regard mathematical spacetime as a substance does not itself invalidate relativistic predictions: the alternative must predict those same observed clock, motion and gravity measurements wherever they have been verified. A preferred ordering parameter alone does not give a laboratory an operational measurement of absolute uniform velocity. The present SI numerical value c0=299792458c_0=299\,792\,458 m/s is a unit-defined local reference, not an independent derivation of a cosmic constant. Differences in nature must appear in dimensionless ratios or comparisons of distinct physical processes.

C-01: the origin of a local emission-speed ceiling

The two-spacecraft thought experiment

Imagine two spacecraft surviving from different long-disappeared cosmic bubbles. Each retains an inherited uniform trajectory from an earlier epoch of physical separation, without a continuing thrust. Neither detects an acceleration merely by being in uniform motion. In a chosen common coordinate description, ships moving toward one another with signed velocities +u+u and −u-u close at rate 2u2u. For example u=0.9Cu=0.9C gives a pair closing rate 1.8C1.8C, without either coordinate velocity exceeding CC. Opposing causal fronts may likewise close at 2C2C without transmitting one signal at that rate. The thought experiment motivates the distinction between physical motion and signal propagation; it does not establish unbounded single-body velocity or additive emission velocities. Different historical domains provide a setting in which to ask what physical environment establishes the emission ceiling.

Investigator’s clarified intention. The central question is not whether light goes at 4c04c_0, but what physically establishes the maximum speed at which newly emitted light begins in our present cosmic environment, and why it should begin at that measured maximum rather than begin faster and subsequently slow to it. The proposed answer is a collective gravitational/inertial influence of the cosmic domain’s matter distribution. A hollow domain, material boundary, or exterior might have different constitutive conditions. This clarification supersedes interpretations that use the thought experiment as evidence of single-photon superluminal propagation.

The hypothesis is related in spirit to Machian inertia, including Sciama’s proposal that inertia could arise from inductive effects of distant matter [1]. This is historical motivation only. A constitutive field must supply actual equations, conservation laws, and observable predictions; ordinary gravitational attraction is not a strict speed ceiling. In Newtonian gravity an exactly spherical, uniform shell produces zero net force at every interior point, although the gravitational potential relative to infinity is nonzero. Invoking a universal potential as a locally measurable field additionally requires fixing the potential offset physically. C-01 therefore investigates a physical environmental variable, not merely a choice of gravitational potential zero.

In a changing environment a monotonic history is an additional physical requirement, not implied by a local ceiling.

Physical fields, finite-body screening and photon mass

Common-field candidate and atomic standards

The original A-05c report (Part IX) explores a scalar field ψ\psi coupled to electromagnetism and gravitational propagation. Its representative quadratic screened equation has the schematic form

K∂T2ψ−S∇2ψ+(ms2+gρ)ψ=f,K\partial_T^2\psi-S\nabla^2\psi+(m_s^2+g\rho)\psi=f,

with positive kinetic and gradient coefficients K,SK,S. The local equilibrium response and screening length are not sufficient to determine an actual finite object solution. One must solve the boundary problem, with the relevant range ℓ=S/(ms2+gρ)\ell=\sqrt{S/(m_s^2+g\rho)} and size ratio q=R/ℓq=R/\ell. Small qq can mean weak screening even for a high local density. This distinction is a central finding of Parts XI–XII. A-05c did not find a stable parameter set satisfying its combined redshift, spectroscopy, lensing and multimessenger constraints [A1].

Part XII examined a chameleon-like inverse-power potential and a density-driven symmetry-transition potential. Under the specified linear-density coupling and fixed gradient coefficient, its comparison bound limited the solar central field depletion to about 9.03×10−69.03\times10^{-6}, despite an effective local-minimum estimate q⊙≈2.07×105q_\odot\approx2.07\times10^5. The estimated minimum was not actually attained in the finite object. The leading scalar optical bending could nevertheless be about 1.66 arcseconds, opposite the desired attractive deflection for the chosen coupling. These findings exclude particular parameterized implementations, not every nonlinear scalar theory.

Part XIII investigated screening the electromagnetic coupling instead of the scalar. For a homogeneous, fixed-e,me,ℏe,m_e,\hbar response with the chosen sign of permittivity coupling, generating positive propagation redshift entails a logarithmic fine-structure change at least as large as that redshift. A gated medium additionally produces optical boundaries. Part XIV smoothed the density profile and solved a continuous environmental gate; at amplitude 10−510^{-5} the example ray at half a 100-kpc halo radius had approximately 1.814 arcseconds of extra deflection. Changing a step to a smooth gradient does not eliminate refraction. A cosmological model cannot independently tune intergalactic optical evolution, atomic frequencies, and gravitational lensing without justifying each interaction.

Positive-mass electromagnetic sector

A positive rest-mass hypothesis must distinguish the measurable photon energy E=ℏωE=\hbar\omega from a positive mass gap. A local massive-vector candidate can be represented by a gauge-invariant Stueckelberg construction (Part XIX); a scalar compensator transforms with the vector potential so that a combination of vector potential and scalar derivative is gauge invariant. For the matched transverse and longitudinal coefficients used there, the modes have local dispersion

The phase velocity is not the information speed. A finite-frequency positive-gap group packet never reaches CC exactly in this dispersion. When the local dispersion has the usual relativistic form with characteristic speed CC, the rest-energy proxy is ℏΩ=mγC2\hbar\Omega=m_\gamma C^2. Across domains, fixed gap and fixed mass are different assumptions. Photon rest mass is not established merely by radiation pressure, gravitational bending, localization or energy transfer. Proca-like electrodynamics is subject to strong model-dependent upper limits [2]; alternative mass-generation mechanisms require distinct tests [3].

The matched vector theory also gives a static Yukawa potential, proportional to e−r/ℓY/re^{-r/\ell_Y}/r, with ℓY=C/Ω\ell_Y=C/\Omega. A dynamical theory must preserve charge continuity, positive mode energies and observed longitudinal and static-electric limits. The earlier reduced-mode energy exchange checks do not establish a complete interacting quantum theory.

Constant ordering and physical clocks

Let the phase rate of a particular clock species jj be νj(𝐱,𝐯,T)\nu_j(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v},T). Its observed count is Nj=∫νjdTN_j=\int\nu_j\,dT. A clock-rate change can then be described as a physical-process change in the proposed ontology without treating the ordering parameter itself as material. This is a representation, not an empirical alternative until its dependence on environment and velocity is derived. A successful model must account for optical, microwave, nuclear and unstable-particle clocks, including moving muon decay, rather than only perturb electronic orbitals. Atomic emission and detection must be solved in the same model as photon propagation.

Physical recession and energy transport

Astronomical distances, sizes and brightness

Distance definitions and a 1 Mpc control

An arcsecond measures angle; a parsec is a geometric parallax distance convention (approximately 3.26 light-years). A light-year is a defined length, not a statement that every photon travels that length each year. Distinguish present physical separation, emission separation, path length, a constant-speed travel-time estimate, angular diameter distance DAD_A, and luminosity distance DLD_L. These are different observables or inferences. If a stationary source and receiver are actually 1 Mpc apart and the photon’s independently measured average travel speed is v‾\bar v, then

Dinferred=c0τ=Dactualc0v‾.D_{\mathrm{inferred}}=c_0\tau=D_{\mathrm{actual}}\frac{c_0}{\bar v}.

A photon that starts at c0c_0 and slows exponentially in age, v=c0e−γτv=c_0e^{-\gamma\tau}, traverses D=(c0/γ)(1−e−γτ)D=(c_0/\gamma)(1-e^{-\gamma\tau}). If its arrival speed is half the initial speed, Dinferred/D=2ln2=1.38629D_{\mathrm{inferred}}/D=2\ln 2=1.38629. Thus a true 1 Mpc distance would be inferred as 1.386 Mpc from that measured travel time. A globally changing characteristic speed which was faster in the past than today instead gives the opposite sign in a direct time-of-flight estimate. Real astronomical travel ages are usually unknown. An actual redshift-distance prediction requires calculating observed frequency and the chosen conventional inference procedure; a travel-time example is not a distance measurement.

Calibration must establish dimensionless combinations from independent measurements rather than set the desired answer. Strict emission at the ceiling, positive gap, and strict universal age slowing form a central mathematical tension. Maintaining all three requires a revised physical dispersion or nonlocal-history mechanism with a causal local completion. The research should retain a viable approximation if exact equality is not demonstrated, labeling the departure from the charter requirement.

Discussion and conclusion

ArcSecs now has an integrated research architecture rather than six independent explanatory narratives. Its principal question is C-01: whether the local emission ceiling can arise from the physical distribution of cosmic matter and its collective inertial response. A retarded regulator with impedance-matched electromagnetic constitutive coefficients offers a defined route to investigate that possibility. A positive regulator amplitude within a hollow cavity is mathematically compatible with zero ordinary Newtonian shell force; this does not establish the existence of a wall or derive the observed speed ceiling. A material flow on fixed coordinates supplies a precise physical-recession research direction, while the operational clock and ray endpoint maps distinguish spectral redshift from measured temporal stretching.

The new Part XXX calculation strengthens the wall-crossing requirement: a tiny fixed gap makes passive drag inefficient at reducing speed, and monotone crossing into a larger characteristic speed demands a severe energy loss. Constant drag strong enough to satisfy monotonicity in the illustrative wall stops the packet inside it. A dynamic gap or causal history field must therefore be derived and account for energy, recoil, dispersion and detector response. These results narrow the permissible implementations of the proposed aging mechanism without assuming a favorable empirical outcome. The one-megaparsec example demonstrates a travel-time inference bias; it is not a proof that angular or luminosity distances are overestimated. Any reduction of apparent extreme-object sizes must follow from an anchor-calibrated optical and timing model.

The immediate research priority is an autonomous retarded matter-regulator–radiation–reservoir system with stable characteristics and an actual physical emission and history mechanism. Advance the six branches together through the validation gates, not by adding unrelated fitted effects. The twelve foundations and C-01 clarification remain the research charter; no complete observationally successful implementation is yet demonstrated. The revised paper is intended for review, correction and continued constructive mathematical development.
