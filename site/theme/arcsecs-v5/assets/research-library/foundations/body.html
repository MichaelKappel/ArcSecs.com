ArcSecs Twelve Principles — authorial charter and test targets

AUTHORITATIVE CHARTER VERSION: 12 · November 23, 2026. This document supersedes the project’s prior eleven-principle enumeration. The change inserts a distinct age of the universe is unknown commitment at #11 and moves the formerly numbered #11 distance-and-size position to #12. The original 41 dated research manuscripts remain archival primary project records; their reproduction of the old enumeration must not override the present charter.

The sponsor’s twelve stated foundations

  1. Photons have mass.
  2. Time is constant.
  3. Light slows down over time.
  4. Time does not physically exist; it is the “When.”
  5. Space does not physically exist; it is the “Where.”
  6. All that physically exists at any moment occupies area and has mass at that moment.
  7. Spacetime does not physically exist.
  8. Light is not the fastest thing in the universe.
  9. Things move apart from each other faster than causality; time, space, or spacetime cannot be physically stretched because they are not physical substances to stretch.
  10. The size of the universe is unknown.
  11. The age of the universe is unknown.
  12. Objects in the universe are smaller and closer than they appear.

Technical interpretation and test objectives — separate from the charter

  1. Photon mass. Photons have directly observed energy and momentum and an equivalent mass contribution. Nonzero intrinsic invariant rest mass is a separate, not-yet-positively-detected proposition requiring a distinctive experiment.
  2. Constant time. Formulate one progression parameter T and derive variable physical clock-process rates, while preserving measured oscillator and particle-lifetime data.
  3. Slowing light. Test whether photon group speed changes over propagation history. Distinguish this from changing spectral energy, plasma dispersion and source effects. Specify a destination for exchanged energy and momentum.
  4. When. Event order is not a material fluid; investigate mechanisms behind clock counts.
  5. Where. Spatial coordinates describe physical relations rather than a stretchable object.
  6. Physical mass and extent. Define mass and occupied region operationally by entity; measured quantum localization is not necessarily a rigid classical boundary.
  7. No physically existing spacetime substrate. Seek physical entity/interaction mechanisms, not a material-fabric explanation. A coordinate framework is not automatically a force carrier.
  8. Beyond light speed. Design a measurable physical transfer-channel test; faster separation alone does not demonstrate faster information transmission.
  9. Super-causal separation and no stretching. Specify relative physical separation rates, instrumental signals and causality definitions without reifying geometry.
  10. Unknown total size. Distinguish a survey’s observable reach from total physical extent.
  11. Unknown total age. Treat any single numeric cosmic age as conditional on a cosmic-history model. Preserve measured stellar and nuclear chronometers, lookback inferences, and observational lower bounds; construct an independent age/chronology test and consider external bubble histories. The age of everything that exists is not established by a finite cosmic survey.
  12. Closer and smaller objects. Audit distance/size inferences and foreground misclassifications. Some named sources have proved far closer, while spectroscopic high-redshift sources require separate testing. The categorical project position guides research; the supporting evidence must identify the affected populations rather than claim every source was reclassified.

Editorial mandate: Advance ArcSecs research without re-litigating its purpose. Scientific mandate: Keep direct instrumental results and limits intact; clearly label model interpretations, premises and incomplete mechanisms. An authorial principle alone is not a verified positive detection.

Age versus size — separate research questions

  • #10 asks how extensive the physical cosmos is, including regions beyond our observational boundary.
  • #11 asks how long its physical history extends, including potentially prior cosmic domains or cycles. Neither a modeled observable-horizon radius nor a model-based 13.8-billion-year age establishes an unconditional total.
  • #12 asks whether particular astronomical distances and dimensions are overestimated after revisiting propagation and source classification. This does not logically follow merely from either unknown total size or unknown total age.
