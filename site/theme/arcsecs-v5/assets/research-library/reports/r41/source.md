# R41 — Source-Anchored Attractive Fields, Causal Propagation, and an Exterior Bubble Candidate

**ArcSecs independent working study, 23 November 2026.** Status: research prototype, AI-assisted synthesis, not peer reviewed and not an observational discovery. This export-friendly primary report deliberately contains no embedded equations, LaTeX, code blocks, or numerical calculation tables. Formal derivations, runnable code, and synthetic data reside in separate companion files.

**ArcSecs editorial reading key:** This report accepts the eleven ArcSecs propositions as the project's research premises, not as findings independently proved by this numerical experiment. It develops a physical-interaction candidate consistent with the project's non-material view of time, space, and spacetime. Measurements from established experiments are preserved as measurements, while their causal interpretations are separately identified. The analysis is affirmative and technical rather than a debate over the sponsor's chosen philosophical starting point.

**interpretation-versus-observation checkpoint:** A laboratory records accelerations, phase shifts, signal arrival events, photon trajectories, or oscillator counts. A mathematical explanation is an interpretation of those records. Agreement with a known measurement cannot prove a chosen microscopic field unless a distinctive prediction is subsequently tested. A working numerical program proves only that its implemented rules behave as reported, not that nature implements those rules.

## Executive abstract

**Objective.** Derive and computationally demonstrate a bounded attractive physical interaction with finite-speed propagation, a source and receiver that recoil, an explicitly energetic and momentum-carrying intermediary, and a test case representing influence from a hypothetical neighboring cosmic domain. This work advances R38's entity/field ontology, R39's reciprocal photon momentum exchange, and R40's finite-speed mechanical messenger. R40's inward-moving messenger pushes a receiver away from the exterior source. The R41 task is to construct a distinct interaction that actually pulls toward that source, without declaring coordinates or time to be physical substances.

**Methods.** A one-dimensional, source-coupled scalar field is represented by energy-bearing, position-dependent degrees of freedom. Two localized physical bodies contribute field source density. The same coupling changes their momenta in response to the field gradient. The field has a finite propagation-speed parameter, a finite screening scale, positive kinetic and gradient-energy terms, and a negative source-interaction contribution to the closed-system energy. A translation-preserving spectral discretization supplies a field-momentum ledger; reversible symplectic integration limits energy drift. The numerical setup is synthetic and deliberately uses dimensionless choices rather than astronomical fits.

**Findings.** An interior receiver with a like-coupled source to its right initially experiences essentially no force, then accelerates toward that source after the source-created disturbance propagates across the gap. The source recoils in the opposite direction. The model's total momentum remains conserved to floating-point accuracy; total energy varies slightly because a finite numerical step only approximately preserves a continuous Hamiltonian, and its error falls by approximately a factor of four when the step is halved. The source-free and zero-coupling limits vanish; an opposite-sign coupling reverses the force; and symmetric exterior sources cancel the central receiver's net force. A source beyond an assigned computational domain boundary can produce an inward-communicated attractive influence. None of these outcomes confirms a real exterior universe, a physical cosmic wall, or an astronomical gravitational law.

**Conclusion.** A finite-speed attractive exchange need not be described by a material spacetime substrate. It can be stated instead as dynamics among physical sources, a physical mediator, and receivers, indexed by constant universal progression. The actual mediator, the coupling to photon energy, consistent three-dimensional gravitational optics, and the effect of a moving cosmic boundary remain unestablished. The next decisive advance is an experimentally distinguishable prediction with fitted parameters frozen before an independent holdout observation.

## 2. The problem transferred from R38, R39, and R40

R38 specifies physical entities, composites, excitations, field states, interactions, and relations. Location and event order are descriptive relations. R39 uses a physical, reciprocal momentum-exchange force, but its potential acts instantaneously in the simplified calculations. R40 adds finite signal travel through a messenger carrying energy and momentum. That messenger reaches a receiver and pushes it in the messenger's incident direction. The sign of its impulse is not an optional detail: an incoming messenger from a source to the right gives a receiver a leftward mechanical push, opposite to the intended attractive effect.

A scientifically useful ArcSecs candidate should therefore meet several simultaneous requirements. Its attraction must arise from a stated source/receiver coupling, not from a narrative that an unspecified gravity 'pulls.' Its influence must not arrive before an allowed causal front. The interaction carrier must have states, energy and momentum that can be counted in a closed-system ledger. A force on a receiver must have corresponding backreaction on the source and field. The interaction must vanish under a controlled zero-coupling limit. It must not imply an arbitrary gravitational or electromagnetic observation that has not been checked. And it must distinguish an exterior pressure source, a single outside attractor, and a surrounding shell, because they produce different patterns of force.

This work chooses a finite-range scalar mediator strictly as a *candidate physical interaction*. The term field refers to an energetically accountable physical state associated with sources and receivers. It is not a claim that an empty coordinate grid, time interval, or spacetime manifold has material properties. A physical mediator can be hypothetical while the position and time variables used to describe it remain non-material.

## 3. What physically exists in the candidate

The candidate contains physical bodies possessing mass, location and momentum. They source an interaction through a charge-like parameter. A distributed field stores energy and momentum and communicates source changes across a finite speed. Each interaction occurs when the field and a local receiver affect one another. A reference time index labels the order of those events but has neither inertia, surface tension, energy storage nor an independent mechanical force. The chosen one-dimensional numerical positions serve as the 'Where.' They do not stretch, flow, attract anything or carry charges. The field is the physical candidate, not the coordinates.

The source and receiver charges are illustrative, not measurements of new particles. The current program does not declare a fundamental field particle's positive invariant rest mass. It assigns a screening frequency to the field, and physical rest and energy accounting to the modeled material bodies. Source-coupled field energy is real within the defined model, but its true gravitational mass conversion and compatibility with atomic scattering remain separate investigations. This distinction matters for ArcSecs Principle 6: a modeled energy density should not be mistaken for direct detection of a new material entity merely because it appears in a computer.

The computational boundary is just a marker dividing a modeled interior and exterior. It does not establish that the observable universe possesses an edge. The same physical source-and-receiver rules are used regardless of which side of the marker holds the source; that makes an external-domain hypothesis a specific geometry of a candidate interaction instead of an unsupported assertion about literal bubbles.

## 4. The attractive interaction and its sign

A scalar source generates a field that becomes larger near the source after propagation has had time to establish the interaction. A like-coupled receiver experiences a force toward the region where that source-generated field is stronger. In the static limit, and for the defined positive coupling convention, the effective interaction energy of a separated pair decreases when their distance shrinks. Thus source and receiver attract each other. Opposite signs under the same convention yield a repulsive interaction. The force sign is a consequence of the chosen coupling term, not an empirical demonstration that gravity is scalar.

The physical field is not a passive bookkeeping device. While it evolves, field excitations transport energy and momentum; a body absorbs momentum through the coupling, and the field carries the corresponding opposite momentum. In the model's closed periodic test domain, the initial total momentum is zero and the sum of body and field momentum stays near zero after the distant interaction arrives. Since total energy includes a negative interaction term, simply adding particle kinetic energy to the positive free-field energy would create an apparent energy violation. The full energy ledger is essential.

The implementation uses smoothed sources rather than ideal zero-width delta points. That is a mathematical and physical regularization choice; it makes source distributions locally extended and allows the force to be computed without a divergent self-field. The source width must not be interpreted as a measured radius of an actual fundamental particle. An externally supported theory would need to explain the width from a physically motivated source structure and test its dependence against scattering observations.

## 5. Causal propagation versus static field approximations

A static field profile is a description of the response after source disturbances have propagated. It does not authorize an instantaneous change at distant locations. In R41 the field begins without a source-generated profile and develops over constant universal progression. The receiver cannot respond appreciably to the distant source until a wavefront crosses the relevant physical separation. The program uses a finite propagation speed supplied as an input, without identifying it with, above, or below the vacuum light benchmark.

The numerical spectral scheme represents localized sources through smooth Gaussian profiles. Gaussian tails are technically nonzero at arbitrarily distant coordinates, and a spectral method does not give an exact compact-support theorem at finite resolution. Therefore the claim in the computation is **effective delayed arrival above a stated threshold**, not an exact proof of zero field outside a mathematically sharp light cone. Improving this point requires compact-support source kernels, explicit conservative numerical fluxes and grid-convergence checks. The external reproduction guide records that limitation and the present detection thresholds.

The causal constraint is especially important when applying the model to hypothetical exterior domains. A field already established over an ancient cosmic history and an abrupt message emitted now from outside the observable domain are different things. The latter needs a travel mechanism and arrival interval. Neither the phrase 'another bubble' nor a freely adjustable signal speed explains the actual propagation physics. A proposed faster-than-light channel would require a new physical source, receiver, dispersion law and timing experiment, and cannot be inferred from this toy model.

## 6. What the numerical trial actually demonstrates

The baseline trial places a receiver at the central reference position and an exterior-labeled source to its right at a separation of twelve synthetic length units. The field's propagation parameter is two synthetic length units per progression unit. The source and receiver each carry positive coupling and large mechanical masses to keep their displacement small during the short observation window. The field begins at zero and is then driven by both localized sources. Their self-forces cancel by symmetric regularization. For the first few progression units, the receiver's force from the distant source remains below the chosen numerical detection threshold.

Near the modeled travel-time window, the receiver registers a positive force directed toward the right-hand exterior source. Its accumulated momentum becomes positive while the exterior source's momentum becomes negative. Field momentum temporarily carries any difference between these two contributions. By the end of the demonstration window the interaction is attractive, not a messenger impact that pushes away from the source. Halving the numerical time step reduces the magnitude of the total-energy deviation by roughly four, as expected for a second-order time integrator in the tested regime. The total-momentum error remains close to floating-point noise because the spectral coupling preserves translation symmetry to high accuracy.

In the companion experiment, changing the exterior source from right to left reverses the attractive impulse. Reversing only one source's coupling sign produces repulsion rather than attraction. Removing the external source eliminates its influence, and a zero-coupling setup produces no field. If two otherwise identical sources sit symmetrically to the left and right, their pulls cancel at the exact central reference point. A receiver displaced from this center need not have zero force; in a finite-range model it can experience a spatial gradient. This is a useful starting pattern for asking whether an approximately surrounding cosmic environment could create relative acceleration, but a genuine three-dimensional shell requires its own derivation.

The synthetic program has no observed data in its state vector, and its arbitrary numerical speed and coupling values are not converted into megaparsecs, solar masses, gravitational constants or astronomical fit quality. A claim that a cosmic bubble has exerted a measured gravitational force would require observational data that have not been supplied by the simulation. No such detection is asserted.

## 7. Conserved energy and momentum are not optional

A force proposed as physical must come with a fully closed inventory of what supplies it. Source recoil and receiver acceleration are not sufficient if the carrier's stress or momentum flux is omitted. The R41 model stores field momentum in the correlation between the field's local progression-rate change and its spatial gradient. Particle momentum is recorded separately, and the sum is nearly constant in the numerical evolution. The field's positive kinetic, spatial-gradient and screening energy is reported apart from the negative source-field interaction contribution and the bodies' kinetic energy. The complete sum is approximately conserved under the stated time integrator, not exactly invariant to unlimited decimal places.

The mechanical kinetic energies of the source and receiver increase after attraction becomes active. That energy is balanced by changes in the field and interaction energy. It does not arrive from a time variable, an elastic space fabric or a newly created energy reservoir. For future cosmic applications, a source fixed in an external background would make the studied subsystem open, so its apparent failure to conserve matter-only energy could reflect work performed by the larger system. A fully closed greater-cosmos model must include moving outside sources, field boundary flux, any domain-wall energy, and any feedback on interior mass-energy.

The present periodic spectral box is a practical solver boundary and not a declaration that our cosmic environment is periodic. It prevents an explicit surface-flux term only because the computational edges are identified mathematically. A future physically bounded wall requires an independent boundary condition, surface-force law and flux ledger. Directly inserting a wall into the existing toy without specifying that energy transfer would not be a legitimate physical derivation.

## 8. A neighboring-bubble universe as an exterior source

For a testable scenario, represent a neighboring domain as an external collection of physical sources rather than as an adjacent patch of material spacetime. That collection can create an interaction field if its contents carry the hypothesized coupling. The model then predicts that a localized change in the outside source affects interior receivers after a finite travel interval. The sign of the force points toward the outside source for the tested like-sign scalar coupling. An asymmetric neighboring domain therefore produces a directional influence and potentially differential acceleration between interior objects at different positions.

A single outside domain does not automatically cause uniform expansion of every distance within ours. Much of its force could accelerate the domain as a whole; what observers could measure inside is the remaining differential gradient. Symmetric exterior sources can cancel at a central position. A concentric distribution under a finite-range interaction might produce an outward relative acceleration away from the center, as developed in the book's separate static screened-shell study, but transferring the one-dimensional results to that three-dimensional claim requires spherical integration with source motion and field backreaction. Ordinary inverse-square gravity's spherical shell cancellation is a distinct benchmark and cannot be replaced by assertion.

Several mechanisms must remain separate until a common physical law exists: a physical wall under a pressure difference, a localized neighbor creating tidal attraction, a surrounding screen-like source field, and a collision or shock at a boundary. All may be investigated from the ArcSecs research perspective. They are not interchangeable reasons for redshift or cosmic motion. Neither the model nor astronomical surveys presently establish an external bubble universe; a publication-quality claim needs a signal, a pre-registered analysis and rejection of instrumental or local astrophysical explanations using data rather than rhetoric.

## 9. Does an attractive field explain photon deflection?

Not yet. The R41 field couples to idealized mechanical bodies through a chosen scalar charge. It does not automatically couple to light's physical energy-momentum, distinguish photon polarization, reproduce an observed solar-limb bending angle, generate Cassini's radio-link frequency shift, or produce black-hole photon capture. Those measurements belong in the intended verification program. R39 reproduced a weak-field bending coefficient after choosing it to agree with a benchmark; R41 does not inherit an independently derived explanation merely because both studies use words such as physical field.

The next photon extension must derive a term that transfers energy and transverse momentum between an electromagnetic wave packet and the source-anchored mediator. The same rule should generate recoil and respect electric charge conservation, frequency measurements, laboratory dispersion bounds and multi-messenger arrival timing. If a new scalar couples only to rest-mass-like body charges, it may fail to deflect photons at all. Adding a photon charge by hand solely to reproduce an observed deflection would be parameter fitting, not an explanation of its microscopic origin.

The objective is a precise interaction, not an assertion that photons have already been demonstrated to possess a positive invariant rest mass. Radiation's measured energy and momentum are physical. The separate rest-mass question has upper bounds and remains an experimental task. This project can investigate a massive-photon hypothesis in parallel without presenting those upper bounds as affirmative detections.

## 10. Does it explain the changing rate of physical clocks?

Not yet. Physical clocks count transitions, and the ArcSecs constant-When model asks whether an external physical interaction modifies atomic or nuclear process rates without a physical manipulation of time. R41 contains no atomic bound-state Hamiltonian and no rate law for cesium, strontium, thorium, muons or other clocks. The bare presence of a scalar force cannot establish that the measured frequency gradients are caused by this field.

A future candidate should specify how the mediator changes internal binding energies or transition probabilities and calculate a ratio that could differ between two clock species under the same potential gradient. The MICROSCOPE experiment supplies an additional stringent constraint on composition-dependent acceleration. The operational results must be preserved no matter which interpretation eventually succeeds. An ArcSecs manuscript that treats every measured clock offset as a victory, without predicting how it arose, would still be missing the requested physical mechanism.

## 11. Required independent observational tests

The first experimental target is a force-range and composition test, not a full-sky cosmological fit. A candidate physical coupling strength and range should be defined in ordinary calibrated units and checked against torsion-balance, spacecraft tracking and equivalence-principle measurements. Published short-range experiments already limit additional attractive Yukawa-like interactions, with important dependence on range and material composition. The next paper must not choose a parameter after seeing its holdout data. It must state the source mass, distance, detector sensitivity, shielding and predicted acceleration before unblinding an independent dataset.

Second, the proposed carrier speed should be tested using modulated sources and independently timed receivers, with a clearly stated upper limit on response before the expected front. Third, differential force and time-delay predictions should be compared against Cassini spacecraft tracking and solar-limb deflection, while taking account of instrumentation and solar plasma effects. Fourth, if the coupling is supposed to influence cosmic distances, an explicit angular/redshift mapping and emission history must be built and compared against spectroscopically confirmed objects. Fifth, any neighboring-bubble imprint requires sky-direction controls and foreground checks, and a null result must be published as a useful limit rather than renamed as an unseen detection.

These are not invitations to reinstate relativity as the project's ontology. Existing theories' published numerical predictions and experiments can be used as calibrations or controls, but the explanatory candidate under development is the physical source-field-receiver interaction. If observational constraints exclude the proposed coupling at the needed strength, the correct scientific conclusion is that this particular candidate fails or needs a new mechanism. The program's general research questions can remain active without editing away the failed test.

## 12. Limits and alternative physical explanations within the research program

The current numerical model has one spatial dimension, periodic computational boundaries, fixed Gaussian source width and a scalar mediator whose existence is assumed for exploration. It has no independent test showing the carrier's microscopic physical identity. It has not implemented photons, atomic clocks, fluid walls, general three-dimensional moving mass distributions, radiation damping or nonlinear high-field gravitational phenomena. The chosen field speed and screening scale are freely selected. Momentum balance uses a spectral identity and energy balance is approximate at finite time step. The model has not reconstructed any telescope image or spacecraft telemetry.

An attractive sign is insufficient as proof of new gravity. Many scalar interaction theories can generate attractive effective forces, and serious experimental bounds apply to some of them. This study's novelty within ArcSecs is the transparent source/field/receiver accounting and its link to the prior R38–R40 program, rather than a discovery that no one has previously considered scalar attraction. Future work must treat the toy as a minimal reference architecture, avoid claims of observational confirmation, and seek an independent distinctive effect.

There is also a conceptual distinction between rejecting a literal material spacetime and dismissing mathematical differential geometry as a calculation language. This project does not use geometry as a material cause in its proposed interaction. Nevertheless, a published mathematical benchmark could correctly fit an experiment even if the book's ontology disputes its physical interpretation. Preserving that distinction protects the quality of ArcSecs evidence while keeping research effort focused on constructive mechanisms.

## 13. Evidence register: observation, inference and working hypothesis

**Observed in instrument records:** Cassini's radio tracking found a gravitationally associated fractional response with a published gamma-parameter fit near unity and an uncertainty near twenty-three parts per million [1]. MICROSCOPE compared the accelerations of titanium and platinum test masses and reported no detected differential violation at the quoted sensitivity [2]. Laboratory inverse-square-law measurements probed separations down to roughly fifty micrometers and constrained certain additional-force strength/range combinations [3,4]. A gravitational-wave signal and a gamma-ray burst were measured with a roughly 1.74-second arrival offset; propagation-speed constraints rely on source-emission assumptions [5]. None of these instruments directly observed a neighboring universe or a source-coupled scalar carrier.

**Model-dependent inference:** A specified effective fifth-force strength or range may be excluded by fitting those results with assumptions about coupling universality, screening, source composition and instrumental systematics. One must not apply constraints to an unspecified interaction with no mathematical rule. Similarly, the physical explanation for a measured clock-frequency difference is a theory-dependent assignment and should not be relabeled as a direct measurement of changing material time.

**ArcSecs proposal:** Matter and radiation might interact through physical source-anchored fields that propagate under constant progression and can be screened over cosmic distances. R41 supplies one mathematical implementation of a finite-speed attractive *mechanical* interaction. The presence of that physical field in nature remains unverified. The bigger-than-observable-universe source scenario is a hypothetical geometry, not astronomical evidence.

**Synthetic calculation only:** R41's numerical tests show delayed attractive force, reciprocity, energy convergence and cancellation controls. Its self-consistent energy and momentum bookkeeping demonstrates mathematical coherence of this limited model; it does not promote the model to established physics.

The report should be submitted for human review only with the full companion archive and with the verified source links included.

For a future peer-reviewed paper, the team must provide a permanent repository or archived DOI for the exact code version, a software environment lockfile, independently reproduced test output, an uncertainty budget, transparent prior and parameter selection, an unblinded held-out dataset, a statement of competing interests and funding, and a completed human authorship review. The proposed model also requires a dimensional analysis mapping the synthetic parameters into calibrated physical quantities before it can be compared to a measured effect. The named sources in this paper have been checked at the publication and abstract level, not all full texts or raw data reprocessed. External review is required before citation-level assertions are represented as independently replicated.

## 15. Specific next mission: R42

R42 should generalize the attractive exchange from one dimension to three-dimensional moving sources, derive its field stress and boundary flux, and calculate the effect of a surrounding spherical distribution versus an isolated neighboring source. It should then couple the field to a physical photon wave packet without adding a coefficient solely to fit the observed solar deflection. Separate the mechanical field interaction from changes in photon energy and speed. If the new field couples to clock transitions, pre-register a species-dependent test. The objective is a common source-and-coupling law that can yield a new observable, not another verbal explanation of why a calculation must be wrong.

The accepted deliverables must include a complete R42 report with source-linked references and clearly identified model claims, a separate readable methods guide, source-code tests and numerical output, and a proposed real-data holdout study. Preserve the current eleven ArcSecs research premises. Preserve all experimental measurements, including null results. Never present coordinates, space, time or spacetime as physical matter or a force-producing substance. Make no claim to have observed external bubbles, nonzero invariant photon rest mass, or superluminal signaling without a verified measurement.
