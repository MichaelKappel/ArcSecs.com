## **Abstract**

Precision atomic clocks at differing elevations and unstable elementary particles at relativistic speeds accumulate measurably different cycle counts and decay distributions. Standard general and special relativity interpret these discrepancies as geometric differences in proper time along distinct spacetime worldlines. This study evaluates the competing ArcSecs hypothesis: that time itself is a fundamentally constant universal progression parameter, whereas gravity and motion physically alter internal dynamical process rates without invoking a malleable temporal substance. We formulate two quantitative realizations without assuming stretchable time—a universal physical-process scaling model (Model A) and a composition-dependent, interaction-specific scaling model (Model B)—and benchmark both against standard relativity and a rate-invariance null control (Model C) across four independent observation classes: millimeter-to-kilometer optical atomic clock gravitational redshifts, space-borne maser and global navigation satellite comparisons, relativistic muon storage-ring decays, and stored-ion Doppler and dissimilar-clock null tests. Using nine pre-2022 calibration datasets and two held-out 2022–2023 millimeter-scale strontium optical clock datasets, we establish verbally that Model A is mathematically and observationally equivalent to standard relativity across all local measurements, yielding a calibration chi-squared statistic of 5.54 over nine degrees of freedom (p-value 0.785) and a held-out chi-squared of 0.55. Model B yields no out-of-sample improvement, incurs severe information-criterion penalties, and is disfavored by exact analytical Bayes factors exceeding ten billion under weakly informative priors, while Model C is decisively excluded. Consequently, Model A represents an empirically indistinguishable reinterpretation rather than a novel discovery, whereas Model B is excluded above current fractional bounds of parts in 10^5 to parts in 10^8 across tested sectors. We design a two-year co-located thorium-229 nuclear versus strontium-87 optical clock null experiment capable of probing differential nuclear-to-electronic gravitational couplings down to parts in 10^13.

## **1\. Introduction and Required Final Decision**

Evaluating the ArcSecs hypothesis—that time is fundamentally constant while gravity and motion alter physical processes rather than time itself—requires separating an ontological debate about what time is from an empirical question about what clocks and decaying particles measurably do. * **ArcSecs Model B (Composition- and Interaction-Dependent Process Scaling) — Outcome C (Excluded in tested parameter space) and Outcome D (Undecidable below current experimental precision floors):** Any mechanistic model in which gravity or motion couples differently to optical electronic transitions, microwave hyperfine transitions, weak-interaction lepton decays, or nuclear binding energies predicts violations of Local Position Invariance (LPI), Local Lorentz Invariance (LLI), and the Weak Equivalence Principle (WEP)9. Current calibration data exclude species-dependent rate deviations at 95 percent confidence above fractional bounds of 1.92 × 10^-4 in optical clocks, 4.81 × 10^-5 in microwave masers, 3.92 × 10^-8 in relativistic ion spectroscopy, and 1.61 × 10^-3 in weak muon decay1. ## **2\. Operational Definitions and Ontological Boundaries**

Preventing circularity—specifically, ensuring the ArcSecs framework does not smuggle a stretchable temporal substance back in through concealed definitions—requires defining "constant time" operationally and classifying every conceptual claim by its epistemic status.

### **2.1 Operational Definition of Constant Time**

The assertion that time is constant encompasses three distinct meanings in physics that must be separated rigorously to answer Primary Question 1\. First, as **strict event ordering** (epistemic class: operational definition), time denotes only a monotonic topological sequence of causal events along worldlines; pure ordering lacks an intrinsic metric scale and therefore cannot define a constant rate of progression. Second, as an **invariant universal progression parameter T** (epistemic class: hypothesis and coordinate foliation), time is operationally defined as a global, single-valued progression coordinate calibrated in SI seconds by an idealized unperturbed reference oscillator located at asymptotic spatial infinity (zero gravitational potential) and at rest relative to a specified global inertial background frame—such as the Earth-Centered Inertial frame for orbital metrology, the solar-system barycentric frame for annual tests, or the cosmological rest frame on large scales7. By construction, this reference progression parameter advances uniformly everywhere, independent of local matter distributions or clock velocities. Third, as an **unobservable reparametrization** (epistemic class: derived mathematics), if every physical length standard, light signal, atomic transition, nuclear state, and decay channel transforms universally so that no local coincidence or closed-path measurement can detect the preferred rest frame or absolute simultaneity slice of parameter T, then T functions mathematically as an unobservable coordinate gauge7.  
Two strictly defined experimental observables would establish that constant progression parameter T is physically distinct rather than an unobservable reparametrization7. The first is **non-universal rate scaling**, observed if two co-located physical systems governed by different fundamental interactions (such as a nuclear isomeric clock and an electronic optical clock, or an atomic oscillator and a weak-decay ensemble) exhibit a drifting ratio of accumulated counts when jointly transported through a changing gravitational potential or velocity state. The second is **preferred-frame anisotropy**, observed if clock-rate ratios or electromagnetic propagation phases vary with orientation or velocity relative to a cosmic rest frame, thereby violating Local Lorentz Invariance7. In the absence of either observable signature, constant universal time T remains empirically indistinguishable from relativistic coordinate time.

### **2.2 Event-Count Ratios Versus Metaphysical Substance**

Popular expositions frequently equate experimental measurements of "time dilation" with proof that time is an elastic physical medium that literally stretches or slows. Operationally, no physical apparatus measures time directly; every precision experiment compares dimensionless ratios of physical event counts5. In Bothwell et al. (2022), the directly measured physical observable is the atomic excitation fraction of roughly 100,000 ultracold strontium-87 atoms interrogated on an optical clock transition and imaged via fluorescence spectroscopy on an electron-multiplying charge-coupled device (EMCCD) camera at 6.04-micrometer vertical pixel resolution5. In Delva et al. (2018) and Herrmann et al. (2018), the direct observable is the carrier-phase and clock-bias residual time series between space-borne passive hydrogen masers and ground stations, audited against independent satellite laser ranging residuals2. In Bailey et al. (1977), the direct observable is the exponential arrival-time histogram of decay electrons recorded around the CERN storage ring against laboratory oscillator pulses6. In Botermann et al. (2014), the direct observable is the simultaneous resonance condition of collinear and anticollinear laser beams interrogating circulating lithium-7 ions4.  
As articulated in Harvey Brown's dynamical approach to relativity—extending J. S. Bell's 1976 constructive pedagogy—spacetime geometry need not be treated as a mysterious ether pushing clocks to run slow; instead, clocks behave identically across inertial and gravitational states because the underlying quantum field laws governing matter and radiation obey universal Lorentz covariance and universal gravitational coupling8. Describing the resulting dimensionless ratio of accumulated cycles as "geometric proper time" versus "universal physical-process scaling under constant progression T" is therefore a **philosophical interpretation**, not an empirical disagreement, so long as both frameworks yield identical dynamical ratios.

### **3.1 Class I: Optical Atomic Clock Gravitational Redshifts (Millimeter to Kilometer Scales)**

Optical atomic clocks have moved gravitational redshift testing from sounding rockets down to sub-millimeter tabletop ensembles17. At the centimeter scale, Chou et al. (2010) compared two aluminum-27 single-ion optical clocks at NIST, elevating one clock by 33 centimeters and measuring a net fractional frequency increase of positive 4.1 plus or minus 1.6 parts in 10^17, matching the general-relativistic prediction of 3.60 parts in 10^17 while also resolving second-order kinematic Doppler shifts at relative speeds below 10 meters per second1. At the hundred-meter scale, Takamoto et al. (2020) operated a pair of 18-digit transportable strontium-87 optical lattice clocks separated by 450 meters in altitude at Tokyo Skytree, constraining the LPI gravitational-redshift deviation parameter to positive 1.4 plus or minus 9.1 parts in 10^5—an order-of-magnitude advance in terrestrial clock tests rivaling orbital missions28. Follow-on transportable optical lattice summaries through 2025 document continued maturation of chronometric geodesy32.  
At the millimeter scale, Bothwell et al. (2022) resolved the gravitational redshift within a single 1-millimeter vertical sample of roughly 100,000 ultracold strontium-87 atoms trapped in a shallow optical lattice at JILA5. Achieving an atomic coherence decay time of 36.5 plus or minus 0.7 seconds (quality factor 3.6 × 10^16; rounded to 37 seconds in the NIST summary) and a differential fractional frequency uncertainty of 7.6 × 10^-21 over 92 hours of synchronous comparison between uncorrelated sub-regions (reported as roughly 90 hours in the NIST overview), the team extracted a raw weighted vertical frequency gradient of minus 1.00 plus or minus 0.12 parts in 10^19 per millimeter and a systematically corrected gradient of minus 0.98 plus or minus 0.23 parts in 10^19 per millimeter, closely matching the expected minus 1.09 parts in 10^19 per millimeter5. Because automated direct retrieval from the Nature publisher webpage was blocked during verification, all numerical budgets for Bothwell et al. (2022) were verified directly against the authors' complete arXiv manuscript and the official NIST technical summary5. Independently, Zheng et al. (2023) conducted a formally blinded laboratory test across five vertically arrayed strontium-87 ensembles in a single optical lattice at the University of Wisconsin–Madison3. After freezing all systematic corrections and revealing a pseudorandom blinding gradient of positive 3.7 × 10^-18 per centimeter, they measured a fractional frequency gradient of minus 12.4 plus or minus 0.7 (statistical) plus or minus 2.5 (systematic) parts in 10^-19 per centimeter (linear fit minus 11.9 plus or minus 2.6 parts in 10^-19 per centimeter), consistent with the predicted minus 10.9 parts in 10^-19 per centimeter, constraining LPI deviations to positive 0.13 plus or minus 0.23 and rejecting the zero-redshift hypothesis at 4.9 sigma across millimeter-to-centimeter separations3.

### **3.2 Class II: Transportable Clocks, GNSS Constellations, and Space-Borne Masers**

Macroscopic altitude and velocity baselines test the combined interplay of gravitational and kinematic rate scaling in operational aerospace systems. Hafele and Keating (1972) flew four cesium-beam atomic clocks around Earth on commercial jetliners, observing directional asymmetry relative to the underlying non-rotating inertial frame: flying eastward (adding aircraft speed to Earth's rotation) yielded a net clock loss, whereas flying westward (reducing inertial speed while maintaining altitude) yielded a measured gain of 273 plus or minus 7 nanoseconds38. On a sounding-rocket trajectory reaching roughly 10,000 kilometers altitude over a two-hour flight, Gravity Probe A (Vessot et al., 1980\) compared a space-borne hydrogen maser against ground masers via a continuous two-way microwave link that canceled first-order Doppler effects, verifying the combined relativistic frequency shift to a 1-sigma uncertainty of 7.0 × 10^-5 and constraining pure gravitational redshift deviations to 1.4 × 10^-49.  
In continuous global navigation infrastructure, Ashby (2002, 2003\) detailed how GPS satellites orbiting at a semi-major axis of 26,561.75 kilometers and circular speed of 3,873.83 meters per second experience a gravitational speedup of positive 45.79 microseconds per day and a second-order kinematic slowdown of minus 7.21 microseconds per day relative to Earth's rotating geoid1. Without the pre-launch factory clock offset of minus 4.4647 × 10^-10 compensating the net positive 38.58 microseconds per day (reported as 38.57 microseconds per day in Ashby's rounded summary), plus orbital eccentricity and Sagnac corrections, positioning errors would drift by roughly 11.6 kilometers per day (since 4 nanoseconds of timing error corresponds to roughly 1 meter of range)1. Complementing GPS rubidium-clock analyses covering 6,640 days of eccentric Block IIF satellite data45, the accidental 2014 injection of Galileo satellites GSAT0201 and GSAT0202 into elliptical orbits created a 400-nanosecond peak-to-peak periodic gravitational modulation2. Analyzing 1,008 days of onboard passive hydrogen maser data, Delva et al. (2018) bounded the LPI violation parameter to positive 0.19 plus or minus 2.48 parts in 10^5 (statistical uncertainty 1.32 × 10^-5; systematic uncertainty 1.91 × 10^-5, dominated by magnetic-field variations), improving upon Gravity Probe A by a factor of 5.62. Herrmann et al. (2018) independently analyzed the same spacecraft clocks and confirmed an uncertainty limit of 2.5 × 10^-5 (a fourfold to fivefold improvement over Gravity Probe A depending on single-clock conservatism versus combined three-clock modeling)18. Looking ahead, ESA reported in August 2025 that the Atomic Clock Ensemble in Space (ACES)—combining the laser-cooled cesium clock PHARAO with an active Space Hydrogen Maser on the ISS Columbus module at 400 kilometers altitude—targets a 30-month mission with microwave and optical links to reach 2–3 × 10^-6 redshift accuracy19; final ACES science bounds were not yet published by 22 November 2026 and are therefore excluded from quantitative fitting.

### **3.3 Class III: Relativistic Muon Lifetime and Accelerator Decay Experiments**

Because unstable muons decay strictly through the **weak nuclear interaction** rather than electromagnetic atomic binding, relativistic storage-ring lifetimes test whether motion scales weak-sector decay rates identically to optical and microwave clocks. At rest, the MuLan Collaboration (Tishchenko et al., 2013\) measured the positive muon lifetime at the Paul Scherrer Institute to 2.1969803 microseconds, fixing the Fermi coupling constant to 0.6 parts per million1. In flight, Bailey et al. (1977) stored positive and negative muons in the 7.0-meter-radius CERN Muon Storage Ring at the "magic" Lorentz factor of 29.327 (speed 0.99942 times the speed of light), where electric quadrupole focusing fields cancel spin-precession perturbations1. They measured laboratory decay lifetimes of 64.419 plus or minus 0.058 microseconds for positive muons and 64.368 plus or minus 0.029 microseconds for negative muons, agreeing closely with the relativistic expectation of 64.431 microseconds (using the modern MuLan rest lifetime) at minus 0.21 sigma and minus 2.17 sigma respectively1. Because the Nature publisher webpage for Bailey et al. (1977) was inaccessible during automated retrieval, these exact lifetime measurements were cross-verified across multiple independent peer-reviewed physics publications6. Crucially, the circulating muons sustained a transverse centripetal acceleration of 1.31 × 10^15 standard Earth gravities in the laboratory frame—corresponding to 1.12 × 10^18 standard gravities in the muon's instantaneous rest frame—with no detectable acceleration-driven departure from pure velocity-dependent decay scaling1.

## **4\. Explicit Hypotheses and Competing Models**

In accordance with preregistered statistical discipline, we define four competing quantitative models prior to data fitting:

> 1. **Model A (ArcSecs Universal Physical-Process Scaling — Zero free deviation parameters):** Time is modeled as an invariant universal progression parameter T, while gravitational potential and coordinate speed universally rescale the internal dynamical rate of every physical system by the exact product of the local weak-field gravitational lapse factor and the inverse Lorentz factor. All species-specific LPI offsets, kinematic anomalies, inter-species differential couplings, and proper-acceleration terms are fixed identically to zero.

## **Deliverable 5: Plain-English ArcSecs Advocacy Summary (Evidence-Accurate Brief)**

### **Rethinking "Time Dilation": What Experiments Actually Show—and Where the Next Discovery Could Hide**

When physicists report that time ticks slower at sea level than atop a skyscraper, or that high-speed subatomic particles experience stretched time, it is natural to picture time as an invisible rubber fabric warped by gravity and speed. The **ArcSecs research program** starts from a disciplined, mechanism-first alternative: *What if time itself never bends or stretches at all? What if time is simply a constant, steady progression—and what gravity and motion actually alter are the physical rates at which atoms oscillate, optical cavities resonate, and unstable particles decay?*  
This inquiry does not deny a single laboratory measurement. Nobody disputes the numbers. In 2022, researchers at JILA observed strontium atoms at the top of a one-millimeter cloud ticking faster than atoms at the bottom by roughly one part in ten quintillion5. In 2023, a formally blinded University of Wisconsin experiment confirmed the same millimeter-to-centimeter shift at nearly five-sigma certainty3. GPS satellites require a factory frequency preset compensating 38.6 microseconds per day so that everyday navigation does not drift by over eleven kilometers every twenty-four hours1. And inside the CERN storage ring, muons traveling near light speed live nearly thirty times longer than muons at rest6.  
The deeper scientific question is **what causes those measured rate differences**, and whether treating time as constant yields any testable prediction that departs from Einstein's relativity. Our independent analysis of eleven benchmark experiments across atomic metrology, space navigation, and particle accelerators gives an honest, two-part answer.

### **Why the Core ArcSecs Intuition Survives—Without Refuting Relativity**

First, precision experiments do **not** prove that time is a stretchable physical substance. No instrument has ever measured "time itself." Every clock test counts physical events—laser wave cycles, atomic fluorescence transitions, radio carrier phases, or decay electrons—and compares one count against another2. Prominent physicists and philosophers of relativity, including J. S. Bell and Harvey Brown, have argued that Einstein's mathematical predictions can be understood purely through the dynamical laws governing matter and fields rather than through a physical spacetime substance pushing clocks around8.  
When we built the cleanest constant-time physical-process model—**Model A**, in which gravity and motion rescale every physical interaction by the exact same universal factor—it matched every calibration benchmark and every held-out millimeter-scale optical clock test seamlessly1. Scientific honesty, however, demands a crucial caveat: **Model A produces the exact same numerical predictions as Einstein's relativity for every clock and decay experiment we know how to run.** Calling the phenomenon "universal physical rate scaling under constant time" rather than "curved proper time" shifts our philosophical picture, not the measurable outcome. Model A therefore cannot be claimed as an empirical discovery.

### **Where a Physical-Process Theory Could Actually Diverge—and What Remains Open**

If gravity and motion physically alter material processes rather than time itself, there is one decisive way nature could reveal that mechanism: **different fundamental forces might respond slightly differently.** Optical clocks rely on electromagnetic electron orbitals; hydrogen masers rely on magnetic spin flips; muons decay via the weak nuclear force; and nuclear clocks rely on the strong nuclear force binding atomic nuclei. If gravity couples even slightly differently across these sectors (**Model B**), two dissimilar clocks placed side by side on the exact same trajectory would gradually drift apart as Earth's elliptical orbit moves closer to and farther from the Sun each year61.  
Existing data show **no trace of such drift yet**. Our statistical evaluation excludes species-specific rate differences down to parts in a hundred thousand in space masers, parts in ten million in atomic clock comparisons, parts in fifty million in relativistic ion beams, and parts in a quadrillion in orbiting free-fall accelerometers1. Adding species-dependent parameters today does not improve predictions on new optical clock data; it simply overfits measurement noise.  
One major physical domain, however, remains largely untested: **the strong nuclear force inside atomic nuclei.** In September 2024, researchers achieved the first direct frequency link between a thorium-229 nuclear transition and a strontium-87 optical lattice clock69. Because thorium-229 amplifies subtle imbalances between strong nuclear and electromagnetic forces by ten thousand to a million times71, our Monte Carlo simulations show that a two-year side-by-side thorium-229 versus strontium-87 null-redshift campaign can probe six orders of magnitude deeper than every previous atomic-clock test1. Embracing the conceptual clarity of physical-process thinking while investing in that decisive nuclear-versus-optical null test is the only rigorous path from philosophical intuition to empirical discovery.

## **Deliverable 6: Referee-Style Self-Critique — Five Strongest Reasons the ArcSecs Hypothesis Could Fail**

Adopting the perspective of a skeptical senior referee for *Physical Review D* or *Nature Physics*, five core theoretical and experimental vulnerabilities could cause the ArcSecs constant-time program to fail as either a predictive framework or an explanatory ontology:

> 1. **The "Universal Conspiracy" Objection (Unexplained Fine-Tuning in Model A):** If time is truly a flat, constant progression parameter T and gravity and motion merely perturb internal physical mechanisms, why do four utterly disparate sectors—optical electronic orbitals, microwave hyperfine splittings, weak W-boson lepton decays, and solid-state cavity dimensions—rescale by the **exact same universal function** down to parts in 10^8 to 10^1511? In general relativity, universality flows effortlessly from all fields inhabiting one spacetime metric. In a non-metric constant-time theory, identical scaling across unrelated interactions appears as an unexplained fine-tuned coincidence—the very symmetry loss Mansouri and Sexl warned against in 19777.  
> 2. **Severe Energy-Conservation and Free-Fall Bottlenecks (Schiff's Conjecture Against Model B):** Any composition-dependent gravitational rate scaling faces a potent indirect clamp from free-fall universality. By energy conservation in a static gravitational field, a fractional difference in how gravity shifts electromagnetic versus strong-nuclear transition energies implies a proportional anomaly in the gravitational-to-inertial mass ratio of internal binding energy9. Because the MICROSCOPE mission already bounds titanium-versus-platinum free-fall differences to 1.5 × 10^-1511, wide classes of Model B couplings are already severely squeezed unless fine-tuned cancellations mask free-fall violations while preserving clock-rate anomalies.  
> 3. **Failure of Mechanical Deformation Models Under 10^18 Earth Gravities:** Intuitive physical-process analogies often imagine inertial stress or medium drag deforming a moving clock. Yet Bailey et al. (1977) demonstrated that elementary muons—point-like leptons lacking internal mechanical structure—endure proper centripetal accelerations exceeding 10^18 Earth gravities in the CERN storage ring with zero measurable departure from pure velocity-dependent inverse-Lorentz decay scaling1. Any mechanistic process hypothesis reliant on structural distortion or acceleration stress is already falsified.  
> 4. **Breakdown of Global Constant-Time Foliations in Strong Gravity:** The observational equivalence proof for Model A holds strictly within weak-field, smoothly foliated spacetime regions (terrestrial laboratories, Earth orbit, and the solar system). In strong-field general relativity—near black-hole event horizons or dynamical strong-gravity regimes probed by astrophysical black-hole and gravitational-wave observations—no single global, singularity-free universal progression parameter T preserves flat-space causality everywhere10. Extending a constant-time ontology into strong gravity without contradicting astrophysical tests remains an unresolved theoretical challenge.  
> 5. **Risk of Permanent Empirical Sterility Under Null Nuclear-Clock Results:** Even if the proposed two-year thorium-229 versus strontium-87 null-redshift campaign reaches its projected 1.75 × 10^-13 electromagnetic coupling sensitivity1, a null measurement consistent with zero would push Model B into ever-smaller corners of parameter space while leaving Model A permanently indistinguishable from general relativity. Under that outcome, a critical reviewer would justifiably conclude that constant universal time offers zero empirical advantage over standard relativity and survives solely as an unfalsifiable semantic interpretation.
