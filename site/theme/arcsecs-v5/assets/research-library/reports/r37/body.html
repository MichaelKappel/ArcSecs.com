2. Operational Definitions and Physical-First Ontology

To prevent ontological conflation between raw instrument readings and conventional geometric interpretations, every observable in this report is defined operationally before analysis. An atomic clock measurement records a dimensionless ratio of electromagnetic transition frequencies between two physical oscillators at specified gravitational potentials and velocities18. A cosmological redshift records the fractional wavelength shift of identified atomic emission or absorption spectral lines between a distant source and a terrestrial spectrograph13. A supernova light-curve width records the elapsed arrival interval in universal progression units between standardized rise and decline flux thresholds across fixed observer-frame photometric bandpasses13.
Similarly, galactic rotation measurements convert Doppler frequency shifts of neutral hydrogen twenty-one-centimeter emission or optical H-alpha lines, combined with sky-projected disk inclinations and photometric surface brightnesses, into circular speed profiles as functions of galactocentric radius1. Weak gravitational lensing measures coherent shape distortions (tangential reduced shear) of background galaxy images around foreground lenses, from which an azimuthally averaged excess surface mass-energy density and effective deflection potential are reconstructed without presupposing a dark halo shape2. Baryon acoustic oscillation surveys measure the angular and radial clustering peak scales of galaxy and quasar catalogs in redshift-angle space, reported as dimensionless ratios of transverse and line-of-sight intervals to a reference acoustic correlation length9.

3. Literature Review and Primary Empirical Benchmarks

3.1 Galactic Kinematics and Extended Weak-Lensing Profiles

The Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves (SPARC) catalog compiled by Lelli, McGaugh, and Schombert (2016) integrates near-infrared Spitzer surface photometry with over three decades of interferometric neutral hydrogen and optical rotation curves for 175 late-type spiral and dwarf irregular galaxies, supplemented by 16 rotating early-type galaxies with outer gas rings and 9 non-rotating early-type galaxies with X-ray halos20. Near-infrared photometry minimizes stellar population scatter, enabling nearly uniform stellar mass-to-light priors of roughly 0.5 solar masses per solar luminosity for disks and 0.7 for bulges1. Across nearly 2,700 resolved measurements in the initial sample (and 3,391 points across the full 175-galaxy compilation), McGaugh, Lelli, and Schombert (2016) established the Radial Acceleration Relation (RAR): the observed centripetal acceleration correlates tightly with the Newtonian acceleration expected solely from observed stars and gas, departing systematically below a characteristic acceleration scale of 1.20 × 10^-10 m/s^2 (quoted with ± 0.26 × 10^-10 m/s^2 uncertainty in subsequent comparison fits)1. Empirical scatter around the relation is small (~0.11 dex in McGaugh et al. 2016, and as low as 0.054 dex in high-quality subsamples dominated by observational uncertainties)4. Individual fits across the 175 SPARC galaxies by Li et al. (2018) recovered median stellar mass-to-light ratios of 0.50 for disks and 0.67 for bulges1, while Lelli et al. (2016, 2019) bounded intrinsic scatter in the Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation below 0.11 dex20.
Weak-lensing observations extend these kinematic patterns by two orders of magnitude in radius and acceleration. Brouwer et al. (2021) used 1,006 square degrees of fourth-release Kilo-Degree Survey (KiDS-1000) weak-lensing data covering roughly one million foreground lenses to measure the lensing RAR far outside visible disks31. Applying an analytical deprojection formula to isolated KiDS galaxies having no neighbors exceeding ten percent of their stellar mass within four megaparsecs, Mistele et al. (2024) demonstrated that inferred circular velocities remain remarkably flat out to roughly one megaparsec (with 300 kiloparsecs cited as a conservative lower bound for strict isolation reliability) and continue to follow the Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation for both late-type and early-type galaxies2. A September 2026 preprint assessing weak-lensing circular velocity profiles across four baryonic mass bins further reported that Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) and Burkert halo profiles failed to adequately fit all four mass bins simultaneously under the tested concentration models, whereas a cored pseudo-isothermal density profile succeeded across all four bins33. Meanwhile, Ciocan et al. (2026) analyzed 79 intermediate-redshift star-forming galaxies at redshifts between 0.33 and 1.44, reporting that while the RAR persists, the best-fit characteristic acceleration rises systematically with redshift from roughly 1.99 × 10^-10 m/s^2 in the lowest redshift bin to 2.71 × 10^-10 m/s^2 in the highest—roughly 19 sigma above the canonical zero-redshift SPARC benchmark and 5 sigma above the MIGHTEE-HI low-redshift estimate of 1.69 ± 0.13 × 10^-10 m/s^24.

3.2 Merging Galaxy Clusters and the Bullet Cluster

In galaxy clusters, the visible baryonic mass budget is dominated not by stars in galaxies, but by hot X-ray-emitting intracluster plasma. In the merging cluster 1E 0657-558 (the Bullet Cluster, redshift 0.296), Chandra 500-kilosecond X-ray observations combined with Magellan, VLT, and Hubble Space Telescope optical imaging reveal two primary galaxy concentrations separated by 0.72 megaparsecs on the sky—a hotter eastern main cluster (roughly 14 keV) and a cooler western subcluster (roughly 6 keV, with shock-heated gas reaching 17.4 ± 2.5 keV and merger speeds estimated at 3,950 to 4,500 km/s)5. Ram pressure during the supersonic collision slowed and stripped the collisional plasma, leaving the X-ray gas clouds lagging behind the collisionless galaxies5. Weak and strong gravitational lensing reconstructions by Clowe et al. (2004, 2006) and Bradač et al. (2006), alongside total lensing mass estimates near 2.0 × 10^15 solar masses (Coe et al. 2010, cited in Lage 2014), established that the dominant convergence peaks align spatially with the galaxy concentrations rather than the much more massive central plasma clouds, exhibiting an eight-sigma statistical centroid offset5. Similar gas-lensing decoupling appears in MACS J0025.4-122234. A November 2026 Figshare sensitivity preprint notes a roughly 200-kiloparsec gas-to-lensing peak separation and observes that the quoted eight-sigma figure measures statistical shear noise rather than systematic model sensitivity37; likewise, Brownstein and Moffat (2007) explored Modified Gravity (MOG) fits to the system38. Nevertheless, any strictly local force law anchored solely to total baryonic surface density cannot shift lensing convergence peaks away from the dominant X-ray gas5.

3.3 Cosmic Microwave Background, BAO, Supernovae, and 2025–2026 Tensions

Full-mission Planck 2018 temperature, polarization, and lensing measurements constrain the angular acoustic scale of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) to 0.03 percent precision (100 times angular scale equals 1.0411 ± 0.0003)12. Under the flat six-parameter cold-dark-matter plus cosmological-constant framework (Lambda-CDM), Aghanim et al. (2020) explicitly separate model-dependent late-Universe inferences—a Hubble parameter of 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc, baryon density parameter 0.0224 ± 0.0001, and cold dark matter density parameter 0.120 ± 0.001—from direct angular-scale measurements, while noting a mild over-two-sigma excess in CMB peak-smoothing lensing amplitude relative to base predictions12. Relativistic modified-gravity extensions such as the Aether-Scalar-Tensor (AeST) theory of Skordis and Złośnik (2021) prove mathematically that reproducing the second-to-third CMB acoustic peak ratios and linear matter power spectrum without particle cold dark matter requires additional dynamical scalar and unit-timelike vector degrees of freedom capable of mimicking pressureless dust perturbations before recombination while preserving light-speed tensor propagation11.
In March 2025 and July 2026, the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Data Release 2 (DESI DR2) published baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements from more than 14 million galaxies, quasars, and Lyman-alpha forest sightlines across seven tracer bins spanning effective redshifts from 0.295 to 2.3309. Combined with Planck CMB data, DESI DR2 prefers an evolving dark-energy equation of state (present parameter greater than minus one, evolution parameter less than zero) at 3.1 sigma over Lambda-CDM, shifting to 2.8 to 4.2 sigma when paired with Pantheon+, Union3, or DES-SN5YR Type Ia supernova compilations9. However, independent 2025–2026 audits caution against declaring dynamical dark energy established. Cross-probe analyses reveal persistent internal inconsistencies: CMB versus DESI DR2 displays roughly two-sigma parameter tension, supernova datasets disagree with CMB plus DESI at 1.5 to over 3 sigma, wide priors push matter density near 0.5, and excluding the LRG1 (redshift 0.510) or LRG2 (redshift 0.706) bins collapses Bayesian model preference back toward inconclusive or weak Lambda-CDM support10. On the supernova side, Vincenzi et al. (2025) investigated a 0.04-magnitude redshift-dependent offset between overlapping Pantheon+ and DES-SN5YR events highlighted by Efstathiou (2024), showing that reverting to earlier intrinsic dust-scatter models lowers DES-SN5YR dynamical preference from 3.9 to 3.3 sigma and identifying an additional three percent host-property systematic error45. A September 2026 preprint combining Pantheon+ and DES-SN5YR (“Supernovae Unite”) confirmed only weak Bayesian evidence for time-varying dark energy despite 2.5 to 3.3 sigma frequentist likelihood shifts46. Correspondingly, the 2026 Particle Data Group Cosmological Parameters review concludes that neither the Hubble tension nor hints of evolving dark energy are securely established8.
The local distance-ladder tension likewise remains unresolved. The SH0ES Cepheid-plus-supernova calibration yields 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc51, and JWST Cepheid observations have reduced period-luminosity dispersion from 0.45 to 0.17 magnitudes while excluding distance-dependent stellar crowding at 8.2 sigma52. Yet JWST tip-of-the-red-giant-branch calibrations by Freedman et al. (2025) yield lower expansion estimates on smaller host subsets51, while evolving dark-energy models shift inferred Hubble values by only ~0.5 km/s/Mpc, leaving the local-versus-CMB discrepancy intact54. Crucially for light-propagation alternatives, White et al. (2024) measured DES-SN5YR supernova light-curve widths across g, r, i, and z optical bands out to redshift ~1, establishing a power-law scaling exponent in (1 + z) consistent with unity within roughly 0.015 total statistical plus systematic uncertainty13. Any static-universe hypothesis without physical wave-packet lengthening is strongly contradicted by those arrival-interval data13.

4.1 Hypothesis A (ArcSecs-R): Relational Baryon-Sourced Momentum Coupling

Below that threshold, long-range relational momentum exchange transitions smoothly to a linear-distance inverse law, so that total radial acceleration approaches the geometric mean of Newtonian baryonic acceleration and the threshold scale, smoothed by the standard exponential RAR transition profile. Circular orbital speeds squared divided by radius equal this total radial acceleration, naturally producing asymptotically flat rotation curves and a Baryonic Tully-Fisher mass-velocity exponent of four with zero per-galaxy dark-halo tuning parameters.
For gravitational lensing, photons carrying energy and linear momentum equal to energy divided by vacuum speed experience transverse momentum impulse when passing near a galaxy. Crucially, a purely static scalar central acceleration equal to that acting on non-relativistic stars underpredicts observed photon deflection by a factor of two—the classical Newtonian light-deflection shortfall. Therefore, to match KiDS weak-lensing shear profiles without invoking curved spacetime, ArcSecs-R must include an explicit velocity-dependent transverse momentum coupling (analogous to a gravitomagnetic or refractive index gradient sourced by baryons) that doubles the transverse impulse on relativistic light packets while leaving slow orbital speeds unchanged. As an untuned out-of-sample prediction, ArcSecs-R requires that weak-lensing circular velocities of isolated galaxies stay flat along the Baryonic Tully-Fisher line out to 300–1,000 kiloparsecs2, and that lensing convergence peaks in any galaxy or cluster system remain anchored strictly to the center of total baryonic mass-energy.

4.2 Hypothesis B (ArcSecs-F): Physical Field-Energy and Low-Coupling Mass Component

ArcSecs-F preserves exact inverse-square attraction for all mass-energy, but takes Premise 6 literally: the gravitational field surrounding baryons—or an unclustered, non-luminous physical matter component—possesses real spatial distribution, localized mass-energy density, and independent inertial momentum. Unlike ArcSecs-R, in high-velocity cluster collisions (~4,000 km/s)34 the diffuse field-energy or cold compact component experiences negligible electromagnetic ram pressure compared to ionized intracluster plasma, allowing it to travel inertially alongside the collisionless stellar galaxies5.
As an untuned prediction, ArcSecs-F predicts that weak-lensing shear profiles around isolated galaxies follow cored isothermal envelopes better than cuspy NFW profiles at hundred-kiloparsec scales33, and that merging clusters exhibit lensing convergence peaks tightly coincident with stellar galaxy centroids rather than stripped X-ray gas—while requiring an explicit, independently measurable mass-to-baryon budget and stability law preventing premature radiative cooling or collapse.

6. Uncertainty Model, Covariances, and Systematics

In galactic kinematics, observational variance on observed centripetal acceleration is dominated by galaxy distance errors (typically 5 to 15 percent for non-primary distance methods, shifting radius and baryonic mass oppositely in the acceleration plane) and disk inclination errors near face-on orientations1. Approximately ten percent of the SPARC sample exhibits lower-quality or non-circular kinematic features that inflate chi-squared regardless of the underlying force law1. In weak lensing, external field effects from neighboring galaxies and two-halo large-scale structure terms induce systematic uncertainty beyond 300 kiloparsecs, mitigated in Mistele et al. (2024) via strict four-megaparsec isolation cuts2.
In DESI DR2 BAO inference, transverse and line-of-sight distance ratios within each redshift tracer share negative off-diagonal covariances (correlation coefficients near minus 0.452 for LRG1 and minus 0.395 for LRG2), so diagonal chi-squared figures serve strictly as a baseline diagnostic rather than full likelihood significance17. In supernova cosmology, dust-law assumptions, host-galaxy stellar-mass step corrections, Malmquist selection biases, and photometric cross-calibration between low-redshift anchors and high-redshift surveys introduce 0.01 to 0.05 magnitude systematics capable of shifting evolving dark-energy significance by more than 0.6 sigma45. In direct-detection propulsion limits, halo velocity dispersion (roughly 220 km/s Maxwellian truncated near 484.6 km/s escape speed) and local density uncertainties (0.3 to 0.42 GeV/cm^3) introduce factor-of-two flux uncertainty—wholly negligible relative to the twenty-order-of-magnitude cross-section deficit17.

7.2 Cluster Mergers and the Bullet Cluster

In 1E 0657-558, X-ray observations show that hot plasma is the dominant baryonic mass component, yet weak and strong lensing convergence maps place the two primary mass peaks directly on the western and eastern galaxy concentrations, displaced from the stripped X-ray plasma by roughly 200 kiloparsecs at eight-sigma statistical significance5. 8. Separate Mandatory Propulsion Feasibility Analysis

A physical or hypothesized dark sector is not automatically an engineered propellant or energy source. Feasibility requires an exact mass-flux, interaction-probability, momentum, and power ledger anchored to verified local density and direct-detection bounds. If ArcSecs-R (purely relational baryonic gravity with zero ambient dark particles) were true, local dark-matter substrate density would be identically zero, yielding zero harvestable flux by definition. Feasibility is therefore assessed under the most favorable physical-substrate assumption (ArcSecs-F or particle dark matter at the Particle Data Group local benchmark).

8.1 Exact Unit Conversion and Ideal Mass Throughput

The Particle Data Group standard local dark-matter energy-density benchmark is 0.3 GeV/cm^3 (with galactic halo models extending near 0.42 GeV/cm^3)14. Converting exactly via energy-mass equivalence (1 GeV equals 1.602176634 × 10^-10 joules, vacuum light speed 2.99792458 × 10^8 m/s, 10^6 cubic centimeters per cubic meter) yields 5.348 × 10^-22 kg/m^3 at 0.3 GeV/cm^3, and 7.131 × 10^-22 kg/m^3 at 0.4 GeV/cm^3—never 0.01 kg/m^3, which overstates ambient density by nineteen orders of magnitude17. For an extreme megastructure spacecraft collector with capture cross-sectional area of one square kilometer (10^6 m^2) moving relative to the galactic halo at 220 km/s17, even assuming unphysical 100-percent capture efficiency yields a maximum ambient mass-equivalent flux passing through the entire square-kilometer aperture of 1.177 × 10^-10 kg/s, or 3.71 grams per year17.

8.2 Realistic Capture Probability, Drag, Shielding, and Energy Ledger

Actual capture probability in a physical collector of target number density, species-specific interaction cross section, and path thickness scales as one minus the negative exponential of their product (approaching target number density times cross section times path length in the optically thin regime). Evaluating every physically bounded species class separately demonstrates total capture infeasibility.
For weakly interacting massive particles undergoing nuclear scattering, using the LUX-ZEPLIN 2025 upper limit for 40 GeV/c^2 particles (spin-independent cross section below 2.2 × 10^-48 cm^2 per nucleon)15 and applying coherent mass-number-squared nuclear scaling for a one-meter-thick solid tungsten collector (atomic mass 183.84 u, density 19,250 kg/m^3, target density 6.306 × 10^28 nuclei/m^3), the effective tungsten-nucleus cross section is at most 9.318 × 10^-45 m^217. The resulting mean free path inside solid tungsten is 1.702 × 10^15 meters (0.180 light-years), giving a capture probability across a one-meter tungsten wall of 5.876 × 10^-1617. For a one-square-kilometer by one-meter tungsten collector weighing 1.925 × 10^10 kilograms, realistic captured mass throughput drops to 6.913 × 10^-26 kg/s17. Even if 100 percent of that captured mass-energy were converted into an idealized collimated photon exhaust (thrust equal to captured mass rate times light speed), upper-bound thrust is 2.073 × 10^-17 newtons, compared to intake momentum drag (captured mass rate times craft speed) of 1.521 × 10^-20 newtons17. In the optically thin limit, both captured flux and collector mass scale linearly with collector area multiplied by thickness, so maximum acceleration cancels geometry entirely at 1.077 × 10^-27 m/s^217. Thermal heating and radiation shielding requirements from interstellar gas, solar wind, and cosmic rays across a square-kilometer collector impose structural mass and cooling penalties vastly exceeding any conceivable dark-matter recoil power.
Gravitational funneling via Bondi-Hoyle capture is equally negligible. Because dark matter couples universally to gravity, one might consider purely gravitational capture; however, the Bondi-Hoyle gravitational capture radius of a 1,000-tonne (10^6 kg) spacecraft moving at 220 km/s is 2.758 × 10^-15 meters—smaller than a single proton17. Gravitational focusing by any artificial spacecraft produces zero collectable flux.
Resonant electromagnetic capture for axions or dark photons faces an insurmountable thermodynamic deficit. Haloscopes such as ADMX convert coherent axion fields into microwave photons inside multi-tesla superconducting resonant cavities, operating receiver chains sensitive to powers below 10 rontowatts (10^-26 watts)16. Even converting 10^-26 watts of microwave power into collimated photon exhaust yields ~3.3 × 10^-35 newtons of thrust while demanding multi-kilowatt cryogenic dilution refrigeration and magnet power—a negative net energy balance by nearly thirty orders of magnitude, compounded by narrowband mass-tuning restrictions.

11. Separate Final Verdicts and Conclusions

Verdict 1 — Astrophysical Explanatory Power:
Within the binding ArcSecs framework, a strictly baryon-sourced relational acceleration law (ArcSecs-R) efficiently organizes zero-redshift galactic rotation curves and isolated weak-lensing velocities out to one megaparsec around a single acceleration scale (1.20 × 10^-10 m/s^2), outperforming multi-parameter halo fits in parameter parsimony on relaxed galaxies1. However, pure ArcSecs-R fails decisively on the Bullet Cluster’s ~200-kiloparsec gas-lensing offset5, underpredicts photon deflection by a factor of two without an explicit transverse momentum coupling, and faces a reported ~19-sigma increase in characteristic acceleration at redshift ~14. Switching to a physical field-energy model (ArcSecs-F) resolves cluster offsets only by reintroducing an independent, collisionless gravitating mass-energy component constituting the majority of cluster mass5. Cosmologically, neither ArcSecs hypothesis presently explains Planck CMB acoustic peak heights (100 times angular scale equals 1.0411 ± 0.0003)11, while naive non-stretching light-loss models are quantitatively excluded by both DES-SN5YR supernova light-curve width scaling13 and DESI DR2 Alcock-Paczynski distance ratios17. While 2025–2026 Hubble and dynamical dark-energy tensions demonstrate real unresolved anomalies in standard cosmological fits8, ArcSecs currently lacks a completed physical cosmology.
Verdict 2 — Engineered Substrate-Capture Propulsion Feasibility:
Engineered spacecraft propulsion via ambient dark-sector capture is physically infeasible under every verified observational and laboratory limit. Even under the optimistic assumption of a real particle substrate at 0.3 GeV/cm^3 (5.348 × 10^-22 kg/m^3), a one-square-kilometer collector moving at 220 km/s intercepts only 3.71 grams of mass-energy per year (1.177 × 10^-10 kg/s)17. At the LUX-ZEPLIN 40-GeV spin-independent limit (2.2 × 10^-48 cm^2), a one-meter solid tungsten slab captures a fraction 5.876 × 10^-16, yielding a maximum conceivable photon-rocket acceleration of 1.077 × 10^-27 m/s^2 regardless of collector area15. Gravitational Bondi-Hoyle capture (2.758 × 10^-15 m for a 1,000-tonne craft) and ADMX-scale resonant axion receiver power scales (below 10^-26 watts against kilowatt cryogenics) yield similarly negligible thrust16. If dark matter does not exist as ambient particles (pure ArcSecs-R), collectable substrate flux is identically zero.
Required Mechanism / Missing-Mechanism Statement: “The ArcSecs relational mechanism proposes that baryonic mass-energy exchanges finite-speed momentum across Euclidean positions within constant universal progression T, transitioning below 1.20 × 10^-10 m/s^2 to an asymptotic inverse-distance coupling (ArcSecs-R) or storing gravitating energy in a physical, ram-pressure-free halo field (ArcSecs-F); however, ArcSecs currently lacks three essential physical derivations: (1) a transverse momentum or refractive coupling doubling Newtonian photon deflection without curved spacetime, (2) a Euclidean perturbation mechanism reproducing Planck CMB acoustic peak ratios without particle cold dark matter, and (3) an achromatic broadband wave-packet elongation mechanism reproducing (1 + z) Type Ia supernova light-curve width scaling and DESI DR2 Alcock-Paczynski ratios without violating fast radio burst dispersion limits below 3.9 × 10^-51 kg.”

Companion E: PLAIN_ENGLISH_SUMMARY.md (Plain-English ArcSecs Summary)

Under the ArcSecs guidelines—where time (“When”) and space (“Where”) are not physical fabrics that stretch or warp, and all real physical effects come from mass, energy, and direct interactions—this study tested two concrete questions: can physical-only interaction laws replace “dark matter” and “dark energy” across galaxies, clusters, and the deep universe, and could a spacecraft harvest any real dark substrate as rocket fuel?
On the scale of individual galaxies, the evidence is remarkably encouraging for relational physics. Measurements of 175 galaxies in the SPARC catalog and weak-lensing maps extending one megaparsec into surrounding space show that a single acceleration threshold (1.20 × 10^-10 m/s^2) tightly predicts how fast stars and gas orbit purely from the visible stars and gas—without tuning invisible dark-matter halos galaxy by galaxy1. However, three hard observational walls block a complete explanation today. First, in the colliding Bullet Cluster, gravitational lensing maps locate the bulk of the gravitating mass next to the galaxies, roughly 200 kiloparsecs away from the hot gas clouds that hold most of the visible ordinary matter5. A simple force tied only to visible matter puts the pull in the wrong place, forcing ArcSecs to include an invisible, collisionless physical field or compact mass component. Second, 2026 observations of distant galaxies suggest the galactic acceleration threshold rises at earlier epochs, challenging a fixed universal law4. Third, light from distant Type Ia supernovae arrives stretched out in duration by (1 + z), and DESI DR2 galaxy-clustering scales show specific transverse-to-radial shapes that simple “light loses energy on the way” models fail to match13. While standard cosmology faces genuine internal tensions in 2025–2026 over the Hubble constant and whether dark energy evolves8, ArcSecs has not yet built the mathematical replacement needed to match cosmic microwave background ripples or supernova pulse widths.
On propulsion, the verdict is unambiguous: dark-matter harvesting cannot power a spacecraft. The local density of dark matter (0.3 GeV/cm^3) equals just 5.35 × 10^-22 kg per cubic meter—meaning even a one-square-kilometer funnel sweeping through the galaxy at 220 km/s passes through less than four grams of dark matter per year17. Because particle dark matter passes through solid metal almost completely untouched—with a mean free path of nearly two-tenths of a light-year in solid tungsten under current LUX-ZEPLIN limits—a one-meter-thick tungsten collector traps fewer than one in a quadrillion particles15. Even converting trapped mass at 100-percent efficiency into photon thrust produces an acceleration of 10^-27 m/s^2, effectively zero17. And if ArcSecs explains galaxy speeds without dark particles at all, there is zero ambient fuel to collect.
