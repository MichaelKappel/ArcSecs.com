## **2\. Literature Review and Status of Evidence Through 22 November 2026**

### **Bubble-Collision Theory and Early CMB Searches**

The quantitative observational program for eternal inflation was pioneered by Feeney, Johnson, Mortlock, and Peiris (2011)2. Exploiting the residual SO(2,1) symmetry of a two-bubble collision spacetime, they showed that a collision intersecting our surface of last scattering imprints an azimuthally symmetric circular disk bounded by a causal ring where the temperature or its radial derivative can change abruptly2. Because the collision primarily advances or retards the scalar inflaton field inside our bubble, it acts as a multiplicative modulation across ordinary inflationary perturbations2. Applying a Bayesian search to WMAP 7-year observations with the conservative KQ75 mask, Feeney et al. (2011) concluded that the data did not warrant adding bubble collisions to the cosmological model2, placing an upper limit of fewer than 4.0 observable collisions on the sky at 95% confidence1.  
To handle full-sky datasets computationally, McEwen et al. (2012) built an optimal matched-filter preprocessing stage on the sphere, identifying 16 candidate peaks (including 8 new candidates) in foreground-cleaned WMAP 7-year W-band data for subsequent Bayesian marginalization20. Feeney et al. (2013) formalized the hierarchical Bayesian relic-detection framework31, while Johnson et al. (2012) and Wainwright et al. (2014) advanced from analytic approximations to full numerical-relativity simulations of colliding bubbles1. Solving the scalar-field and Einstein dynamics on an adaptive C grid linked to Python and CosmoTransitions—with time steps finer than one-thirtieth of the inflationary oscillation period to resolve Lorentz-contracted walls—Wainwright et al. (2014) connected underlying potential shapes directly to CMB observables1. Feeney, Elsner, Johnson, and Peiris (2015) extended this framework to CMB polarization, forecasting how E-mode signatures help separate genuine collisions from random cosmic-variance temperature fluctuations3.

### **Planck Isotropy, Topology, and Unreviewed Preprints**

The ESA Planck mission established the all-sky benchmark for CMB isotropy and global geometry. Observing across nine frequency bands (30 to 857 GHz) and resolving 18 acoustic peaks across temperature and polarization, Planck constrained five of the six standard cosmological parameters to better than 1% and the acoustic angular scale to 0.03%21. *Planck 2018 results. VII* audited CMB statistical isotropy and large-angle anomalies6, while Planck's 2018 inflationary analysis (arXiv:1807.06211v2) explicitly reported that polarization data do not support physical models of a scale-dependent dipolar modulation, reinforcing single-field inflationary predictions7. Earlier, *Planck 2015 results. XVIII* (*Astronomy & Astrophysics* 594, A18) investigated background geometry and compact spatial topologies via matched-circle searches6 (while full numerical radius bounds from that paper were not present in retrieved excerpts, its searches constrain compact topologies below the horizon scale). Separately, a non-peer-reviewed preprint posted on Preprints.org (Bhattacharjee et al.) analyzed the full Planck 2018 sky for parallel-universe collision templates, reporting null detections down to disk brightness thresholds of 153–211 microkelvin for bubble collisions and 244–383 microkelvin for cosmic textures; because that manuscript is explicitly unreviewed, its thresholds must be treated as preliminary rather than archival consensus3.

### **High-Resolution CMB Data: ACT DR6 and Joint Polarization Analyses**

High-multipole ground-based measurements reached major milestones in 2025–2026. The Atacama Cosmology Telescope Data Release 6 (ACT DR6.02, hosted on NASA LAMBDA) covers 19,000 square degrees observed between 2017 and 2022 at 0.5-arcminute pixelization to a median depth of 10 microkelvin-arcminute across 90, 150, and 220 GHz channels, releasing day-night and night-only co-adds alongside four time-split maps per band and semi-analytic covariance matrices8. After removing Galactic emission and point sources, 25% of the sky contributes to clean power-spectrum estimation8. Joint reconstructions combining Planck PR3/PR4, ACT DR6, and SPT confirm a nearly scale-invariant, slightly red-tilted primordial power spectrum41.  
In a peer-reviewed joint analysis published in *Physical Review D* on 15 August 2026, Eskilt (2026) tested ACT DR6 and Planck DR4 polarization spectra for isotropic cosmic birefringence—a parity-violating rotation of CMB linear polarization26. Jointly sampling 17 instrumental miscalibration angles, three Galactic dust parameters, and one global rotation angle yielded a rotation angle of 0.277 ± 0.057 degrees, appearing consistently across ACT-only (0.207 ± 0.073 degrees), ACT–Planck cross-spectra (0.178 ± 0.078 degrees), and Planck-only (0.387 ± 0.094 degrees) blocks8. Critically, however, Eskilt (2026) emphasizes that the ACT team reported an unexplained discrepancy between the polarization angles of its PA5 90 GHz and 150 GHz arrays, meaning unknown instrumental systematics in neither Planck nor ACT can yet be excluded26. Moreover, frequency-independent isotropic rotation points toward a uniform pseudoscalar coupling rather than a localized bubble-collision wall26.

### **Large-Scale Structure: DESI DR2 and Euclid DR1-Foundation**

In large-scale structure, DESI DR2 Results II (March 2025; *Phys. Rev. Although consistent with a flat cosmological-constant expansion history internally, DESI DR2 BAO parameters show a 2.3-sigma tension with Planck CMB determinations, rising to a 3.1-sigma preference (2.8 to 4.2 sigma when combined with supernovae) for a time-evolving dark-energy equation of state unless an unknown systematic error affects one or more datasets9. Follow-up 2025 analyses highlight that negative spatial curvature—a natural consequence of open bubble interiors—remains partially degenerate with dynamical dark-energy parametrizations and requires higher-redshift spectroscopic surveys to separate cleanly43.  
On 12 November 2026, ESA released Euclid Data Release 1-Foundation, covering 1,900 square degrees of the Wide Survey (\~500 square degrees North and \~1,400 square degrees South, observed 14 February 2024 to 21 May 2025\) and totaling 1.7 petabytes13. ### **Cosmic Dipoles, Bulk Flows, Gravitational Waves, and Photon-Mass Bounds**

Large-scale dipole and velocity anomalies are frequently discussed as candidate probes of external structure. The CMB temperature dipole is measured at 3,361.90 ± 0.04 microkelvin (statistical), implying a Solar System velocity near 370 kilometers per second and a Local Group velocity near 630 kilometers per second relative to the CMB rest frame12. Because leading-order adiabatic inflationary super-horizon perturbations cancel identically between Doppler and Sachs–Wolfe dipoles, a genuine mismatch between the CMB frame and the distant matter dipole would signal physics beyond standard inflation12. While Secrest et al. (2021) reported a roughly 5-sigma discrepancy in the quasar number-count dipole46 and Kashlinsky et al. (2008) previously proposed a large-scale cluster bulk flow via the kinetic Sunyaev–Zel'dovich (kSZ) effect49, Planck Collaboration (2014) investigated peculiar-velocity constraints (*Astronomy & Astrophysics* 561, A97; full text not retrieved in available excerpts)50, and a 2025 historical review (arXiv:2510.05340v1) underscores persistent unresolved controversies over bulk-flow amplitudes, coherence scales, and selection biases52. Contemporary 2026 kSZ cross-correlations between ACT DR6 and DESI Legacy Survey galaxies probe halo baryon fractions rather than exterior cosmic attraction53. Meanwhile, isolated photometric surprises—such as an apparently overly mature high-redshift galaxy or a misclassified foreground brown dwarf in deep imaging—arise from astrophysical modeling or classification errors (H3) rather than external bubble dynamics.  
In gravitational waves, LVK stochastic background searches continue to return null detections and tighten upper limits10. Combined O1–O3 observations bounded the 95% credible dimensionless energy-density fraction at 25 Hz (log-uniform prior) to below 5.8 × 10⁻⁹ for a flat spectrum (99% sensitivity in 20–76.6 Hz), below 3.4 × 10⁻⁹ for an index of two-thirds (20–90.6 Hz), and below 3.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ for an index of three (20–291.6 Hz)10. Adding O4a data (arXiv:2508.20721; *Phys. Rev. D*) tightened those bounds by factors of 1.2 to 2.3—to below 2.8 × 10⁻⁹ (flat), 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ (index two-thirds), 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ (index three), and 2.9 × 10⁻⁹ (marginalized, down from 6.6 × 10⁻⁹ in O1–O3)10. On 6 November 2026, *Physical Review D* accepted LVK's updated O4 search incorporating data through April 2025 (arXiv:2608.23477; DOI 10.1103/fvk7-qcjr)11. Because full numerical tables from that November 2026 acceptance were inaccessible in retrieved summaries, we cite O1–O4a as the verified quantitative limit and note O4b solely for release scope.  
Finally, testing ArcSecs' working question regarding nonzero photon inertia relies on localized Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs). Recent 2024–2026 analyses—combining up to 104 localized FRBs with BAO, supernovae, and CMB after restoring a missing normalization factor in the Macquart intergalactic dispersion distribution19, alongside neural-network cosmology-independent fits25 and *JHEP* (November 2026\) Abelian-Higgs phase-diagram analyses24—provide strong empirical support for massless photons, bounding Type II symmetry-broken photon masses below 3 × 10⁻¹³ electronvolts and Proca masses in FRB tests near 10⁻¹⁴ electronvolts19.

### **What "Outside" Means Physically, Bubble Walls, and Drivers of Motion**

A literal boiling-pot picture is scientifically insufficient unless three physical questions are answered explicitly: what lies outside, whether a physical boundary wall exists, and what drives interior motion. ### **Appendix F: Five Referee-Style Objections and Data-Grounded Responses**

> 1. **Objection 1 (Statistical Significance and Look-Elsewhere Bias):** *“Matched-filter searches on WMAP found 16 candidate peaks; why aren't these tentative detections of cosmic bubbles?”* **Response:** Matched-filter peaks on a Gaussian random field are guaranteed whenever millions of sky-pixel and angular-radius combinations are scanned. As Table 3 shows, preserving a global 3-sigma confidence threshold across 100,000 effective trials requires a local peak exceeding 5.44 sigma4. In WMAP 7-year data, hierarchical Bayesian marginalization over those preprocessing candidates yielded no statistical preference over standard cosmology (fewer than 4.0 collisions at 95% confidence)1.  
> 2. **Objection 2 (Cosmic Variance Floor):** *“If Planck has far lower detector noise than WMAP, why didn't Planck push smooth bubble-collision bounds dramatically deeper?”* **Response:** As Table 2 demonstrates, smooth collision ramps concentrate power at multipoles below 50, where intrinsic cosmic variance of the primordial CMB exceeds detector noise in both WMAP and Planck4. Cutting white noise from 70 to 33 microkelvin-arcminute and sharpening the beam from 13 to 5 arcminutes improves smooth 30-degree ramp signal-to-noise by just \~2.1% (from 2.43 to 2.48)4. Planck's advantage lies in sharp causal-boundary steps and E-mode polarization cross-checks4.  
> 3. **Objection 3 (Bulk Flows and Quasar Dipoles):** “Secrest et al. reported a \~5-sigma excess in the quasar dipole relative to the CMB frame46. Doesn't that support an exterior-domain gravitational pull?” **Response:** No. Table 4 shows that any localized exterior mass capable of accelerating our Local Group to 630 kilometers per second from 2 to 10 Hubble radii induces a tidal CMB quadrupole of 0.00030 to 0.00150—30 to 150 times larger than the \~10⁻⁵ baseline CMB anisotropy scale4. Furthermore, super-horizon adiabatic modes cancel to leading order in the CMB dipole12. Explaining the quasar-dipole anomaly requires either survey selection/evolution systematics (H3) or intrinsic primordial isocurvature, not an exterior gravitational pull.  
> 4. **Objection 4 (Stochastic Gravitational Waves):** *“Could LVK O4 upper limits—or a future LISA detection—confirm inter-universe bubble collisions?”* **Response:** Not from the isotropic frequency spectrum alone. As Table 5 demonstrates, an ordinary first-order phase transition *within our own universe* at 100 to 10⁸ gigaelectronvolts generates stochastic gravitational-wave spectra peaking across the exact same millihertz-to-kilohertz bands with fractional energy densities near 2.42 × 10⁻¹⁰4. Breaking this degeneracy requires directional correlation with an independently confirmed CMB collision disk.  
> 5. **Objection 5 (Premature Claim on Euclid DR1):** “Since Euclid released DR1-Foundation on 12 November 2026 with over 900 million galaxies14, why is weak-lensing shear omitted from your empirical constraints?” **Response:** Official ESA and Caltech DR1 documentation explicitly states that DR1-Foundation (12 November 2026\) delivers raw/calibrated imaging, strong-lens candidates, and initial spectroscopy only; validated weak-lensing shear catalogs and 3D clustering products are deferred until DR1 Complete in mid-202713. Citing unreleased shear constraints would violate evidence-vintage integrity.
