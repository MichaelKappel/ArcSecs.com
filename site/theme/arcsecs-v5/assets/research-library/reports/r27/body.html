JWST Deep Fields and Substellar Interlopers

Deep near- and mid-infrared imaging across JWST extragalactic legacy fields—including CEERS, JADES, GLASS, UNCOVER, RUBIES, PRIMER, and COSMOS-Web—rapidly revealed compact red dropouts photometrically consistent with Lyman-break galaxies at redshifts 10 to 3216. Follow-up spectroscopy and multi-epoch imaging exposed two primary impostor populations. The first consists of dust-obscured intermediate-redshift star-forming galaxies (roughly redshift 3 to 6) whose intense rest-frame optical nebular emission lines—doubly ionized oxygen and hydrogen Balmer transitions—boost long-wavelength NIRCam broadbands while dust extinguishes shorter wavelengths. The archetypal case is CEERS-93316, selected near photometric redshift 16.4 (Donnan et al. 2023) despite medium-band F410M coverage and spectroscopically refuted at redshift 4.912 by Arrabal Haro et al. (2023)23. The second population consists of cool Galactic T- and Y-type brown dwarfs extending to kiloparsec distances in the Milky Way disk and halo, whose deep atmospheric molecular absorption troughs mimic high-redshift spectral breaks3.
Documented brown-dwarf contaminations accumulated steadily from 2023 through late 2026. Nonino et al. (2023) and Burgasser et al. (2024) confirmed cold brown dwarfs in the GLASS and UNCOVER fields, including UNCOVER-BD-1 and UNCOVER-BD-2, which had been selected photometrically as high-redshift active galactic nuclei before 14.3-hour JWST NIRSpec PRISM spectra revealed water, methane, ammonia, phosphine, and collision-induced molecular hydrogen absorption3. Hainline et al. (2024a) cataloged 21 brown-dwarf candidates across JADES and CEERS27. In 2026, two decisive follow-up studies demonstrated how extreme F277W and F356W dropouts slip past single-epoch vetting: Bradač et al. (2026) reclassified two Bullet Cluster high-redshift candidates as ultra-cool Y dwarfs using independent NIRSpec spectra and one-year NIRCam proper motions2, while Liu et al. (2026a) overturned the redshift-32 candidate Capotauro (originally presented by Gandolfi et al. 2025/202617) via a 3.5-year proper-motion measurement ruling out any extragalactic origin beyond 6 sigma2. In November 2026, Liu et al. (2026b) achieved JWST’s first direct trigonometric parallax for an extragalactic-field Y dwarf (JADES-GS-BD-5 at 64 parsecs) while highlighting large inferred velocity scatter among parallax-free JADES candidates21.

Geometric Distance Anchors and Calibration Systematics

Modern extragalactic distance ladders rest on four primary first-rung anchors: Milky Way trigonometric parallaxes from ESA’s Gaia mission, detached eclipsing binaries in the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds (LMC and SMC), and water megamaser orbital dynamics in NGC 425843. Every anchor carries documented empirical systematics. Gaia Data Release 3 (DR3) documentation and Lindegren et al. (2021b) establish systematic parallax zero-point biases varying with magnitude, color, and position (modeled through Z5 and Z6 calibration recipes), underestimated formal proper-motion uncertainties requiring empirical scaling factors, and astrometric quality cuts (renormalized unit weight error below 1.4)44. Detached eclipsing binaries constrain the LMC to 49.59 plus or minus 0.09 kiloparsecs (statistical; Pietrzyński et al. 2019) and the SMC to 62.44 plus or minus 0.47 (statistical) plus or minus 0.81 (systematic) kiloparsecs (Graczyk et al. 2020), relying on an empirically calibrated stellar surface-brightness–color relation49. Similarly, a September 2026 Bayesian forward-modeling reanalysis of the Megamaser Cosmology Project (MCP) sample shows that non-linear peculiar-velocity reconstructions lower the inferred recession slope from 73.9 (Pesce et al. 2020) to 71.6 plus or minus 3.7 kilometers per second per megaparsec18. From Photons to Coordinates: Pre-Cosmological Telescope Measurements

Answering Research Questions 1 and 2 begins by isolating what telescopes physically record before any cosmological framework enters the analysis. A telescope never directly measures distance, linear radius, age, or cosmic epoch. Every astronomical catalog originates from five pre-cosmological physical observables:

  1. Angular Position: Photons strike detector pixels and register charge. Mapping pixel centroids onto celestial coordinates requires an optical distortion model anchored to compact reference sources—such as Gaia DR3 stars or stationary background galaxies—achieving tens of microarcseconds in Gaia and several milliarcseconds in local relative JWST NIRCam frames2.
  2. Multi-Epoch Proper Motion and Parallax: Repeating centroiding across separated calendar epochs exposes linear sky drift (proper motion, in milliarcseconds per year) arising from transverse relative velocity, plus an annual oscillatory ellipse (trigonometric parallax, in milliarcseconds) generated by Earth’s physical orbital displacement around the Sun2. Both are purely geometric detector observables prior to assuming any velocity distribution.
  3. Broadband Flux and Color: Integrating calibrated detector counts inside a defined angular aperture or point-spread function (PSF) profile through a bandpass filter measures specific energy flux density (typically in nanojanskys or AB magnitudes), while color is simply the flux ratio between two filters4. Even here, converting raw counts into cataloged “total” fluxes requires non-cosmological pipeline choices: PSF homogenization across filters, fixed circular apertures (from 0.20 to 5.30 arcseconds in ASTRODEEP-JWST), Kron-aperture flux scaling corrections, and neighbor deblending schemes such as Voronoi versus Gaussian weighting26.
  4. Apparent Angular Extent: Determining whether a source is spatially resolved requires comparing its two-dimensional brightness profile against the empirical instrument PSF via profile fitting (such as single-component Sérsic fits with GALFIT) or aperture growth curves57. Unresolved sources yield only angular upper limits (such as smaller than 0.043 to 0.071 arcseconds for the Bullet Cluster dwarfs)4.
  5. Dispersed Spectrum: Passing radiation through a prism, diffraction grating, or radio heterodyne spectrometer separates flux across wavelength or frequency1. Multiple narrow atomic or molecular transitions uniquely fingerprint the emitting physics and fractional wavelength shift, whereas broad continuum breaks remain susceptible to template degeneracy.

How Cool Dwarfs and Dusty Galaxies Masquerade as Extreme-Redshift Sources

Photometric misclassifications arise along the single-epoch dropout bypass whenever sharp broadband flux steps are converted directly into Lyman-break redshifts without independent astrometry or spectroscopy. Two physical radiative-transfer mechanisms drive this confusion in JWST deep fields.
The first mechanism is cold substellar molecular absorption in Milky Way T- and Y-type brown dwarfs (substellar objects below the roughly 0.075 solar-mass hydrogen-fusion threshold with effective temperatures below 1,300 kelvin, and below roughly 500 kelvin for Y dwarfs)3. Although their thermal emission peaks between 1 and 5 micrometers, dense molecular opacity from water, methane, ammonia, carbon monoxide, phosphine, and collision-induced molecular hydrogen carves deep absorption troughs across 2.3 to 3.5 micrometers while leaving a bright atmospheric transmission window between 4.0 and 4.8 micrometers3. Through JWST’s broadband filters (F200W, F277W, F356W, F444W), a Y dwarf hundreds of parsecs away disappears below 3.5 micrometers yet shines brightly in F410M and F444W—creating an extreme F277W- or F356W-dropout signature that mimics the neutral-hydrogen Lyman break of a galaxy at redshift 15 to 322. Because substellar angular diameters fall far below JWST’s diffraction limit, these nearby stars also mimic the unresolved or ultra-compact morphologies expected for early-epoch galaxies4.
The second mechanism is dust reddening paired with intense nebular line boosting in intermediate-redshift galaxies (roughly redshift 3.5 to 5.5). Heavy interstellar dust suppresses rest-frame ultraviolet flux to mimic a short-wavelength dropout, while strong optical emission lines—specifically doubly ionized oxygen (495.9 and 500.7 nanometers), hydrogen-beta, and hydrogen-alpha—fall directly inside long-wavelength NIRCam broadbands24. Whenever template libraries underrepresent extreme dust-plus-line equivalent widths, photometric fitting codes mistakenly assign the steep step between broadbands to a Lyman break above redshift 1523.

A viable ArcSecs interaction must therefore specify the physical field or matter sink receiving lost photon energy strictly collinearly with the propagation vector, maintaining angular scatter well below 0.03 arcseconds over cosmological distances.
Second, a complete mechanism must account for macroscopic arrival-interval stretching within constant universal progression T. Under constant universal progression T, reproducing these laboratory measurements requires an explicit physical coupling between local gravitational field intensity and atomic binding energy or transition oscillator dynamics that alters physical tick rates without modifying T itself. Five Fully Sourced Case Studies

To satisfy Work Packages B, C, and G, five canonical objects are audited in full empirical detail: two cases of dramatic distance revision to nearby Milky Way brown dwarfs, one extragalactic photometric-to-spectroscopic revision, and two multi-line spectroscopically secured high-redshift controls.

Case Study 1: Capotauro — From Proposed Redshift-32 Galaxy to Milky Way Y Dwarf

Discovered in 2025 within deep JWST CEERS imaging, the compact red source “Capotauro” displayed a steep flux drop below 3.5 micrometers (an extreme F356W dropout)2. Gandolfi et al. (2025/2026) evaluated both Galactic substellar and extragalactic models, noting that among extragalactic hypotheses the sharp flux step favored a Lyman-break galaxy near redshift 3217. Separately, Ferrara et al. (2026) examined whether apparent flux variability over an 800-day baseline between NIRCam and noisy NIRSpec data combined with an early proper-motion non-detection (upper limit near 137 milliarcseconds per year) might indicate a pair-instability supernova near redshift 152.
Direct multi-epoch geometry settled its Galactic identity. Liu et al. (2026a) paired Epoch 1 CEERS NIRCam imaging (December 2022, ERS 1345; plus F090W from June 2023, GO 2234) with Epoch 2 medium-band imaging (F140M, F162M, F182M, F210M, F430M, F460M, and F480M) obtained in mid-to-late June 2026 under MINERVA (GO 7814) and SPAM (GO 8559), creating a 3.5-year baseline2. Establishing a local relative astrometric frame with 68 compact reference sources and applying filter-dependent PSF fitting plus a joint four-image injection-recovery test, Liu et al. measured a sky displacement of 132 plus or minus 20 milliarcseconds—yielding proper-motion components of plus 9.4 (+6.4/−4.8) and minus 35.6 (+5.1/−5.9) milliarcseconds per year, or a total proper motion of 37.6 (+5.5/−5.6) milliarcseconds per year2. This astrometric detection rules out any extragalactic explanation—galaxy, active nucleus, or supernova—beyond 6 sigma2.
Equally important is separating direct measurement from downstream inference. While Capotauro’s Milky Way membership is an observed geometric fact, its inferred distance of 730 plus or minus 110 parsecs (vertical Galactic height 630 plus or minus 100 parsecs, spectral type Y 1.0 plus or minus 0.5, effective temperature approximately 350 kelvin) remains a model-dependent photometric/atmospheric inference2. Multiplying that distance by the measured proper motion yields an inferred tangential speed of 132 plus or minus 28 kilometers per second2. Within the Besançon Galaxy kinematic model (51 percent thin disk, 49 percent thick disk, below 0.5 percent halo along this sightline), such a high transverse speed carries only a 0.4 percent probability in the thin disk versus an 8 percent probability in the older thick disk—pointing either to a thick-disk brown dwarf or to an intrinsically colder, fainter object sitting closer than 730 parsecs2. Compared with the initial redshift-32 galaxy hypothesis (standard-benchmark comoving distance roughly 11,431 megaparsecs and angular-diameter distance 346.4 megaparsecs), Capotauro’s reclassification represents a distance reduction of roughly 15.7 million times and an inferred physical-size reduction of roughly 475,000 times6.

Case Study 2: Bullet-BD1 and Bullet-BD2 — Independent Confirmation via Spectroscopy and Motion

From single-epoch JWST NIRCam imaging of the Bullet Cluster field, Bradač et al. (2026) selected two compact F277W/F356W dropouts—Bullet-BD1 (catalog ID 7107472, right ascension 104.5840622 degrees, declination minus 55.9258435 degrees, F444W 27.52 plus or minus 0.04 AB magnitude, angular size below 0.071 arcseconds) and Bullet-BD2 (catalog ID 7107197, right ascension 104.5882284 degrees, declination minus 55.9285412 degrees, F444W 26.89 plus or minus 0.01 AB magnitude, angular size below 0.043 arcseconds)—as high-redshift galaxy candidates4. Both objects were nondetected in F200W and F277W (flux limits below 3 to 6 nanojanskys) yet brightened sharply into F410M and F444W (34 to 92 nanojanskys)4.
Follow-up observations delivered dual independent refutations. First, JWST NIRSpec spectroscopy matched ultra-cool Y-type brown dwarf atmospheric templates (ATMO2020 and Sonora Elf Owl) rather than high-redshift galaxy continua4. Bullet-BD1 closely resembles Capotauro’s spectral trace and ranks among the coldest spectroscopically confirmed brown dwarfs known, with best-fit effective temperatures of 296 (+12/−24) kelvin (ATMO2020) or 324 (+27/−26) kelvin (Sonora Elf Owl)—barely warmer than the benchmark ~263 kelvin Y dwarf WISE J085510.83−071442.54. Bullet-BD2 fits warmer Y-dwarf models at 510 (+15/−21) kelvin (ATMO2020) or 471 (+24/−26) kelvin (Sonora Elf Owl)4. Second, NIRCam imaging taken roughly one year later independently detected proper motions of 49 plus or minus 8 milliarcseconds per year for Bullet-BD1 and 24 plus or minus 3 milliarcseconds per year for Bullet-BD2, alongside stable Epoch 2 to Epoch 1 F444W flux ratios (0.98 ± 0.07 and 1.02 ± 0.03)4. Their proper motions differ by 25 plus or minus 9 milliarcseconds per year (3 sigma), ruling out a gravitationally bound binary (whose maximum Keplerian orbital motion for a 50-Jupiter-mass pair would remain below 0.1 milliarcseconds per year) and showing they are unrelated foreground objects seen in projection4.
Strict terminology matters: although temperatures near 300 kelvin overlap the planetary-mass regime depending on age, Bradač et al. (2026) classify Bullet-BD1 and Bullet-BD2 strictly as ultra-cool Y-type brown dwarfs—not confirmed planets—because neither mass is dynamically measured4. Similarly, their inferred distances (160 +20/−10 parsecs or 240 ± 40 parsecs for Bullet-BD1; 580 +50/−70 or +60/−50 parsecs for Bullet-BD2) explicitly fix the substellar radius to one Jupiter radius without propagating radius uncertainty4. Pairing those model distances with the measured proper motions implies physically plausible Milky Way tangential speeds of 37 to 56 kilometers per second for Bullet-BD1 and 66 kilometers per second for Bullet-BD26.

Case Study 3: CEERS-93316 — Extragalactic Photometric Refutation Without Local Origin

A balanced audit must not assume every failed high-redshift dropout is a Milky Way star. Early Cycle 1 JWST imaging flagged CEERS-93316 as a bright galaxy candidate near photometric redshift 16.4 (Donnan et al. 2023), even with F410M medium-band photometry28. Follow-up JWST NIRSpec spectroscopy by Arrabal Haro et al. (2023) refuted the redshift-16.4 hypothesis by detecting bright, unambiguous rest-frame optical emission lines—doubly ionized oxygen (495.9 and 500.7 nanometers), hydrogen-beta, and hydrogen-alpha—fixing the spectroscopic redshift at 4.91223.
CEERS-93316 demonstrates two key lessons. First, dust attenuation combined with intense nebular lines at redshift 4 to 5 creates a severe broadband degeneracy that fools photometric redshift algorithms24. Second, revising CEERS-93316 from redshift 16.4 down to redshift 4.912 exhibits opposite physical-size behavior under competing cosmologies: while its standard-benchmark luminosity distance drops from roughly 181,050 to 45,689 megaparsecs (reducing inferred intrinsic luminosity by roughly 15.7 times), its standard-benchmark angular-diameter distance actually increases from 598.0 to 1,307.2 megaparsecs—making its inferred physical radius 2.19 times larger rather than smaller6. Under a monotonic static Euclidean propagation law, by contrast, lowering redshift from 16.4 to 4.912 reduces both inferred distance and physical radius simultaneously6.

Case Study 4: JADES-GS-z14-0 — Multi-Facility Confirmation Overcoming Interloper Suspicion

JADES-GS-z14-0 provides the clearest empirical counterweight proving that bright, unexpected JWST dropouts are not uniformly local or low-redshift interlopers. Found in GOODS-South imaging from JADES and FRESCO, JADES-GS-z14-0 was initially flagged as a probable low-redshift interloper (Hainline et al. 2024; Williams et al. 2024) because of its high brightness and a redshift-3.475 foreground neighbor only 0.4 arcseconds away1. Deep JWST NIRSpec prism spectroscopy by Carniani et al. (2024) overturned the low-redshift flag via a sharp Lyman break (redshift 14.32 +0.08/−0.20), a tentative 3.6-sigma detection of the ionized-carbon doublet (190.7/190.9 nanometers) at 2.89 micrometers (redshift 14.178 ± 0.013), and excess MIRI F770W flux pointing to rest-optical oxygen and hydrogen-beta emission1.
Even more compellingly, blind ALMA submillimeter spectroscopy (Schouws et al. 2024/2025) detected a 6.6-sigma emission line at 223.528 plus or minus 0.009 gigahertz within 0.12 arcseconds of the JWST centroid (well inside the 0.18-arcsecond positional uncertainty of the 1.08-arcsecond ALMA beam)1. Identifying this line as the 88-micrometer doubly ionized oxygen fine-structure transition locks the spectroscopic redshift at 14.1793 plus or minus 0.0007—improving precision over NIRSpec by more than 180 times, matching the tentative UV carbon doublet to four significant figures, and measuring a velocity line width of 136 plus or minus 31 kilometers per second1. Although alternative continuum models have been explored—including a 2025 PNAS study fitting the NIRSpec continuum and extended profile (0.27 arcseconds in that Sérsic model versus a 260-parsec benchmark radius in JADES/ALMA) as a zero-metallicity supermassive dark star near redshift 14.44–14.5269—the blind 6.6-sigma ALMA oxygen detection confirms both heavy-element enrichment and an identical wavelength shift spanning a factor of 460 in rest-frame wavelength. No Milky Way brown dwarf or intermediate-redshift dusty galaxy reproduces a 1.85-micrometer break, a 2.89-micrometer carbon doublet, a 7.7-micrometer excess, and a 223.5-gigahertz submillimeter oxygen line simultaneously.

Case Study 5: GN-z11 — Multi-Line, Multi-Instrument High-Redshift Control

GN-z11 in GOODS-North anchors our highest-confidence spectroscopic control tier. Initially selected from Hubble Space Telescope grism spectroscopy (Oesch et al. 2016)35, GN-z11 was secured at redshift 10.603 by Bunker et al. (2023) using high-signal-to-noise (above 20 per spectral resolution element) NIRSpec prism and medium-resolution grating detections of multiple rest-frame ultraviolet emission lines alongside a crisp Lyman-alpha break8. Deep Cycle 4 G140M spectroscopy by the SPURS Collaboration (2026) refined the systemic redshift to 10.6016 and measured resolved profiles for Lyman-alpha (observed at 1.412 micrometers), four-times ionized nitrogen (1.444 micrometers), excited neutral oxygen (1.514 micrometers), triply ionized nitrogen (1.725 micrometers), the doubly ionized nitrogen quintuplet, and the doubly ionized carbon doublet (1.907/1.909 micrometers)25. Complementary JWST observations revealed a rest-UV Balmer-like continuum excess near 354.6 nanometers (Ji et al. 2025) and rest-optical hydrogen emission via MIRI medium-resolution spectroscopy (Álvarez-Márquez et al. 2025)36. While astronomers continue debating whether GN-z11’s compact nuclear region (benchmark effective radius below 100 parsecs) is dominated by an intense metal-poor starburst or an accreting active black hole8, its multi-transition fractional wavelength shift of 10.603 is a robust observational fact that any physical cosmology must address directly rather than reclassifying as a local star.

Why Proper Motion Is Not an Independent Parallax

Interpreting 2026 brown-dwarf discoveries requires a strict conceptual barrier between proper motion and trigonometric parallax. Proper motion measures only angular velocity on the sky; translating it into distance requires either assuming a Galactic velocity prior (such as the Besançon simulation)2 or fitting an atmospheric luminosity/radius model and checking whether the resulting tangential speed appears realistic. Table 5 of Liu et al. (2026b) starkly exposes the hazard of parallax-free substellar distances: whereas JADES-GS-BD-5 possesses a direct trigonometric parallax of 15.7 (+1.4/−1.3) milliarcseconds fixing its distance cleanly at 64 (+6/−5) parsecs (tangential speed 15.0 kilometers per second), the faint moving candidate JADES-GN-BD-16 lacks a parallax and yields an SED-inferred distance of 960 (+450/−310) parsecs that—paired with its proper motion of 233 (+192/−91) milliarcseconds per year—implies an extreme, likely unphysical tangential speed of 1,082 (+982/−486) kilometers per second21. Until direct multi-epoch trigonometric parallax is achieved, model-derived brown-dwarf distances can err by factors of two or more.

Deep Audit of the Premier Extragalactic Anchor: NGC 4258

Beyond the Magellanic Clouds, water megamasers orbiting the central supermassive black hole of NGC 4258 serve as the single most pivotal geometric extragalactic anchor, calibrating Cepheid and tip-of-the-red-giant-branch zero-points while avoiding the greater-than-10-magnitude dynamic-range gap between Milky Way and extragalactic Cepheids5. VLBI monitors angular sky positions, Doppler line-of-sight velocities, and line-of-sight centripetal accelerations of maser clouds in a warped accretion disk, geometrically breaking the black-hole mass–distance degeneracy5.
However, van der Boom and Kushnir’s (2026) reanalysis (MNRAS 550, stag1327) uncovered a substantial latent systematic in the archival dataset5. Canonical determinations—Humphreys et al. (2013) at 7.60 ± 0.17 (statistical) ± 0.15 (systematic) megaparsecs and Reid, Pesce, and Riess (2019) at 7.576 ± 0.082 (statistical) ± 0.076 (systematic) megaparsecs—averaged heterogeneous 1994–2004 VLBA epochs into 358 to 370 collective maser entries, fitting 716 to 740 nuisance polar coordinates plus five free error-floor parameters5. By marginalizing analytically over individual azimuth and angular-radius nuisance variables, van der Boom and Kushnir compressed the global disk model to 14 free parameters and closely reproduced Reid et al.’s distance (7.547 to 7.551 megaparsecs) on the averaged dataset, confirming numerical consistency5. Yet when they abandoned data averaging to track individual, spatially resolved maser components across epochs, most archival epochs lacked sufficient resolution and had to be excluded, and statistically robust model fits spanned 6.7 to 8.1 megaparsecs5. Because maser amplification is exponential, small fluctuations in seed radiation drive large intensity and spectral shifts across historical 4-to-8-month observing gaps, rendering spot-to-spot identification ambiguous5. Even this premier geometric anchor therefore harbors a roughly 20 percent methodological envelope until high-cadence VLBI monitoring resolves individual spot trajectories.

Complementing this single-anchor audit, a September 2026 Bayesian forward-modeling reanalysis of all six Megamaser Cosmology Project galaxies reproduced NGC 4258 at 7.558 plus or minus 0.073 (statistical) megaparsecs on the published spot catalog while showing that correcting the five more distant megamaser hosts (spanning roughly 50 to 150 megaparsecs) with non-linear peculiar-velocity reconstructions (Manticore-Local) lowers the inferred local recession slope by 1.6 to 2.1 kilometers per second per megaparsec—from 73.9 ± 3.0 (Pesce et al. 2020) down to 71.6 ± 3.7 kilometers per second per megaparsec18. Meanwhile, Freedman’s 2026 review documents cumulative 1.4 to 4 percent calibration shifts across SH0ES Cepheid and supernova rungs between 2016 and 2022, including a 0.035-magnitude Cepheid distance-modulus change, a 0.040-magnitude LMC Wesenheit revision, and a 4 percent drop in the Milky Way-calibrated Hubble slope from 76.18 to 73.02 kilometers per second per megaparsec19. Independent geometric anchors therefore robustly establish an extragalactic scale extending from roughly 50 kiloparsecs (Magellanic Clouds) through several megaparsecs (NGC 4258) to roughly 150 megaparsecs (MCP galaxies), while percent-to-ten-percent systematics remain active at every rung.

Angular Versus Physical Size and Surface Brightness Scaling

Research Question 6 and Work Package E confront the central geometric test of ArcSecs Premise 11 (“Objects in the universe are smaller and closer than they appear”). Transforming a measured angular radius into a physical linear radius depends directly on the angular-diameter distance predicted at a source’s redshift. To evaluate objectively when alternative propagation laws predict smaller versus larger physical sizes, we executed a four-model numerical comparison (analysis/angular_size_models.py, verified via Python quadrature integration during research preparation) anchored to a common fiducial local Hubble length of 4,282.75 megaparsecs (local slope 70.0 kilometers per second per megaparsec)6:

  1. Benchmark Model 1 — Standard Flat Expanding Benchmark: The conventional Friedmann–Robertson–Walker flat calculation reference (matter density 0.3, dark-energy parameter 0.7), documented solely as a comparative benchmark6. Its angular-diameter distance rises to a turnaround peak of 1,747.57 megaparsecs (8.47 kiloparsecs per arcsecond) at redshift 1.605, and thereafter decreases monotonically to 824.3 megaparsecs at redshift 10.603 and 668.1 megaparsecs at redshift 14.1796.
  2. Model 2 — Static Euclidean Exponential Tired Light: A non-expanding Euclidean law in which photon energy declines exponentially with distance at the local Hubble attenuation scale (distance equal to the Hubble length multiplied by the natural logarithm of one plus redshift), causing angular-diameter distance to rise monotonically with redshift6.
  3. Model 3A — Bounded / Asymptotic Decelerating-Propagation Model: A non-expanding Euclidean distance law in which distance equals the Hubble length multiplied by redshift divided by one plus redshift, approaching an asymptotic horizon at one Hubble length6.
  4. Model 3B — Steep-Attenuation Local-Redshift Model: An illustrative non-expanding exponential model with an effective attenuation scale five times shorter than the local Hubble length (856.6 megaparsecs), testing how compressed a non-expanding scale must become to yield smaller radii than the expanding benchmark6.

Deciding the Sign: Smaller Versus Larger Physical Sizes and Surface-Brightness Data

These executed calculations resolve Research Question 6 clearly and prevent confirmation bias. Objects are demonstrably closer and smaller only under two specific conditions: when an unverified photometric redshift is overturned by foreground reclassification (Capotauro, Bullet-BD1, Bullet-BD2, UNCOVER-BD-1, and UNCOVER-BD-2), or if a confirmed high-redshift source’s wavelength shift were predominantly non-cosmological (intrinsic) so that its physical distance sat far below the standard angular-diameter distance (below 668 megaparsecs for JADES-GS-z14-0 or below 824 megaparsecs for GN-z11)2.
Conversely, for spectroscopically confirmed extragalactic sources whose redshifts accumulate along cosmological paths anchored to the local Hubble slope, both standard static exponential tired light and bounded asymptotic propagation predict substantially larger physical radii than the expanding benchmark6. Under static exponential tired light, JADES-GS-z14-0’s benchmark 260-parsec effective radius (roughly 0.080 arcseconds) expands to 4.53 kiloparsecs (17.44 times larger), and bounded propagation expands it to 1.56 kiloparsecs (5.99 times larger); GN-z11’s compact benchmark core similarly grows by 12.74 times under static tired light1. Notably, this explains why several non-expanding cosmologists (Lopez-Corredoira 2014; Lerner 2018; LaViolette 2021) argued that static tired light eliminates the need for strong galaxy size evolution—specifically because static geometry scales high-redshift galaxies up to familiar kiloparsec-scale local sizes rather than shrinking them26.
Four observable diagnostics decide the empirical sign. First, dynamical velocity dispersions scale linearly with radius in virial enclosed-mass estimates: ALMA’s 136 ± 31 kilometer per second ionized-oxygen line width in JADES-GS-z14-0 yields roughly 1 billion solar masses at 260 parsecs, but scales up to roughly 17 billion solar masses under static tired light—a difference testable against stellar mass budgets and lensing profiles1. Second, surface-brightness scaling (the Tolman test) in the 531,173-object ASTRODEEP-JWST catalog (Merlin et al. 2024; Tsymbal et al. 2026) exhibits a raw observer-frame decline proportional to one plus redshift to the minus 1.3 power, far flatter than the minus 3.3 to minus 4.0 non-evolving expanding baseline26. Third, aperture, selection, and deblending controls strongly influence measured surface-brightness slopes: Desmond, Yasin, Stiskalek, and von Hausegger (2026) showed that while PSF and deep-field sky noise alter the slope by less than 0.005, aperture compactness cuts shift it by 0.06 and switching Voronoi to Gaussian-weighted deblending shifts it by 0.15, while IllustrisTNG intrinsic galaxy luminosity-density evolution (power-law exponent 2.23 plus or minus 0.20) reproduces both ASTRODEEP surface brightness and ultracompact radio distance-duality scaling (2.50 plus or minus 0.26)26. Fourth, rest-frame wavelength matching (measuring optical profiles up to 4.4 micrometers across 1,395 CEERS galaxies, Ormerod et al. 2024) limits dust and bandpass biases57. Because intrinsic luminosity evolution and geometric distance scaling remain degenerate in raw surface-brightness plots, deciding the sign decisively requires direct dynamical-mass mapping and spectroscopic-only deblending audits rather than broadband surface brightness alone.

Programmatic Red-Team Pipeline and Independent Held-Out Validation

To satisfy Work Package F without post-hoc tuning, we define an automated reclassification pipeline tailored to public JWST deep-field archives. Because raw JWST FITS mosaics were not downloaded during this commission, this seven-stage protocol is explicitly delivered as a preregistered, unexecuted methodological specification; data-download coordinates and validation checks are logged in companion METHODS_TO_REPRODUCE.md.

  1. Multi-Epoch Registration and Astrometric Alignment: Acquire calibrated NIRCam mosaics spanning cycles separated by at least 1.0 to 3.5 years. Align Epoch 2 frames to Epoch 1 using a local network of at least 50 compact, high-signal-to-noise background reference sources anchored to Gaia DR3 where available, applying iterative sigma-clipping to achieve sub-10-milliarcsecond local registration (matching Liu et al. 2026a’s 68-source reference frame)2.
  2. Filter-Matched PSF Modeling and Dual Deblending: Construct empirical PSFs per filter and epoch, extract PSF-matched aperture photometry alongside PSF-fitting centroids, and run both Voronoi and Gaussian-weighted deblending passes (following Desmond et al. 2026)26 to flag close-neighbor blending (such as JADES-GS-z14-0’s 0.4-arcsecond neighbor)1.
  3. Synthetic Injection-Recovery Calibration: Inject 1,000 synthetic point sources and compact Sérsic galaxies per field across magnitudes 26.0 to 29.0 AB with zero motion and known displacements (10 to 100 milliarcseconds per year) directly into the Epoch 1 and Epoch 2 mosaics to calibrate empirical centroiding bias, proper-motion completeness, and false-motion rates.
  4. Joint Proper-Motion and Parallax Extraction: Fit multi-epoch joint astrometric models to extract total proper motion and—where three or more epochs sample opposite phases of Earth’s orbit—annual trigonometric parallax (replicating Liu et al. 2026b’s JADES-GS-BD-5 method)21. Any source exceeding 5-sigma proper motion is automatically reclassified as Galactic regardless of photometric redshift.
  5. Competing Spectral Template Tournament: Fit multi-band fluxes (incorporating F410M/F430M/F460M/F480M medium bands where available) concurrently against four mandatory libraries without an extragalactic-only prior: standard Lyman-break star-forming galaxies, heavily dust-reddened intermediate-redshift galaxies with extreme rest-optical line equivalent widths, compact reddened active galactic nuclei, and ultracool substellar brown dwarf grids (ATMO2020, Sonora Elf Owl, LOWZ, and SAND across 250 to 1,500 kelvin and subsolar metallicities)3. Apply Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate control across full field catalogs to suppress multiple-comparison artifacts.
  6. Mandatory Human Spectral Review Gate: Any candidate with photometric redshift above 12 and unresolved angular profile (FWHM within 15 percent of the empirical PSF) is barred from entering a “confirmed high-redshift” catalog until either (a) multi-transition spectroscopy (at least two independent lines, or one spectral break plus one greater-than-5-sigma atomic/submillimeter line) passes blinded dual-reviewer inspection, or (b) multi-epoch proper motion is bounded below 10 milliarcseconds per year at greater than 3 sigma.
  7. Blind Held-Out Field Split: Tune all compactness thresholds, injection calibrations, and Bayesian template priors exclusively on a Development Set comprising CEERS, Abell 2744 (UNCOVER/GLASS), and the Bullet Cluster2. Freeze every parameter and record configuration checksums before running a single untouched validation pass on two Blind Held-Out Fields: PRIMER-COSMOS and PRIMER-UDS (included in public ASTRODEEP-JWST, VizieR J/A+A/691/A240)26, recording out-of-sample confusion matrices in companion CONFUSION_MATRICES.csv.

Discussion and Non-Negotiable Scientific Verdict

Synthesizing all six research questions produces a disciplined scientific verdict that separates verified measurement vulnerabilities from unsupported universal generalizations. On the constructive side for ArcSecs skepticism, deep-field astronomy has repeatedly demonstrated that single-epoch broadband photometry is an unreliable substitute for direct geometry. In 2026 alone, multi-epoch astrometry and follow-up spectroscopy moved Capotauro, Bullet-BD1, and Bullet-BD2 inward from proposed extragalactic distances to hundreds of parsecs inside the Milky Way, joining earlier UNCOVER T-dwarf AGN impostors and dusty intermediate-redshift emission-line interlopers like CEERS-933162. Beyond individual dropouts, archival geometric anchors themselves harbor nontrivial methodological sensitivities: replacing epoch-averaging with individual maser spot tracking widens NGC 4258’s statistically robust distance range to 6.7–8.1 megaparsecs5, Gaia DR3 requires empirical magnitude-, color-, and position-dependent zero-point and proper-motion error corrections44, and ASTRODEEP-JWST surface-brightness slopes shift by 0.15 solely from deblending algorithm choices26. Auditing provenance before accepting cataloged distances is empirically warranted.
At the same time, two independent empirical pillars prevent generalizing these corrections into a universal claim that all distant cosmic objects are closer and smaller. First, multi-line and multi-facility spectroscopy—anchored by GN-z11 (multiple UV and mid-infrared transitions at redshift 10.603), GHZ2 (redshift 12.34), and JADES-GS-z14-0 (NIRSpec UV break plus ionized-carbon doublet matched by a blind 6.6-sigma ALMA 88-micrometer ionized-oxygen detection at redshift 14.1793)—robustly secures a population of genuine high-redshift extragalactic sources that cannot be reclassified as nearby brown dwarfs1. Second, our executed geometric audit establishes that monotonic non-expanding propagation models (static exponential tired light and bounded propagation) anchored to the local Hubble scale predict 5 to 17 times LARGER, rather than smaller, physical radii for confirmed high-redshift galaxies than the standard expanding benchmark6. Rendering confirmed high-redshift galaxies physically smaller than standard estimates would require either an attenuation distance scale drastically shorter than the local Hubble length—conflicting with megamaser and eclipsing-binary geometric anchors out to 150 megaparsecs—or a large intrinsic, non-distance component to extragalactic redshifts.
Our non-negotiable final scientific decision is therefore precise: severe distance and physical-size overestimation is empirically established for specific photometrically selected subclasses—most notably compact F277W/F356W ultracool brown-dwarf impostors and dusty emission-line galaxy interlopers—and percent-to-ten-percent calibration systematics remain active inside extragalactic geometric anchors; however, a universal “closer and smaller” generalization across all distant cosmic objects is not yet demonstrated and runs opposite to the sign of standard static tired-light size predictions for spectroscopically confirmed high-redshift galaxies.

Conclusion

This independent geometry-first audit shows that enforcing a strict boundary between raw detector observables and model-dependent inference clarifies modern deep-field astronomy without requiring philosophical detours. Direct multi-epoch astrometry and follow-up spectroscopy in 2024–2026 confirmed that headline extreme-redshift dropouts such as Capotauro, Bullet-BD1, Bullet-BD2, UNCOVER-BD-1, and UNCOVER-BD-2 are foreground Milky Way brown dwarfs, while exposing dusty intermediate-redshift interlopers like CEERS-933162. Simultaneously, individual-component VLBI tracking in NGC 4258 revealed a 6.7–8.1 megaparsec systematic span beneath published percent-level anchor errors5. Yet multi-facility spectroscopy firmly anchors genuine high-redshift controls including GN-z11, GHZ2, and JADES-GS-z14-01, and executed numerical modeling proves that standard static tired-light and bounded-propagation laws predict larger rather than smaller physical radii for those confirmed systems6. Productive continuation of the ArcSecs program now hinges on targeted empirical execution: running blind proper-motion vetting on held-out JWST deep fields, pursuing monthly VLBI monitoring of NGC 4258, and constructing a testable, blur-free microphysical mechanism capable of addressing supernova light-curve durations, optical-clock frequency ratios, and photon rest-mass limits within constant universal progression T.

Companion 5: REFEREE_CHALLENGES.md (Technical Peer-Review Audit)

Challenge 1 — Does reclassifying Capotauro and the two Bullet Cluster dwarfs establish ArcSecs Premise 11 across deep extragalactic surveys? Response: It substantiates the methodological skepticism of Premise 11 for a specific, high-profile subclass—single-epoch compact F277W and F356W broadband dropouts—where distances were overestimated by five to seven orders of magnitude2. However, a targeted three-object sample cannot be extrapolated to a universal contamination rate, and multi-line spectroscopic controls (GN-z11, GHZ2, JADES-GS-z14-0) demonstrate that genuine high-redshift extragalactic sources coexist alongside foreground brown dwarfs1.
Challenge 2 — Your executed calculations show that static tired light makes confirmed high-redshift galaxies 5 to 17 times LARGER than the expanding benchmark. Doesn’t that conflict with ArcSecs Premise 11? Response: Yes—for spectroscopically confirmed extragalactic sources evaluated under monotonic static tired-light laws anchored to the local Hubble length, inferred physical radii are strictly larger, not smaller, than in the expanding benchmark6. Highlighting this sign reversal defines a concrete theoretical fork for ArcSecs: either confirmed high-redshift galaxies are physically larger (alleviating compactness tensions while requiring revision of Premise 11’s universal “smaller” claim for confirmed galaxies), or an alternative ArcSecs framework must derive a substantial intrinsic, non-distance redshift mechanism that compresses high-redshift distances without violating 10-to-150-megaparsec megamaser calibrations.
Challenge 3 — Why didn’t Capotauro’s earlier proper-motion non-detection prevent misclassification, and how secure is Liu et al.’s 2026 detection? Response: Early observations lacked a sufficient time baseline, yielding only a coarse upper limit near 137 milliarcseconds per year—well above Capotauro’s actual motion4. Extending the JWST NIRCam baseline to 3.5 years (December 2022 to June 2026), anchoring 68 compact reference sources, and validating via four-image injection-recovery enabled Liu et al. (2026a) to measure a 132 ± 20 milliarcsecond shift (37.6 +5.5/−5.6 milliarcseconds per year), ruling out an extragalactic origin beyond 6 sigma2. Its distance (730 ± 110 parsecs) remains model-dependent and awaits direct trigonometric parallax (Test EXP-05).
Challenge 4 — If NGC 4258 individual maser tracking spans 6.7 to 8.1 megaparsecs, does that collapse the extragalactic distance scale? Response: No. A 6.7-to-8.1-megaparsec sensitivity span represents roughly a ±10 percent methodological envelope—large enough to bear directly on percent-level Hubble-constant calibration disputes, but incapable of relocating megaparsec-scale galaxies into the Milky Way5. It demonstrates that geometric anchors are orders of magnitude more secure than broadband dropouts yet still require monthly VLBI cadence to achieve claimed 1 percent precision.
Challenge 5 — How can ArcSecs retain constant universal progression T when Type Ia supernovae exhibit light-curve durations scaling as one plus redshift to the 1.003 ± 0.015 power? Response: This observation is logged explicitly as Constraint REQ-01 and identified as a currently missing physical mechanism12. Pure photon energy loss at fixed light speed across a static path fails REQ-01. Within constant T, matching both wavelength shift and arrival-interval stretching requires either physical kinematic recession or a mathematically consistent secular deceleration of light speed that also satisfies local laboratory and planetary-ranging bounds (Test EXP-06) without introducing chromatic dispersion or angular blurring. No completed microphysical derivation yet meets all criteria concurrently.
Challenge 6 — Are Bullet-BD1 and Bullet-BD2 confirmed rogue planets? Response: No. While temperatures near 300 kelvin overlap mature giant-planet atmospheres (similar to JADES-GS-BD-5’s benchmark status as a planetary-mass analogue), neither object has a direct dynamical mass measurement, and Bradač et al. (2026) classify both strictly as ultra-cool Y-type brown dwarfs4.
Challenge 7 — Does Desmond et al. (2026) completely resolve the Tolman surface-brightness test against non-expanding models? Response: Desmond et al. (2026) show that raw ASTRODEEP-JWST surface brightness (exponent −1.3) and radio distance-duality data are consistent with galaxy luminosity-density evolution in IllustrisTNG (exponent 2.23 ± 0.20)26. Simultaneously, they show that the raw trend lies between non-evolving expansion and static tired light and shifts by 0.15 from deblending choices alone26. Because luminosity evolution is simulation-inferred rather than directly measured geometrically, surface-brightness tests remain model-degenerate and require independent kinematic mass mapping (Test EXP-04) and spec-z-only deblending audits (Test EXP-02).
