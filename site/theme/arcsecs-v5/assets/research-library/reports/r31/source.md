### **Solar Deflection and Radio Interferometry**

The modern observational record began with the May 29, 1919 total solar eclipse expeditions to Sobral, Brazil, and Principe, West Africa, organized by Dyson, Eddington, and Davidson (1920)6. Against a background of bright Hyades stars, the Sobral 4-inch astrographic plates yielded an extrapolated solar-limb deflection reanalyzed in modern commentary at 1.90 ± 0.11 arcseconds, while cloudy plates from Principe yielded 1.61 ± 0.31 arcseconds16. Problematic, thermally distorted 16-inch Sobral plates yielded 0.93 arcseconds if plate scale was left as a free parameter or 1.52 arcseconds assuming no scale change, reanalyzed in modern work as 1.55 ± 0.34 arcseconds16. Crucially, the higher-quality 4-inch and Principe sets aligned with approximately 1.75 arcseconds rather than the 0.87 arcsecond prediction of a simple Newtonian scalar corpuscle calculation18. Subsequent optical eclipse campaigns (such as the 1976 Texas-Mauritanian expedition) and recent Gaia space-astrometry solar-deflection reductions were not present in the verified source retrieval for this commission and are therefore recorded as unretrieved comparison benchmarks rather than cited with unverified figures.  
Radio Very Long Baseline Interferometry (VLBI) eliminated solar-eclipse weather and optical daylight limitations by measuring angular positions of extragalactic radio quasars relative to the Sun across the whole sky20. Using the parametrized post-Newtonian (PPN) deflection parameter gamma strictly as an operational scaling benchmark (where gamma of 1 corresponds to 1.75 arcseconds at the solar limb and gamma of 0 corresponds to the 0.87 arcsecond scalar-corpuscle baseline), Lebach et al. (1995) measured a solar deflection ratio of 0.9998 ± 0.000822. Analyzing 1979–1999 geodetic VLBI data, Shapiro et al. (2004) obtained gamma equal to 0.99983 ± 0.0004520. Extending the dataset through 2009 and 2010 with more than 1.7 million Very Long Baseline Array (VLBA) observations, Lambert and Le Poncin-Lafitte (2009, 2011\) tightened the measurement from 0.99984 ± 0.00015 to 0.99992 ± 0.0001225. Using phase-referenced VLBA observations of 3C 279 and 3C 273 at 15, 23, and 43 GHz, Fomalont et al. (2009) achieved 0.0003 precision while showing that 43 GHz observations suppress solar corona plasma refraction to negligible levels beyond 3 degrees from the Sun25. More recently, Titov et al. (2018) analyzed targeted geodetic VLBI sessions near the Sun to reach an operational precision of approximately 9 × 10⁻⁵ on gamma20. Planetary lensing has also been directly measured with Jupiter as the deflector, yielding gamma of 1.01 ± 0.03 (Fomalont and Kopeikin 2008\) and 0.984 ± 0.037 (Li et al. 2022b), along with a retarded-deflection ratio of 0.98 ± 0.1928. Next-generation Square Kilometre Array VLBI (SKA-VLBI) simulations project microarcsecond astrometry capable of reaching 10⁻⁷ sensitivity on solar gamma and 10⁻⁴ on Jupiter28.

### **Spacecraft Signal Delay (Shapiro Delay)**

Rather than measuring transverse bending angles, Bertotti, Iess, and Tortora (2003) measured the round-trip gravitational signal delay and fractional frequency shift of coherent radio waves passing the Sun during the 21 June 2002 solar conjunction of the Cassini spacecraft at a geocentric distance of 8.43 astronomical units (AU)7. With a minimum impact parameter of 1.6 solar radii and 18 tracking passes compressed into 1,094 data points at 300-second integration, the team achieved root-mean-square two-way Doppler residuals of 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ Hz on an 8.4 GHz carrier, corresponding to a one-way range-rate precision of 2.2 × 10⁻⁶ meters per second31. By implementing a multi-frequency X-band and Ka-band link measuring three distinct phases on the ground, Cassini directly measured and removed dispersive coronal plasma noise—even during major coronal mass ejections observed simultaneously by SOHO—yielding PPN parameter gamma equal to 1 plus (2.1 ± 2.3) × 10⁻⁵7. While secondary commentary has questioned whether unverified gravitomagnetic modeling assumptions expand the experimental uncertainty32, the 2.3 × 10⁻⁵ bound remains the tightest peer-reviewed solar-system benchmark for light propagation delay7. Proposed interplanetary optical-clock missions near Earth-Sun Lagrange point L1 aim to push signal-delay sensitivity toward 10⁻⁸33.

### **Nuclear Resonance and Space-Clock Frequency Shifts**

Precision experiments comparing emitter and absorber frequencies across gravitational potential differences provide a third empirical pillar. Pound and Rebka (1960) and Pound and Snider (1964, 1965\) used the recoilless 14.4 keV Mössbauer nuclear transition of iron-57 across a 22.5-meter (75-foot) vertical path in the Jefferson Physical Laboratory at Harvard34. Operationally, the experiment measured the tiny Doppler velocity required to restore resonant gamma-ray absorption between the upper and lower stations, recovering 0.9990 ± 0.0076 (with an estimated systematic error limit of 0.010) of the predicted fractional two-way frequency shift of 4.905 × 10⁻¹⁵35. Vessot and Levine (1980) elevated this test to space in the 1976 Gravity Probe A (GP-A) mission, launching a hydrogen-maser frequency standard nearly vertically to 10,000 kilometers altitude and tracking its continuous two-way microwave link against ground masers for roughly two hours, verifying the gravitational frequency shift to 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ (with frequency-shift agreement at 7 × 10⁻⁵)34. In 2018 and 2019, Delva et al. analyzed 1,008 days of onboard passive hydrogen maser clock-bias telemetry from two European Galileo satellites (GSAT0201 and GSAT0202) inadvertently injected into eccentric orbits in 201436. After modeling Earth oblateness (40 picosecond peak-to-peak effect) and lunar-solar tides (12 picoseconds) against a 400-nanosecond peak-to-peak orbital potential modulation, Delva et al. constrained fractional deviations from the gravitational potential frequency scaling to (0.19 ± 2.48) × 10⁻⁵, representing a factor of 5.6 improvement over GP-A38. A complementary analysis of 899 days of circular Galileo satellite data constrained the same deviation to (0.29 ± 2.00) × 10⁻² at one standard deviation40.

### **Strong Gravitational Lensing, Shadows, and Multi-Messenger Timing**

On galactic and extragalactic scales, strong gravitational lensing produces multiple images, Einstein rings, and measurable arrival-time delays between images41. The Sloan Lens ACS (SLACS) survey cataloged 85 galaxy-galaxy lenses with multi-band Hubble Space Telescope follow-up, augmented by 40 lower-mass systems in S4TM and 28 quasar lenses in SQLS42. In 2025, a TDCOSMO collaboration manuscript analyzed eight strongly lensed quasars (TDCOSMO-2025) incorporating James Webb Space Telescope (JWST-NIRSpec) stellar velocity dispersions for five deflectors to break the well-known Mass-Sheet Degeneracy (MSD), wherein rescaling a lens galaxy's radial mass profile simultaneously rescales inferred cosmological distances42. Note that SN Refsdal (Kelly et al. 2015\) was outside the verified primary records retrieved for this commission and is not cited with unverified numbers.  
At event-horizon angular scales, the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) imaged the core of giant elliptical galaxy M87 at 1.3 millimeters wavelength (230 GHz) with roughly 25 microarcsecond diffraction-limited resolution, revealing in April 2017 an asymmetric bright emission ring with diameter 42 ± 3 microarcseconds and central flux depression exceeding 10:144. Independent April 2018 observations incorporating the Greenland Telescope confirmed a persistent median ring diameter of 43.3 (+1.5, −3.1) microarcseconds while detecting a roughly 30-degree shift in the southern brightness peak from southeast to southwest, interpreted in magnetohydrodynamic libraries as accretion turbulence around a central mass near (6.5 ± 0.7) × 10⁹ solar masses45. Meanwhile, the coincident detection of gravitational wave GW170817 and short gamma-ray burst GRB 170817A (LIGO/Virgo/Fermi/INTEGRAL 2017\) established that the gamma-ray burst followed the gravitational wave signal by approximately 1.7 seconds (1.74 seconds in conference summary), bounding the fractional speed difference between gravitational waves and gamma rays to between minus 3 × 10⁻¹⁵ and plus 7 × 10⁻¹⁵49.

Theoretical frameworks accommodating a nonzero invariant photon rest mass fall into three main classes. Proca electrodynamics adds an explicit mass term to the electromagnetic Lagrangian, breaking gauge invariance, making vector and scalar potentials directly observable, introducing a longitudinal polarization mode, and causing static fields to decay exponentially over the Compton length4. The Stueckelberg mechanism (Ruegg and Ruiz-Altaba 2004\) introduces an auxiliary scalar field to restore gauge invariance and renormalizability while keeping the photon massive55. The Abelian Higgs mechanism (Adelberger, Dvali, and Gruzinov 2007; 2026 phase-diagram study arXiv:2609.26888) generates photon mass dynamically from a Higgs vacuum expectation value that can be locally restored by strong magnetic fields or screened by superconducting vortices4.  
Empirically, the PDG (2025) summary lists an official upper limit of 1 × 10⁻¹⁸ eV3, grounded in Ryutov's magnetohydrodynamic analysis of the solar wind60. Laboratory concentric-sphere tests (Williams, Faller, and Hill 1971\) constrain Coulomb-law deviations to an inverse-square power shift q of (2.7 ± 3.1) × 10⁻¹⁶ and a rest mass below 1.6 × 10⁻⁴⁷ grams9. ## **Strong-Field Follow-Through and the Nonzero Rest-Mass Branch**

Work Packages C and D examine whether strong-field astrophysical imaging and nonzero-rest-mass electrodynamics separate "gravity acts on light" from "gravity attracts all photons equally," and whether any existing measurement constitutes a positive invariant rest-mass discovery.

### **Strong-Field Lensing, Mass-Sheet Degeneracy, and Compact Shadows**

Testing PGFCM beyond the weak solar field requires confronting galaxy-scale Einstein rings, cluster arcs, quasar time delays, and compact-object shadows. In galaxy-galaxy and galaxy-quasar lensing (such as the 85 SLACS lenses and TDCOSMO sample42), the same two-component flat-coordinate field deflection law integrates along thin-lens density projections to produce Einstein rings, multiple images, and geometric-plus-potential arrival delays. Crucially, the TDCOSMO-2025 analysis highlights the **Mass-Sheet Degeneracy (MSD)**: imaging observables alone (ring radii, arc distortions, and flux ratios) remain mathematically invariant under a coordinated rescaling of the deflector's internal mass profile and the inferred source-lens distance scale42. While standard cosmology breaks MSD by calibrating absolute lens mass via JWST-NIRSpec stellar velocity dispersions and assuming a Lambda-CDM expansion history43, the existence of MSD confirms that strong-lensing geometry by itself does not uniquely fix cosmic distances—providing an empirically grounded opening to test ArcSecs Premise 11 ("Objects in the universe are smaller and closer than they appear") whenever independent non-cosmological mass or distance anchors change.  
At the extreme field strengths of M87\*, EHT observations at 1.3 mm reveal a persistent 42 to 43.3 microarcsecond emission ring surrounding a central brightness depression deeper than 10:145. Under PGFCM, an ultra-compact central mass concentrates gravitational field density so intensely that local radial photon speed drops toward zero at a critical trapping radius, while grazing rays orbit multiple times before escaping—forming a persistent photon-ring boundary without invoking a curved spacetime throat or singularity fabric. However, three empirical cautions apply: first, the 2017 and 2018 EHT brightness peaks shifted by roughly 30 degrees due to turbulent magnetized accretion flow45; second, M87\* flared by a factor of two in very-high-energy gamma rays (\>100 GeV) within 36 hours in April 201848; and third, Bisnovatyi-Kogan, Perlick, and Tsupko (2015, 2017\) demonstrated that dense surrounding plasma alters shadow diameters and produces chromatic image magnification12. Consequently, current single-frequency 230 GHz EHT ring diameters cannot distinguish weak-field-calibrated PGFCM from standard metric predictions, nor can they isolate a tiny photon rest mass against plasma-accretion astrophysics.

### **Proca, Stueckelberg, and Abelian Higgs Extensions: Extra Observables and Model Dependence**

If photons carry a tiny nonzero invariant rest mass (Work Package D, Question 3), electromagnetic propagation departs from strict scale invariance in five specific ways4. First, a massive spin-1 field possesses **three polarization states**—two transverse modes plus one longitudinal polarization mode—though in minimal Proca theory coupling of the longitudinal mode to conserved electric currents is suppressed by the square of the ratio of rest-mass energy to photon energy, rendering laboratory production negligible at optical and radio frequencies unless a Stueckelberg or Higgs scalar interacts directly53. Second, static electrostatic potentials and magnetostatic dipole fields undergo **Yukawa exponential screening** beyond the massive-photon Compton wavelength, underpinning the 1971 Wesleyan concentric-sphere test and solar-wind magnetic bounds3. Third, **vacuum group-velocity dispersion** causes higher-frequency photons to outpace lower-frequency photons, producing an arrival-time lag proportional to rest mass squared divided by frequency squared60. Fourth, as derived by Accioly and Paszko (2004) and Accioly et al. (2010) for weak-field gravitational scattering of Proca photons, nonzero rest mass adds a positive correction to **gravitational deflection**, causing lower-frequency photons to bend slightly more than high-frequency photons as they approach the massive-particle deflection regime26; similarly, the *Physical Review D* (2025, arXiv:2412.09806) analysis derived the massive-photon correction to strong-lensing image time delays in a point-mass external-shear lens62. Fifth, in **Stueckelberg and Abelian Higgs extensions**, preserving gauge invariance alters the weak mixing angle and induces photon-neutrino couplings57, while in Abelian Higgs models strong magnetic fields restore gauge symmetry into Type I or Type II superconducting vortex phases—allowing the photon to remain effectively massless inside magnetized galaxies while carrying mass up to 3 × 10⁻¹³ eV in Type II extragalactic space (or higher in symmetry-restored regimes if FRB constraints are evaded)58.

### **Independent Audit of PDG and FRB Photon-Mass Claims**

An independent audit of the Particle Data Group (2025) summary3 and recent FRB analyses10 confirms that **no verified measurement constitutes a positive detection of invariant photon rest mass**. The PDG (2025) bound of mass below 1 × 10⁻¹⁸ eV3 relies on Ryutov's magnetohydrodynamic analysis of the solar wind60 and is model-dependent on Proca electrodynamics without Higgs vortex screening59. Torsion-balance limits (Lakes 1998; Luo et al. 2003; Tu 2006\) rely on assuming a uniform cosmic magnetic vector potential across galactic scales, an assumption invalidated if Higgs vortices exist59. Kinematic FRB dispersion bounds (Chinese Physics Letters 2024: 3.5 × 10⁻⁵¹ kg at one standard deviation11; Physics Letters B 2021: 3.9 × 10⁻⁵¹ kg at 95% confidence10; arXiv:2006.09680: 7.1 × 10⁻⁵¹ kg at 68% confidence60) depend on modeling the intergalactic ionized baryon fraction and host-galaxy plasma dispersion measure72. Notably, a 2025 multi-FRB cosmological fit (arXiv:2504.21129) reported a non-zero central parameter value of (29.4 \+5.80/−15.5) × 10⁻⁵¹ kg at one standard deviation when allowing the intergalactic baryon fraction to float freely, **explicitly noting that the result is statistically consistent with zero photon mass at two standard deviations and strongly anticorrelated with the baryon fraction**72. Treating that one-standard-deviation parameter degeneracy as a positive detection would violate scientific standards. ### **Why Multi-Frequency Lensing and Plasma Effects Are Decisive (Question 4\)**

The central technical obstacle to isolating a tiny photon rest mass in lensing or FRBs is that cold ionized plasma produces an identical inverse-square frequency dependence in signal arrival delay72. Worse still, Bisnovatyi-Kogan and Tsupko (2015, 2017, 2023\) proved that gravitational deflection in a plasma medium becomes chromatic even if the plasma is spatially homogeneous, because the plasma lowers photon group speed and alters the effective gravitational refraction term12. Crucially, however, plasma refraction and Proca invariant rest mass carry **opposite signs** in inhomogeneous diverging atmospheres such as the solar corona or galaxy halos where electron density decreases radially outward: electron density gradients act as a diverging lens, bending lower-frequency radio waves *away* from the gravitating center (decreasing total inward deflection at lower frequencies), whereas nonzero rest mass slows low-frequency photons more strongly near the potential well, bending lower-frequency waves *toward* the gravitating center (increasing total inward deflection at lower frequencies)12. Multi-frequency lensing therefore serves simultaneously as the primary confounder for arrival delays and a sign-reversing discriminant for angular deflection gradients.

## **Methods, Sampling, Dataset Provenance, Controls, and Instrumental Biases**

This investigation utilizes a three-stage source-audit and model-synthesis methodology: first, primary literature retrieval and verification against original journal publications, collaboration releases, and PDG metrology tables; second, operational deconstruction of published observables to strip away curved-spacetime coordinate assumptions and isolate raw measured angles, Doppler frequencies, clock residuals, and pulse arrival times; and third, comparative identifiability analysis across PGFCM, Proca/Stueckelberg, and plasma-lensing models.

## **Appendix D: Rigorous Technical Referee Challenges (REFEREE\_CHALLENGES.md Representation)**

**Challenge 1: If you reject curved spacetime as a physical substance, how does PGFCM avoid the factor-of-two error of Newtonian corpuscular bending without inserting an arbitrary factor of two by hand?**  
*Response:* A scalar Newtonian potential couples only to static mass-energy density, neglecting the spatial momentum flux of a relativistic packet. Following Deser's (1970) flat-coordinate self-interaction derivation8 and the optical-mechanical gradient formulation of Evans et al. (1996)69, universal field coupling to the complete energy-momentum flux tensor pairs one universal gravitational constant G with both scalar energy density and spatial momentum flux. For light, where longitudinal momentum flux equals energy density, longitudinal speed slowing near the source produces an inward wavefront tilt (0.875 arcseconds at the solar limb) while direct transverse force on the photon's energy-equivalent mass adds an equal 0.875 arcseconds, deriving the full 1.75 arcseconds and exact Cassini delay with zero ad hoc multipliers.  
**Challenge 2: Why isn't the Polarizable Vacuum (PV) model of Puthoff (2002) sufficient for ArcSecs as-is?**  
*Response:* While PV reproduces weak-field gravitational optics in flat coordinates using a variable effective refractive index67, PV physically reifies empty space as a "polarizable vacuum medium." That directly violates ArcSecs Premises 5 and 7 ("Space does not physically exist"; "Spacetime does not physically exist"). PGFCM retains the mathematical structure of local speed and momentum gradients while attributing them strictly to an energy-bearing physical gravitational field emitted by matter (Premise 6), never to empty space itself.  
**Challenge 3: If time is constant ("When") and never dilates, how do you account for the 2.48 parts in 10⁵ precision of the eccentric Galileo satellite hydrogen maser test (Delva et al. 2018\) without invoking "time dilation"?**  
*Response:* The Galileo experiment directly measured fractional frequency offsets and clock-tick bias differences between onboard passive hydrogen masers and ground reference masers across eccentric orbital potentials38. Under constant universal progression T, those tick-rate differences are physical oscillator effects: an atomic or nuclear system bound deeper in Earth's gravitational field possesses greater negative gravitational binding energy, reducing its internal mass-energy and transition frequencies relative to an identical clock at higher altitude. Both Pound-Rebka-Snider35 and Galileo38 measure physical process rates in constant T, not distortions of time itself.  
**Challenge 4: Doesn't the one-standard-deviation nonzero central value reported in arXiv:2504.21129 (29.4 × 10⁻⁵¹ kg) already prove ArcSecs Premise 1 in the invariant rest-mass sense?**  
*Response:* No, and claiming it does would violate scientific integrity. As the authors of arXiv:2504.21129 explicitly state, that one-standard-deviation fit emerges only when the intergalactic ionized baryon fraction floats freely, is strongly anticorrelated with that baryon fraction, and remains statistically consistent with zero invariant rest mass at the two-standard-deviation level72. ArcSecs Premise 1 is solidly established for energy-equivalent, inertial, and gravitational-coupling mass; invariant rest mass remains an open, tightly bounded hypothesis requiring the plasma-breaking discriminant of Experiment EXP-01 and EXP-02.  
**Challenge 5: Wouldn't a nonzero photon rest mass ruin the 10⁻⁴ achromaticity of geodetic VLBI and the 2.3 parts in 10⁵ Cassini bound?**  
*Response:* Only if the invariant rest mass is large enough to produce measurable dispersion at gigahertz frequencies. At the PDG (2025) limit of 1 × 10⁻¹⁸ eV3—or the FRB limit near 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ eV10—the fractional velocity deviation at 8.4 GHz is roughly 10⁻²⁰ or smaller, far below Cassini's 10⁻⁵ sensitivity7. As Accioly et al. (2010) showed, VLBI bounds on Proca mass sit orders of magnitude above FRB bounds26, meaning very low frequencies (sub-GHz lensed FRBs) or multi-band microarcsecond SKA-VLBI are required to push gravitational chromaticity bounds further30.  
**Challenge 6: How can ArcSecs claim "light is not the fastest thing in the universe" (Premise 8\) given the tight 10⁻¹⁵ speed agreement of GW170817 and GRB 170817A?**  
*Response:* Strictly speaking, the GW170817 and GRB 170817A constraint bounds fractional speed differences to between minus 3 × 10⁻¹⁵ and plus 7 × 10⁻¹⁵ under standard prompt-emission assumptions51. Two physical points follow: first, the upper bound explicitly permits gravitational waves to outpace gamma rays by up to 7 parts in 10¹⁵ of c (and GRB 170817A followed GW170817 by roughly 1.7 seconds49); second, in PGFCM with a tiny nonzero photon rest mass, all massive photons propagate below the fundamental asymptotic causal limit c, automatically making photons slower than any massless or non-electromagnetic causal signal.  
**Challenge 7: What is the single most vulnerable gap in the ArcSecs position today?**  
*Response:* The absence of a quantitatively calibrated physical mechanism for Premise 3 ("Light slows down over time") across cosmological baselines. Local reversible slowing near gravitating masses is fully accounted for by PGFCM (matching Shapiro delay7), but an irreversible secular slowing or energy-loss law must account for where the lost momentum goes without inducing angular blurring of distant point sources or pulse smearing in millisecond FRBs. Until that secular interaction constant is derived and tested (EXP-05), Premise 3 remains an uncalibrated open hypothesis.

## ArcSecs interpretation-versus-observation checkpoint (editorial addition, R31)

- **Observed or calibrated quantities:** Gravitational lensing, solar light deflection and radio delay are measured, regardless of the physical ontology selected to describe them.
- **What the observation does not determine by itself:** It does not identify a unique fundamental physical ontology or interaction mechanism; experimental limits must remain explicitly labeled as limits.
- **ArcSecs mechanism-building task:** Derive a non-fabric gravitational interaction on photon energy-momentum matching the achromatic deflection and timing data, then isolate tests of nonzero invariant rest mass.
- **Reproducible comparison / sources:** Solar-deflection VLBI, Cassini radio tracking, EHT imaging and FRBs.
- **Falsification rule:** Retain every published measured quantity and uncertainty. If a specified ArcSecs mechanism fails that benchmark, record the failing assumption and design a more complete physical mechanism or a discriminating observation; do not erase or recast the measurement as a successful fit.
- **Scientific release status:** Research working edition. Source bibliography, numerical calculations, author attribution and any claimed original simulations require independent scientific verification before journal submission.
