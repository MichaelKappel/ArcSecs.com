## **2\. Literature Review**

### **Semiclassical Vacuum Decay and Thin-Shell Dynamics**

The relativistic theory of cosmic bubble nucleation begins with Coleman and De Luccia (1980), who incorporated gravitational backreaction into semiclassical false-vacuum decay1. Because the vacuum energy liberated by converting false vacuum to true vacuum grows with bubble volume, the Schwarzschild radius associated with that converted energy eventually becomes comparable to the bubble radius, making gravitational backreaction indispensable on cosmological scales1. Analyzing an O(4)-symmetric Euclidean bounce instanton, Coleman and De Luccia established that decay alters the effective cosmological constant across the wall and that gravity can dramatically suppress transitions—completely stabilizing a flat Minkowski false vacuum against decay into a negative-energy anti-de Sitter (AdS) true vacuum whenever wall tension exceeds a critical gravitational threshold1. Subsequent theoretical studies by Abbott (1987)28, a thin-wall lemma proof in the ResearchGate literature3, and a variational tunneling-potential analysis in *Physical Review D*29 refined these limits: the thin-wall lemma proved rigorous inequalities bracketing the exact decay exponent and confirmed the AdS stabilization threshold3, while the tunneling-potential formulation unified AdS, Minkowski, and de Sitter bounce actions as well as the homogeneous Hawking–Moss transition18.  
Real-time Lorentzian evolution across a boundary joining distinct spacetimes is governed by the covariant thin-shell formalism introduced by Werner Israel (1966) in *Il Nuovo Cimento B*11. Requiring continuity of the induced three-metric across a singular timelike hypersurface while permitting a jump in extrinsic curvature, Israel derived the Lanczos equations relating the extrinsic-curvature discontinuity to a localized surface energy-momentum tensor and covariant conservation constraints11. Applying Israel's junction equations to an inflating false-vacuum de Sitter bubble surrounded by an asymptotically flat Schwarzschild exterior, Blau, Guendelman, and Guth (1987) demonstrated a striking bifurcation2: subcritical bubbles collapse under tension and exterior gravity, whereas sufficiently energetic false-vacuum bubbles expand indefinitely only on a global wormhole-like branch situated behind a black-hole horizon—forming a causally detached baby universe rather than expanding outward through the parent space2.  
In 2026, José M. M. Senovilla extended thin-shell and proper-matching junction rules to arbitrary metric gravitational theories built from curvature scalar invariants and their covariant derivatives up to arbitrary order16. Using distributional calculus, Senovilla proved that thin shells emerge when the m-th covariant derivative of the Riemann tensor is continuous (where m is the highest derivative order in the Lagrangian), while shell-free proper matching requires continuity up to the (m+1)-th derivative16. Crucially, Senovilla demonstrated that continuity of the normal components of the bulk energy-momentum tensor across proper junctions holds universally regardless of the gravitational field equations for minimally coupled matter, and that only General Relativity, F(R) gravity, and quadratic theories exhibit specialized boundary structures such as gravitational double layers16. Higher-curvature junction generalizations therefore tighten mathematical consistency across interfaces without introducing an outward gravitational pull from a symmetric exterior.

## **3\. Theory and Mathematical Architecture**

### **Model A: Classical Material Bubble and Relativistic Membrane Baseline**

Consider a thin spherical interface of radius R (meters) and uniform positive surface-energy density sigma (joules per square meter) separating an interior medium at uniform pressure P\_in from an exterior environment at uniform pressure P\_out (pascals, equivalent to joules per cubic meter). Define the pressure excess as interior pressure minus exterior pressure. Under quasistatic three-dimensional assumptions, the total free energy of the interface equals the positive surface energy (four pi times surface tension times squared radius) minus the mechanical bulk expansion work (four-thirds pi times pressure excess times cubed radius)4. Taking the negative radial variation of this energy reveals two competing mechanical forces: an inward-pulling surface-tension restoring force proportional to tension times radius, opposed by an outward-pushing bulk pressure force proportional to pressure excess times squared radius4.  
Setting these forces in balance defines the **classical three-dimensional Laplace critical radius**, equal to twice the surface-energy density divided by the pressure excess4. Evaluating the second radial derivative of total energy at this equilibrium yields a strictly negative curvature proportional to minus the surface tension, proving that Laplace balance is an unstable saddle point. Bubbles smaller than the critical radius collapse under surface tension; bubbles larger than the critical radius expand indefinitely under positive pressure excess4.  
To equip the wall with exact relativistic inertia and track causal speeds, the interface is promoted to a relativistic Nambu–Goto (Dirac) membrane whose Lagrangian combines minus the surface energy scaled by the inverse Lorentz contraction factor with the bulk pressure-work volume term4. A Legendre transformation yields an exactly conserved total relativistic energy equal to the Lorentz-boosted wall rest energy minus the bulk pressure work4. The resulting radial equation of motion sets coordinate radial acceleration proportional to the product of the squared inverse Lorentz factor and the difference between an outward relativistic pressure-drive term (scaling inversely with surface inertia and suppressed at high velocity by the inverse Lorentz factor) and an inward curvature-tension deceleration scaling as twice the reciprocal radius4.  
Two critical physical distinctions follow immediately from Model A. First, when the exterior possesses a **more negative pressure** than the interior—for instance, an empty zero-pressure interior surrounded by a negative-pressure exterior—subtracting a negative exterior pressure produces a positive pressure excess, driving outward expansion. Because Newton's gravitational constant is completely absent from this Lagrangian, labeling this expansion "external gravitational attraction" is erroneous: it is purely local mechanical pressure-gradient work across the wall. Second, the three-dimensional thermal Laplace radius cannot be transplanted directly into four-dimensional quantum tunneling. In four-dimensional Euclidean flat-space tunneling, balancing a three-sphere hypersurface tension against a four-ball volume energy release gives a critical bounce radius equal to **three** times tension divided by vacuum-energy difference3. Consequently, the 3D Laplace threshold is exactly two-thirds of the 4D quantum nucleation radius, ensuring that a quantum-tunneled flat-space bubble materializes 50% larger than its static force-balance threshold and immediately accelerates along a hyperbolic relativistic trajectory4.

## **8\. Explicit Answers to the Seven Commission Questions**

> 1. **Can a greater outside environment physically exist in a model without presupposing a material spacetime fabric?** **Yes.** Shape Dynamics demonstrates mathematically that gravitational dynamics can be formulated in terms of evolving three-dimensional spatial conformal geometry and dimensionless relational ratios (York time) without a four-dimensional spacetime manifold fabric12. > 2. **What exact energy or matter in the exterior produces the proposed force?** In standard Models A, B, and C, no exterior mass-energy generates an outward gravitational pull; instead, metastable **false-vacuum energy density** acts as the local energy reservoir consumed as the domain wall sweeps outward1. > 3. **Does *external gravity* really pull the bubble outward, or is the mechanism a pressure/stress difference described geometrically?** In every standard classical and relativistic model (Models A, B, and C), the mechanism is **a pressure or vacuum-energy stress difference across the boundary**, *not* external gravitational attraction. External gravity in Coleman–De Luccia suppresses AdS vacuum decay and shrinks de Sitter nucleation radii3; external Schwarzschild mass in Israel thin-shell dynamics collapses regular shells and forces expanding false-vacuum bubbles onto a causally detached wormhole branch2. Describing negative exterior vacuum pressure as an attractive gravitational pull misidentifies bulk pressure work.  
> 4. **What causes nucleation; what causes expansion; what could cause collapse?** In classical systems (Model A), thermal fluctuations nucleate bubbles that expand only above the 3D Laplace critical radius and collapse below it or under negative pressure excess4.
