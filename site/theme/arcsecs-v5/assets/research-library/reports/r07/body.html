2. Literature Review

Semiclassical Vacuum Decay and Thin-Shell Dynamics

The relativistic theory of cosmic bubble nucleation begins with Coleman and De Luccia (1980), who incorporated gravitational backreaction into semiclassical false-vacuum decay1. Because the vacuum energy liberated by converting false vacuum to true vacuum grows with bubble volume, the Schwarzschild radius associated with that converted energy eventually becomes comparable to the bubble radius, making gravitational backreaction indispensable on cosmological scales1. Analyzing an O(4)-symmetric Euclidean bounce instanton, Coleman and De Luccia established that decay alters the effective cosmological constant across the wall and that gravity can dramatically suppress transitions—completely stabilizing a flat Minkowski false vacuum against decay into a negative-energy anti-de Sitter (AdS) true vacuum whenever wall tension exceeds a critical gravitational threshold1. Subsequent theoretical studies by Abbott (1987)28, a thin-wall lemma proof in the ResearchGate literature3, and a variational tunneling-potential analysis in Physical Review D29 refined these limits: the thin-wall lemma proved rigorous inequalities bracketing the exact decay exponent and confirmed the AdS stabilization threshold3, while the tunneling-potential formulation unified AdS, Minkowski, and de Sitter bounce actions as well as the homogeneous Hawking–Moss transition18.
Real-time Lorentzian evolution across a boundary joining distinct spacetimes is governed by the covariant thin-shell formalism introduced by Werner Israel (1966) in Il Nuovo Cimento B11. Requiring continuity of the induced three-metric across a singular timelike hypersurface while permitting a jump in extrinsic curvature, Israel derived the Lanczos equations relating the extrinsic-curvature discontinuity to a localized surface energy-momentum tensor and covariant conservation constraints11. Applying Israel’s junction equations to an inflating false-vacuum de Sitter bubble surrounded by an asymptotically flat Schwarzschild exterior, Blau, Guendelman, and Guth (1987) demonstrated a striking bifurcation2: subcritical bubbles collapse under tension and exterior gravity, whereas sufficiently energetic false-vacuum bubbles expand indefinitely only on a global wormhole-like branch situated behind a black-hole horizon—forming a causally detached baby universe rather than expanding outward through the parent space2.
In 2026, José M. M. Senovilla extended thin-shell and proper-matching junction rules to arbitrary metric gravitational theories built from curvature scalar invariants and their covariant derivatives up to arbitrary order16. Using distributional calculus, Senovilla proved that thin shells emerge when the m-th covariant derivative of the Riemann tensor is continuous (where m is the highest derivative order in the Lagrangian), while shell-free proper matching requires continuity up to the (m+1)-th derivative16. Crucially, Senovilla demonstrated that continuity of the normal components of the bulk energy-momentum tensor across proper junctions holds universally regardless of the gravitational field equations for minimally coupled matter, and that only General Relativity, F(R) gravity, and quadratic theories exhibit specialized boundary structures such as gravitational double layers16. Higher-curvature junction generalizations therefore tighten mathematical consistency across interfaces without introducing an outward gravitational pull from a symmetric exterior.

Bubble Collisions, Eternal Inflation, and CMB Observations

In eternal inflation, exponential metric expansion of the parent false vacuum outpaces bubble nucleation, preventing percolation and leaving our observable cosmos inside a single open-FRW bubble5. Aguirre, Johnson, and Shomer (2007)21, Aguirre and Johnson (2008)22, and Aguirre, Johnson, and Tysanner (2009)21 derived exact thin-wall bubble-collision solutions and evaluated their aftermath. They showed that a two-bubble collision spacetime preserves SO(2,1) azimuthal symmetry, avoids naked singularities when the null energy condition holds, and—for observers within the collision’s future light cone—projects onto the CMB sky as an azimuthally symmetric circular disk bounded by a causal ring across which temperature modulations and potential edge discontinuities arise22.
Feeney, Johnson, Mortlock, and Peiris (2011) executed the first full-sky Bayesian search for these causal-disk signatures in Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP) 7-year observations5. Finding no statistically compelling evidence to augment ΛCDM with bubble collisions, they constrained the average number of detectable collisions across the full sky to fewer than 1.6 at 68% confidence5. Subsequent Planck Collaboration isotropy and statistics releases (Planck 2013 XXIII, Planck 2015 XVI, and Planck 2018 VII) audited large-angular-scale CMB anomalies alongside precision parameter estimates (Hubble constant 67.40 ± 0.50 km/s/Mpc and scalar spectral index 0.9649 ± 0.0042 in Planck 2018), finding overall consistency with standard ΛCDM and no confirmed cosmic-bubble collision detections6. Separately, searches for parity-violating isotropic cosmic birefringence—which rotates linear CMB polarization and generates E-B cross-correlations—yielded an apparent rotation angle of 0.30° ± 0.11° (68% CL) on nearly full-sky Planck Public Release 4 (PR4) data19. However, Diego-Palazuelos et al. (2022) explicitly declined to assign cosmological significance to that measurement because the inferred angle shrinks under larger Galactic masks along a degeneracy with polarized thermal dust foregrounds19.

Model A: Classical Material Bubble and Relativistic Membrane Baseline

Consider a thin spherical interface of radius R (meters) and uniform positive surface-energy density sigma (joules per square meter) separating an interior medium at uniform pressure P_in from an exterior environment at uniform pressure P_out (pascals, equivalent to joules per cubic meter). Define the pressure excess as interior pressure minus exterior pressure. Under quasistatic three-dimensional assumptions, the total free energy of the interface equals the positive surface energy (four pi times surface tension times squared radius) minus the mechanical bulk expansion work (four-thirds pi times pressure excess times cubed radius)4. Taking the negative radial variation of this energy reveals two competing mechanical forces: an inward-pulling surface-tension restoring force proportional to tension times radius, opposed by an outward-pushing bulk pressure force proportional to pressure excess times squared radius4.
Setting these forces in balance defines the classical three-dimensional Laplace critical radius, equal to twice the surface-energy density divided by the pressure excess4. Evaluating the second radial derivative of total energy at this equilibrium yields a strictly negative curvature proportional to minus the surface tension, proving that Laplace balance is an unstable saddle point. Bubbles smaller than the critical radius collapse under surface tension; bubbles larger than the critical radius expand indefinitely under positive pressure excess4.
To equip the wall with exact relativistic inertia and track causal speeds, the interface is promoted to a relativistic Nambu–Goto (Dirac) membrane whose Lagrangian combines minus the surface energy scaled by the inverse Lorentz contraction factor with the bulk pressure-work volume term4. A Legendre transformation yields an exactly conserved total relativistic energy equal to the Lorentz-boosted wall rest energy minus the bulk pressure work4. The resulting radial equation of motion sets coordinate radial acceleration proportional to the product of the squared inverse Lorentz factor and the difference between an outward relativistic pressure-drive term (scaling inversely with surface inertia and suppressed at high velocity by the inverse Lorentz factor) and an inward curvature-tension deceleration scaling as twice the reciprocal radius4.
Two critical physical distinctions follow immediately from Model A. First, when the exterior possesses a more negative pressure than the interior—for instance, an empty zero-pressure interior surrounded by a negative-pressure exterior—subtracting a negative exterior pressure produces a positive pressure excess, driving outward expansion. Because Newton’s gravitational constant is completely absent from this Lagrangian, labeling this expansion “external gravitational attraction” is erroneous: it is purely local mechanical pressure-gradient work across the wall. Second, the three-dimensional thermal Laplace radius cannot be transplanted directly into four-dimensional quantum tunneling. In four-dimensional Euclidean flat-space tunneling, balancing a three-sphere hypersurface tension against a four-ball volume energy release gives a critical bounce radius equal to three times tension divided by vacuum-energy difference3. Consequently, the 3D Laplace threshold is exactly two-thirds of the 4D quantum nucleation radius, ensuring that a quantum-tunneled flat-space bubble materializes 50% larger than its static force-balance threshold and immediately accelerates along a hyperbolic relativistic trajectory4.

In semiclassical quantum field theory coupled to General Relativity, a scalar field evolves in a potential featuring a higher-energy metastable false vacuum and a lower-energy true vacuum separated by an energy barrier1. In the thin-wall regime—valid when the wall thickness set by the barrier mass scale is much smaller than the nucleation radius—wall tension equals the field-space integral of the square root of twice the barrier potential height above the vacuum baseline3.
The decay probability per unit four-volume is exponentially suppressed by the difference between the Euclidean action of an O(4)-symmetric bounce instanton and that of the homogeneous false-vacuum background18. Coupling the scalar sector to Einstein–Hilbert gravity introduces the reduced Planck mass and modifies vacuum decay through three essential backreaction effects1. 8. Explicit Answers to the Seven Commission Questions

  1. Can a greater outside environment physically exist in a model without presupposing a material spacetime fabric? Yes. Shape Dynamics demonstrates mathematically that gravitational dynamics can be formulated in terms of evolving three-dimensional spatial conformal geometry and dimensionless relational ratios (York time) without a four-dimensional spacetime manifold fabric12.   2. What exact energy or matter in the exterior produces the proposed force? In standard Models A, B, and C, no exterior mass-energy generates an outward gravitational pull; instead, metastable false-vacuum energy density acts as the local energy reservoir consumed as the domain wall sweeps outward1. For a genuine exterior pull under Model D, the exterior must contain either a finite, bounded shell of mass-energy coupled to a massive, finite-range Yukawa scalar field4 or an asymmetric exterior mass overdensity generating a traceless tidal quadrupole.
  3. Does external gravity really pull the bubble outward, or is the mechanism a pressure/stress difference described geometrically? In every standard classical and relativistic model (Models A, B, and C), the mechanism is a pressure or vacuum-energy stress difference across the boundary, not external gravitational attraction. External gravity in Coleman–De Luccia suppresses AdS vacuum decay and shrinks de Sitter nucleation radii3; external Schwarzschild mass in Israel thin-shell dynamics collapses regular shells and forces expanding false-vacuum bubbles onto a causally detached wormhole branch2. Describing negative exterior vacuum pressure as an attractive gravitational pull misidentifies bulk pressure work.
  4. What causes nucleation; what causes expansion; what could cause collapse? In classical systems (Model A), thermal fluctuations nucleate bubbles that expand only above the 3D Laplace critical radius and collapse below it or under negative pressure excess4. In quantum cosmology (Model B), O(4)-symmetric quantum barrier tunneling nucleates a bubble at the 4D critical radius, after which local false-vacuum energy release accelerates the wall toward light speed1. Collapse occurs when interior vacuum energy is negative (anti-de Sitter interiors crunch rapidly after nucleation1), when wall tension exceeds the gravitational AdS quenching threshold3, or when a self-gravitating false-vacuum shell sits on the subcritical (+1, +1) branch inside a Schwarzschild exterior4.
  5. What quantitative observation made inside our universe would discriminate the mechanism? Three internal observables distinguish the competing models: (i) Pre-inflationary bubble collisions (H2) produce azimuthally symmetric circular CMB temperature disks with causal boundary rings exceeding the look-elsewhere threshold |SNR_max| >= 3.54; (ii) Open spatial curvature would support an uncollided Coleman–De Luccia bubble interior with minimal inflationary e-foldings21; and (iii) Model D exterior influences predict either a radial outward-velocity gradient (for Yukawa screening matched to the cavity scale, mimicking a local void outflow4) or a coherent traceless ell = 2 quadrupolar shear in CMB maps and peculiar-velocity surveys aligned along a single cosmic axis (for an asymmetric external mass).
  6. What present evidence contradicts it, and which parameters remain completely unconstrained? Present data disfavor visible boundary imprints: WMAP 7-year searches constrain detectable bubble collisions to fewer than 1.6 on the full sky at 68% confidence5, and Planck 2018 isotropy and cosmological parameter analyses (Hubble constant 67.40 ± 0.50 km/s/Mpc, scalar spectral index 0.9649 ± 0.0042) match flat, isotropic ΛCDM without confirmed collision scars6. Claims that a local KBC void explains the Hubble tension remain contested across supernova datasets44. Completely unconstrained parameters include the position and radius of any physical wall outside our particle horizon, the age of any greater parent cosmos, the exterior false-vacuum energy scale if inflation exceeded roughly 60 e-foldings, and any ultra-long-range Yukawa screening scale exceeding our observable horizon.
  7. What would a single affordable computational or observational experiment most decisively test? 14. Companion Deliverable 5: Five Referee-Style Objections and Data-Grounded Responses

  1. Objection 1 (Equivalence of Negative Pressure and Anti-Gravity): “In cosmology, vacuum energy has negative pressure, which drives cosmic acceleration. Why does the report claim that ‘more negative pressure outside’ pulling a bubble wall outward is not gravitational?” Response: In Model A, outward wall acceleration scales with the pressure excess across the interface and persists identically when Newton’s gravitational constant is zero4. When General Relativistic gravity is activated in Models B and C, positive exterior vacuum energy density enters the de Sitter lapse function and reduces the initial CDL bubble nucleation radius relative to flat space (1.600000 vs. 8.000000 at exterior Hubble parameter 0.5)4, while inflating the exterior background rather than pulling the wall into it. Mechanical pressure work and gravitational curvature act through distinct dynamical terms.
  2. Objection 2 (Local Underdensity and KBC Void Evidence): “Galaxy counts in the KBC void (0.46 ± 0.06 underdensity out to 300 Mpc) and the 5.3σ Hubble tension (73.0 vs. 67.4 km/s/Mpc) suggest we actually live inside an expanding bubble pulled outward by surrounding matter44. Doesn’t that support H1?” Response: Under Newton’s shell theorem, a spherically symmetric overdense shell surrounding a local void exerts zero outward radial force inside the cavity; any apparent outflow in a Newtonian void reflects reduced inward self-gravity, not an external pull. Moreover, supernova analyses by Kenworthy, Scolnic, and Riess (2019) and Cai et al. (2020) challenge void explanations of the Hubble tension across redshift45, and Haslbauer et al. (2020) themselves invoked Milgromian dynamics with a constant 0.055 a0 external field to reproduce the KBC velocity field44.
  3. Objection 3 (Generality Beyond Thin-Shell GR): “Israel’s 1966 junction conditions assume Einstein’s General Relativity and zero wall thickness11. Could higher-order modified gravity theories or finite-thickness scalar walls invalidate Model C’s rejection of exterior gravitational pull?” Response: Senovilla’s 2026 generalized junction theorem establishes that across any proper matching in arbitrary metric theories built from curvature invariants and their derivatives, normal components of the bulk energy-momentum tensor remain universally continuous16, and spherical symmetry still shields interior points from radial exterior forces in massless limits. Finite wall thickness smooths the thin-shell jump into a continuous scalar profile, as solved in Coleman–De Luccia bounce equations18, which likewise confirm gravitational suppression rather than outward exterior pull3.
  4. Objection 4 (Look-Elsewhere Conservatism in CMB Searches): “Setting the CMB bubble-collision detection threshold at >= 3.5 risks missing weak early-inflation collisions. Why not inspect 2.5–3.0 circular features?” Response: Our 2,000-realization isotropic Gaussian Monte Carlo calibration across 192 templates demonstrates directly that 2.552 is the median peak amplitude generated by pure random noise, and thresholds of 2.5 and 3.0 yield global false-positive rates of 54.35% and 17.95%, respectively4. Lowering the gate below 3.5 (4.45% global FPR) floods searches with false alarms, motivating the rigorous full-sky Bayesian thresholds used by Feeney et al.5.
  5. Objection 5 (Viability of Model D’s Yukawa Screening): “If Model D’s Yukawa-screened scalar field breaks the shell theorem and pulls the wall outward4, why isn’t H1 declared proved?” Response: Mathematical consistency is necessary, not sufficient, for empirical validity. Candidate D1 produces a noticeable outward pull inside a cavity only if the screening range approximates the cavity scale, the exterior mass shell is finite and substantial, and the scalar coupling is non-negligible4. Because no observational evidence confirms a finite-range cosmological gravitational screening scale or an accessible cavity boundary, and universal fifth-force couplings face stringent precision gravity limits, Candidate D1 is classified accurately as plausible-but-unverified.
