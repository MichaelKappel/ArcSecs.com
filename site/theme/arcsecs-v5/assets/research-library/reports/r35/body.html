Literature Review

Efforts to explain gravitation via physical interactions rather than variable geometry span four principal research traditions.

Corpuscular Attraction and Mach–Weber Relational Mechanics

Early physical accounts treated light as corpuscular mass-energy deflected by Newtonian central attraction. John Michell (1784) and Johann Georg von Soldner (1801) modeled light rays grazing the Sun along hyperbolic paths, with Soldner computing a solar-limb deflection of 0.84 arcseconds using contemporary solar data (equivalent to 0.8756 arcseconds under modern nominal solar constants)4. As Clifford Will (1988) documented alongside Henry Cavendish’s parallel unpublished note, purely Newtonian scalar attraction captures only half the observed optical and radio bending because it omits transverse momentum-current coupling4.
To eliminate absolute spatial frames, Dennis Sciama (1953) formulated a Machian theory of inertial induction modeled after Maxwellian electrodynamics, deriving local inertial resistance from the retarded vector-potential interaction of an accelerating body with all distant cosmic matter19. Sciama’s model linked the Newtonian gravitational constant to cosmic matter density and explained why inertial and gravitational mass coincide19. However, as a linear spin-1 vector analogy, Sciama’s model requires repulsive static behavior unless sign-adjusted and cannot reproduce the full solar light deflection or Mercury’s perihelion advance19.
André Koch Torres Assis (1989, 2014) constructed a relational mechanics implementing Mach’s principle through a Weber-type gravitational force dependent on inter-body separation, radial relative speed, and radial relative acceleration, governed by the postulate that the total vector force on any body vanishes22. While Weber–Mach mechanics generates centrifugal and Coriolis effects from relative cosmic rotation and can match Mercury’s perihelion shift for a tuned radial-acceleration parameter23, it relies on instantaneous action-at-a-distance and—as demonstrated in the computational audit below—fails solar light-bending, Shapiro delay, and binary-pulsar radiation tests4. Julian Barbour and Bruno Bertotti (1977) similarly eliminated absolute frames through configuration-space relational dynamics25, though reproducing electromagnetic and radiative phenomena requires dynamical couplings isomorphic to tensor field equations.

Flat-Background Non-Metric and Tensor Field Formulations

Alfred North Whitehead (1922) proposed an action-at-a-distance gravitational law built on uniform flat kinematics rather than variable spacetime curvature, matching classical solar-system precession, light bending, and redshift values27. However, Gary Gibbons and Clifford Will (2008) showed that Whitehead’s law violates five independent empirical tests, notably Earth-tide gravimeter anisotropy bounds and binary-pulsar timing, excluding it observationally3.
A far stronger non-geometric alternative arises from flat-background field theory. Walter Thirring (1961) modeled gravity as a massless symmetric rank-two tensor field propagating in flat coordinates and coupled universally to conserved stress-energy1. Crucially, Thirring separated the “unrenormalized picture”—in which the gravitational field physically alters particle inertial masses, electromagnetic couplings, measuring rods, and clock transition rates—from the “renormalized picture,” in which those altered physical instruments define an effective Riemannian benchmark metric1. Richard Feynman (1962–1963 lectures) and Stanley Deser (1970) proved that requiring a massless spin-2 field in flat coordinates to couple self-consistently to total energy-momentum, including its own field stress-energy, uniquely yields nonlinear field equations algebraically equivalent to standard metric benchmarks without invoking curved space as a substance2. Anatoly Logunov and Mirian Mestvirishvili (1989, 2001) extended this flat-background field paradigm in the Relativistic Theory of Gravitation (RTG) by assigning a tiny rest mass to the gravitational field to preserve strict flat-coordinate causality and prevent singular horizons13.
As a brief benchmark note, teleparallel equivalents of general relativity reformulate gravitational dynamics using torsion rather than curvature; however, because standard teleparallelism merely swaps geometric descriptors while yielding identical predictions at all tested orders, it does not represent a physical non-substrate explanation or independent evidence against geometric theories, and is excluded from the ArcSecs solution space.

Results

Weak-Field Two-Body Dynamics, Light Deflection, and Shapiro Delay

Quantitative evaluation across weak-field solar-system benchmarks reveals a decisive split between classical corpuscular attraction, instantaneous Weber–Mach forces, and retarded flat-background tensor fields4.
Under pure Newtonian–Soldner corpuscular coupling, light grazing the solar limb (where the dimensionless solar gravitational potential parameter is 2.122503 × 10⁻⁶) deflects by 0.875595 arcseconds—precisely half of the 1.75104 ± 0.00039 arcseconds inferred from geodetic VLBI measurements of PPN parameter gamma (0.99983 ± 0.00045)4. Furthermore, a Newtonian scalar potential accelerates corpuscles passing the Sun, producing a negative one-way Shapiro propagation advance of −65.61 microseconds along the representative 1.6 solar-radius Earth–Saturn trajectory, directly contradicting the +131.21-microsecond flat-background retardation benchmark4.
Testing the relational Assis–Weber velocity-dependent potential exposes an irreconcilable trade-off between planetary precession and optical bending4. Symbolic Euler–Lagrange derivation confirms that the Weber radial-acceleration coupling coefficient must equal 3.0 to shift Mercury’s radial orbital frequency enough to produce 42.9806 arcseconds per Julian century, closely matching the MESSENGER value of 42.9799 ± 0.0009 arcseconds per Julian century4. Yet exact numerical integration at a Weber coefficient of 3.0 predicts a solar-limb light deflection of 3.502383 arcseconds—double the observed 1.751 arcseconds—and yields a one-way Shapiro delay of 101.66 microseconds, underpredicting the 131.21-microsecond benchmark by 22.5%4. Conversely, lowering the Weber coefficient to 1.0 matches the 1.751193-arcsecond solar deflection but cuts Mercury’s advance to one-third of its measured rate and yields an incorrect negative Shapiro advance of −9.85 microseconds4.
By contrast, the flat-background retarded spin-2 representation (Candidate A) matches Mercury’s 42.9806 arcseconds per century, the 1.751190-arcsecond solar-limb deflection, and the +131.21-microsecond Shapiro delay benchmark using benchmark-equivalent equations and zero additional fit parameters beyond the gravitational constant and limiting field speed1.

Machian Inertia Kernel Analysis

Evaluating an explicit Machian inertia-response coefficient defined as a three-dimensional volume integral over all cosmic matter weighted by inverse separation distance demonstrates why deleting nearby matter does not make local inertia vanish4. Because spherical shell volume increases with radius squared while the weighting kernel decreases as inverse radius, distant cosmological shells dominate the integral overwhelmingly over nearby galaxies or the Solar System4.
Using benchmark cosmological values (critical density 8.533 × 10⁻²⁷ kg/m³ and Hubble radius 4448.0 Mpc), obtaining an inertia coefficient of unity under a sharp cosmic cutoff requires an outer integration radius of 1.1547 Hubble radii (5136.1 Mpc) for unit matter density fraction, or 2.0574 Hubble radii (9151.1 Mpc) for a matter fraction of 0.3154. Under smooth exponential attenuation, unity inertia requires an attenuation scale of 0.8165 Hubble radii (3631.7 Mpc)4. Deleting a centered spherical cosmic void of radius 20 Mpc, 50 Mpc, 150 Mpc, or 300 Mpc reduces the local inertia coefficient by only 1.516 × 10⁻⁵, 9.477 × 10⁻⁵, 8.529 × 10⁻⁴, and 3.412 × 10⁻³, respectively, for unit matter fraction (and 4.777 × 10⁻⁶ to 1.075 × 10⁻³ for a 0.315 matter fraction)4. Because the one-over-radius kernel obeys Poisson’s equation, moving a test mass from the center to the edge of a spherical void shifts the void deficit by only one-third of its central magnitude4. However, the kernel depends quadratically on the unknown outer cutoff scale (consistent with Premise 10), and any directional inertia anisotropy from local superclusters is tightly restricted by PPN bounds below 4 × 10⁻⁷ on parameter alpha-2 and 4 × 10⁻²⁰ on parameter alpha-34.

Companion Deliverable 4: Plain-English ArcSecs Summary (Advocacy and Evidence Brief)

For readers approaching the ArcSecs program without specialized mathematical training, the central takeaway of this study is clear: gravity can be modeled as a real, energy-carrying physical force field moving through ordinary flat coordinates during a single constant universal time, without treating space or time as a bendable fabric.
Under the eleven ArcSecs premises, space simply names where objects are, and time simply names when events occur. Neither is a material sheet that can stretch, warp, or slow down. When an atomic clock at the base of the Tokyo Skytree ticks measurably slower than an identical clock 450 meters higher up, time itself has not slowed; instead, the gravitational field physically interacts with the atoms in the lower clock, slightly altering their internal binding energy so their electrons oscillate at a lower physical frequency.
Our mathematical audit tested three non-fabric gravity models against six benchmark experiments: Mercury’s orbit, solar starlight bending, Cassini spacecraft radio delays near the Sun, atomic clocks, orbiting double pulsars, and the nearly simultaneous arrival of gravitational waves and gamma rays from colliding neutron stars. Two older ideas failed decisively: Newton’s simple particle-attraction formula bends starlight by only half the measured angle, and instantaneous “action-at-a-distance” forces (Weber–Mach gravity) either bend light twice as much as observed or fail binary-pulsar and gravitational-wave timing tests because real gravitational changes propagate at the speed of light rather than instantaneously.
By contrast, a finite-speed physical tensor field (pioneered by Walter Thirring and Richard Feynman as a flat-coordinate field theory) passes all six precision tests cleanly. Masses emit physical field energy that steers planets, bends light by the full observed angle, and radiates energy away when neutron stars spiral together. Furthermore, our calculation of a cosmic “Machian” inertia integral shows why hollowing out a giant local void in space would scarcely alter local inertia, because almost all inertial resistance would originate from the most distant cosmic shells.
At the same time, scientific honesty requires marking what ArcSecs has not yet solved. While photons clearly carry energy and momentum that respond to gravity, measurements so far set only tiny upper limits on any intrinsic photon “rest mass” rather than detecting one. Similarly, the ArcSecs ideas that light slows down over cosmic distances and that galaxy rotation curves and colliding galaxy clusters work without unseen dark matter remain incomplete mechanisms awaiting direct testing on the 175-galaxy SPARC catalog and future radio-telescope arrays.

  • Reproducible comparison / sources: Cassini tracking, Mercury MESSENGER, Double Pulsar and GW170817.
  • Falsification rule: Retain every published measured quantity and uncertainty.
