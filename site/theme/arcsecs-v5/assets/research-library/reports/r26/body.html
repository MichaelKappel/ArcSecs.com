Ramping from an initial 300-recoil loading depth down to a shallow operational depth of 12 lattice photon recoil energies—and probing wavefunction delocalization across up to five neighboring lattice sites at 6 recoil energies—the team suppressed collisional and AC Stark shifts, attained 37-second optical coherence and a single-clock stability of 3.1 parts in 10¹⁸ at one second, and performed microscopic in-situ imaging across a one-millimeter vertical cloud10. Between uncorrelated regions of the cloud, they achieved a synchronous fractional frequency comparison uncertainty of 7.6 parts in 10²¹ and measured a linear vertical frequency gradient of minus 9.8 plus or minus 2.3 parts in 10²⁰ per millimeter10. Against the local Boulder Earth-surface gravitational potential gradient benchmark of minus 1.090 parts in 10¹⁹ per millimeter, this measurement agrees within 0.48 standard deviations5.
At the macroscopic terrestrial scale, Takamoto et al. (2020) deployed a pair of transportable strontium-87 optical lattice clocks separated vertically by approximately 450 meters between the ground floor and upper observatory deck of Tokyo Skytree, connected via phase-stabilized optical fiber11. Against a 450-meter gravitational potential benchmark of approximately 4.91 parts in 10⁻¹⁴, their measurement constrained fractional deviations from standard gravitational potential scaling to an uncertainty of 9.1 parts in 10⁻⁵5.
At the orbital satellite scale, two Galileo navigation satellites (Galileo 5 and 6) accidentally injected into eccentric orbits carried passive hydrogen masers across periodic altitude variations of thousands of kilometers. Independent teams led by Delva et al. (2018) and Herrmann et al. (2018) separated orbital velocity from gravitational potential using Satellite Laser Ranging, with Delva et al. constraining fractional deviations from the gravitational potential benchmark to (0.19 plus or minus 2.48) parts in 10⁵37. Next-generation space clock experiments, including the Atomic Clock Ensemble in Space (ACES) and proposed ytterbium optical satellite concepts such as FOCOS, target further order-of-magnitude improvements55.
At the multi-species solar-potential scale, Earth’s orbital eccentricity of 0.0167 modulates the solar gravitational potential smoothly every year by 3.30 parts in 10¹⁰ peak-to-peak (an amplitude of 1.65 parts in 10⁻¹⁰)4. Extending an earlier seven-year comparison (Ashby et al., 200734), Ashby, Parker, and Patla (2018) analyzed over 14 years of fractional frequency comparisons between four subterranean NIST hydrogen masers and primary cesium fountains operated across the United States, France, Germany, Italy, and the United Kingdom3. Their analysis constrained any composition-dependent differential gravitational coupling between hydrogen and cesium hyperfine transitions to (2.24 plus or minus 2.48) parts in 10⁷ (reported as 2.2 ± 2.5 × 10⁻⁷ in the published abstract and 2.2 ± 2.8 × 10⁻⁷ in an associated NIST summary)3. Complementing microwave records, the Boulder Atomic Clock Optical Network (BACON Collaboration, 2021) demonstrated 18-digit frequency ratios linking aluminum-27 ions, ytterbium-171, and strontium-87 across 3.6-kilometer stabilized fibers and free-space optical two-way links, laying the technical foundation for multi-species optical potential tests23.

6.2 How Experiments Separate Potential, Acceleration, Fields, and Temperature

A frequent misconception in alternative physical models is attributing gravitational clock shifts to local gravitational force (acceleration) or uncompensated thermal and electromagnetic artifacts. Primary metrology data cleanly separate these variables. Across the one-millimeter vertical span of Bothwell et al. (2022), local Earth gravitational acceleration varies by roughly 3 parts in 10¹⁰, yet the atomic transition frequency shifts linearly with elevation10. Even more directly, within the shallow vertical lattice of Bothwell et al. (2022), adjacent optical lattice sites experience distinct gravitational potential energies under uniform local acceleration, creating a spectroscopically resolved Wannier-Stark ladder with wavefunction delocalization across up to five sites10. Likewise, in the 14-year Ashby et al. (2018) comparison, Earth falls freely around the Sun (experiencing a shared solar gravitational acceleration of roughly 0.006 meters per second squared across all terrestrial clocks), while the absolute solar gravitational potential varies by 3.30 parts in 10¹⁰ between perihelion and aphelion4. Operationally, gravitational clock shifts track gravitational potential energy per unit mass, not local gravitational force gradients.
Electromagnetic and thermal systematics are similarly isolated through active hardware controls. In Bothwell et al. (2022), grounded copper shields enclosed the in-vacuum cavity mirrors and piezo actuator to prevent stray surface-charge DC Stark shifts, while operating at a calibrated magic trap depth balanced on-site p-wave against neighboring-site s-wave collisional shifts10; subsequent shallow-lattice studies verified lattice light shifts at the low 10⁻¹⁹ level27. For blackbody radiation—typically the largest room-temperature systematic in optical clocks—hybrid radiance-domain infrared thermography paired with finite-element modeling characterizes trapped-ion effective temperatures to 0.155 kelvin (reaching the 2.40 × 10⁻¹⁹ atomic-response floor in ytterbium-171), while engineered thermal-radiation shields in optical lattice clocks have suppressed BBR Stark-shift uncertainties to 1.7 parts in 10²⁰28. Observed gravitational frequency shifts therefore represent robust physical gravitational effects, not hidden thermal or Stark laboratory artifacts.

6.3 Two Competing ArcSecs Physical Coupling Models Under Constant T

Rejecting physical spacetime leaves gravity as a real physical interaction between mass-bearing bodies (ArcSecs Premise 6). Under constant universal progression T, two distinct physical coupling models specify energy accounting, momentum transfer, and observable consequences:

Model 1: Universal Potential-Coupled Binding Rescaling (UPBR)

Under UPBR (an open physical-process hypothesis), gravitational interaction with surrounding mass distributions lowers the total effective mass-energy of any bound physical system situated deeper in a gravitational potential well. Because atomic electron transitions, microwave hyperfine spin flips, and nuclear isomeric transitions all oscillate at frequencies proportional to their internal mass-energy level differences divided by Planck’s constant, lowering a bound system’s internal binding and inertial energy uniformly in proportion to the local gravitational potential reduces its physical transition frequency by the exact fractional potential change—while universal progression T remains strictly constant everywhere.
Energy conservation and signal accounting operate without double-counting. When an excited atom is lowered vertically in Earth’s gravitational field, mechanical work is extracted by the gravitational interaction in proportion to the atom’s total mass-energy, including the minute mass-energy excess of its excited state relative to its ground state. Consequently, the energy gap remaining for optical photon emission at the lower elevation is smaller by the exact fractional gravitational potential difference. In a continuous optical fiber link connecting a lower clock to an upper receiver under constant T, phase continuity along the stationary path guarantees that the number of electromagnetic wave crests emitted per unit constant T equals the number of wave crests arriving at the top per unit constant T. Thus, the upper detector measures a lower incoming frequency strictly because the lower oscillator physically oscillated more slowly due to gravitational binding-energy loading. (The alternative description in which an ascending massive photon loses energy doing gravitational work describes the same energy balance when expressed in locally rescaled frequency units; the two views are complementary energy-bookkeeping frames, never additive effects.)
UPBR matches Bothwell et al. (2022), Takamoto et al. (2020), Delva et al. (2018), Herrmann et al. (2018), and Ashby et al. (2018) with zero free parameters beyond the universal gravitational potential scale. However, precisely because UPBR rescales every physical force sector identically, it is empirically degenerate with standard operational formulas in static gravitational fields. It establishes ontological consistency for constant T, not an experimentally distinguishable departure.

Model 2: Sector-Differential Field-Binding Coupling (SDFBC)

Under SDFBC (an open testable hypothesis), gravitational interaction need not couple with identical dimensionless efficiency to every internal physical energy reservoir. Specifically, optical valence transitions in strontium-87 or aluminum-27 are governed almost purely by electromagnetic Coulomb binding, microwave transitions in cesium-133 and hydrogen depend on magnetic hyperfine coupling and nuclear magnetons3, and the low-energy 8.4-electron-volt thorium-229 nuclear isomer transition arises from an extraordinary near-cancellation between mega-electron-volt strong nuclear binding and nuclear Coulomb repulsion1. If gravitational potential perturbs electromagnetic binding slightly differently from strong nuclear binding—while still conserving total energy across mechanical work, internal states, and gravitational interaction—then nuclear and electronic clocks placed at the same elevation will drift slightly apart in dimensionless frequency ratio whenever the gravitational potential changes. Because the thorium-229 transition reflects a tiny residual difference between huge competing nuclear energies, any fractional electromagnetic-versus-strong gravitational mismatch is amplified by the experimentally calibrated enhancement factor of 5,900 plus or minus 2,3001, compared with roughly 0.06 for strontium-875.
Existing gravitational redshift datasets cannot yet distinguish Model 1 from Model 2. Bothwell et al. (2022) and Takamoto et al. (2020) compared identical atomic species (strontium-87 against strontium-87), rendering them completely blind to composition-dependent couplings10. Multi-species solar-potential tests such as Ashby et al. (2018) compared hydrogen and cesium hyperfine standards whose differential electromagnetic sensitivities are small, bounding differential microwave coupling only near parts in 10⁷3. No terrestrial or solar-orbit comparison has yet paired a thorium-229 nuclear clock against an optical lattice standard. Model 1 and Model 2 therefore remain cleanly separable by prospective experiment, directly motivating the Work Package F design in Section 9.

7. Motion, Kinetic Process Slowdown, and Unstable Particle Decay (Work Package D)

7.1 Why Moving Muons, Fast Ions, Airborne Clocks, and Satellites Are Mandatory Constraints

A common pitfall in non-relativistic physical clock models is explaining elevation shifts through gravity acting on atomic electron shells while setting aside high-speed particle and satellite experiments. That omission is scientifically inadmissible because four independent experimental pillars demonstrate that translational velocity alters physical process rates even when gravitational potential is constant or independently subtracted—and does so identically in non-atomic elementary particles:
First, in the landmark CERN Muon Storage Ring experiments (Bailey et al., 1977, 1979), positive and negative muons circulated inside a 14-meter-diameter magnetic ring (7-meter orbit radius) at a momentum of approximately 3.1 GeV/c, corresponding to a velocity of 0.999419 times the speed of light and a kinetic speed factor of 29.335. Counting decay positrons and electrons against stationary laboratory digital oscillators, Bailey et al. (1977, 1979) measured exponential mean lifetimes of 64.419 plus or minus 0.058 microseconds for positive muons and 64.368 plus or minus 0.029 microseconds for negative muons6. Compared against a modern stationary muon reference lifetime of 2.1969803 microseconds, the exact 29.33-fold kinetic benchmark yields 64.4374 microseconds, agreeing with positive muons within −0.32 standard deviations and with negative muons within −2.39 standard deviations (Bailey et al. 1977 originally inferred a negative muon stationary lifetime of 2.1948 ± 0.0010 microseconds, confirming particle–antiparticle decay symmetry at the 0.1% level)5. Crucially, the circulating muons experienced a laboratory centripetal acceleration of roughly 1.28 × 10¹⁶ meters per second squared (1.31 × 10¹⁵ Earth gravities), corresponding to a proper transverse acceleration of roughly 1.12 × 10¹⁸ Earth gravities5, yet displayed zero measurable acceleration-induced lifetime shift.
Second, fast-ion laser spectroscopy confirms kinetic frequency shifts directly in atomic transitions without gravitational gradients. Ives–Stilwell-type counter-propagating laser experiments on stored relativistic ions (Botermann et al., 2014; full apparatus parameters not verified from retrieved excerpt) isolate the transverse second-order velocity shift in electronic transitions independently of first-order Doppler terms26.
Third, terrestrial airborne clocks and operational navigation satellites demonstrate that velocity slowdown combines additively with gravitational potential and references Earth’s non-rotating inertial center. In the commercial-flight experiments of Hafele and Keating (1972), flying atomic clocks eastward and westward around the globe yielded asymmetric accumulated tick differences relative to ground references—reported in a secondary review summary as minus 59 plus or minus 10 nanoseconds eastward and plus 273 plus or minus 7 nanoseconds westward17. Because eastward flight adds to Earth’s rotational surface velocity while westward flight subtracts from it, the opposite signs confirm operationally that kinetic clock slowdown is referenced to Earth’s non-rotating inertial center rather than the ground or surrounding atmosphere. Similarly, Global Positioning System satellites orbiting at a semi-major axis of roughly 26,562 kilometers with circular speeds near 3,873.8 meters per second require both a gravitational potential increase of +5.30 parts in 10¹⁰ (+45.79 microseconds per day) and a kinetic rate reduction of −8.35 parts in 10¹¹ (−7.21 microseconds per day), producing a mandatory net factory frequency offset of +4.46 parts in 10¹⁰ (+38.58 microseconds per day)—without which positioning would drift by roughly 11 to 12 kilometers daily5.
Fourth, terrestrial directional isotropy experiments—including optical clock comparisons testing orientation-dependent Lorentz symmetry (Sanner et al., 2019), terrestrial cavity-resonator tests (Nagel et al., 2015; Turneaure et al., 1978), and multi-clock daily variation searches (exact numerical bounds for Sanner et al. 2019 and Nagel et al. 2015 not verified verbatim from retrieved full text; compiled in the Standard-Model Extension data tables, arXiv:0801.0287v17)—tightly constrain any sidereal or directional frequency modulation as Earth’s velocity vector rotates relative to distant cosmic matter3.

7.2 Candid Physical Evaluation and the Missing Kinetic Weak-Decay Mechanism

Confronting the CERN stored-muon dataset under constant universal progression T exposes the sharpest unresolved physical challenge of Mission 12. Unlike a strontium atom or a thorium-229 crystal, a muon is an elementary lepton possessing no electron shells, no nuclear binding, and no solid-state lattice. Its decay into an electron, a neutrino, and an antineutrino proceeds solely via the weak interaction. Yet accelerating a muon to 0.999419 times the speed of light suppresses its weak-decay probability per unit constant T by a factor of 29.33—the exact algebraic kinetic factor governing electromagnetic transitions in fast ions, airborne cesium clocks, and orbital GPS rubidium oscillators—even under ~10¹⁸ Earth gravities of transverse magnetic steering5.
Evaluating three candidate physical-process explanations under constant T narrows the theoretical landscape sharply:

  • Candidate D1 — Confining Magnetic-Field Stabilization (Ruled Out): Attributing the 29.33-fold lifetime extension to the storage ring’s magnetic field is incompatible with both the observed independence across ~10¹⁸ Earth gravities of transverse magnetic acceleration and the well-known survival of high-speed cosmic-ray muons traversing Earth’s near-zero-field atmosphere6.
  • Candidate D2 — Dissipative Medium Drag Against Ambient Matter (Strongly Constrained): Hypothesizing that a moving muon collides with or drags against an ambient physical background of massive particles (invoking ArcSecs Premise 6) creates two immediate conflicts: dissipative momentum transfer would rapidly degrade the 3.1 GeV/c beam energy in the storage ring, and Earth’s motion through an ambient cosmic rest medium would induce sidereal directional anisotropies tightly constrained by optical clock and cavity isotropy experiments24.
  • Candidate D3 — Non-Dissipative Kinetic Inertia Loading of Internal Phase Space (Open / Incomplete Candidate): Under a strictly physical energy-accounting view without spacetime, accelerating a particle injects real kinetic energy and momentum into its dynamical state, increasing its inertial mass-energy by the exact velocity factor. If internal transition amplitudes across electromagnetic, nuclear, and weak sectors scale inversely with this dynamical inertia factor—while the local inertial reference frame is anchored dynamically by the surrounding gravitational mass distribution (accounting for the Earth-centered rotational asymmetry observed by Hafele and Keating17)—then both weak decay and atomic clocks slow down under constant T without medium friction or physical spacetime curvature.

Explicit Missing-Mechanism Verdict: While Candidate D3 outlines a physically coherent energy-loading direction, no complete, quantitative, first-principles ArcSecs dynamical theory currently derives the exact universal velocity factor across weak, electromagnetic, and nuclear interactions while ensuring zero beam drag, zero sensitivity to 10¹⁸ g transverse acceleration, and directional isotropy across Earth’s rotation. Following the ArcSecs charter strictly, this report refuses to disguise this gap with rhetoric or silently restore relativistic spacetime; instead, it designates non-dissipative kinetic weak-decay suppression as the smallest missing physical ingredient of the constant-T framework and proposes an archival sidereal muon-decay test (Prediction P3) using high-statistics modern storage-ring datasets such as Fermilab Muon g−26.

8. Cosmic Process Clocks and Photon Propagation Under Constant T (Work Package E)

8.1 The Observational Record of Astrophysical Arrival-Time Stretching

Astrophysical transients extend physical-process timing across gigaparsec baselines. Three independent observational records establish the empirical stretching pattern that any constant-T framework must explain:
First, the Dark Energy Survey Supernova Program (White, Davis, Lewis, Brout, et al., 2024; MNRAS 533, 3365–3378; DOI 10.1093/mnras/stae2008; arXiv:2406.05050; FERMILAB-PUB-24-0293-PPD) analyzed the broadband light curves of 1,504 Type Ia supernovae spanning redshifts out to approximately 1, reporting systematic stretching of observed photometric light-curve durations with increasing redshift12. (Provenance note: Because the direct arXiv full-text page was inaccessible during source retrieval49, the sample size of 1,504 supernovae, redshift span out to ~1, and bibliographic details are independently verified across multiple secondary records, whereas the exact fitted numerical value and uncertainty of the power-law stretch exponent are explicitly marked as not verified from full text and omitted rather than guessed.)
Second, independent of broadband light-curve template fitting, Blondin et al. (2008) tracked multi-epoch spectroscopic aging rates of high-redshift Type Ia supernovae (ApJ 682, 724–736), finding slowed spectral feature evolution matching the redshift factor13. Third, Lewis and Brewer (2023) (Nature Astronomy 7, 1265–1269; arXiv:2306.04053) applied Bayesian damped-random-walk modeling to multi-band high-redshift quasar light curves and measured a positive variability stretch exponent of 1.28 (+0.28, −0.29) as a function of one plus redshift, overturning earlier null claims by Hawkins that had not fully separated rest-frame wavelength-dependent accretion-disk timescales13. Complementing arrival-time measurements, Lubin and Sandage (2001) established steep apparent galaxy dimming with redshift via the Tolman surface-brightness test, constraining static-distance photon-flux models68.

8.2 Kinematic Discrimination Among Three ArcSecs Propagation Mechanisms

Under ArcSecs Premises 1, 2, 3, 7, and 9—photons have mass, time is constant, light slows down over time, and neither time nor space can physically stretch—three distinct physical mechanisms can be tested quantitatively against observed supernova arrival intervals5:

Mechanism E1: Monotonic Universal Light Slowdown Over Constant Progression T

Consider a distant supernova located at a fixed physical distance from Earth (no stretching of physical space) under ArcSecs Premise 3, where the universal propagation speed of light decreases monotonically as a function of constant universal progression T. Suppose the supernova emits two fiducial light signals—such as peak luminosity and fifteen days post-peak—separated by a true source emission interval. The first pulse departs when light speed is slightly higher, while the second pulse departs slightly later when light speed is slightly lower, yet both traverse the identical fixed distance to Earth. Because the total distance covered by each pulse equals the integral of light speed over its transit duration, differentiating the fixed-distance transit condition yields an exact kinematic identity: the observed arrival-time interval at Earth is stretched relative to the emission interval by the exact ratio of light speed at emission to light speed at observation5.
This constitutes a genuinely constructive analytical result for ArcSecs: universal monotonic slowdown of light over constant universal progression T naturally generates an achromatic arrival-time stretching of supernova light curves, spectral aging, and quasar variability without physically stretching time or space.
Crucially, however, the exact same kinematic derivation uncovers an acute spectroscopic falsification diagnostic for bare Mechanism E15. When light speed varies solely with universal progression T across a fixed distance, the arrival rate of electromagnetic wave crests (observed frequency) drops by the ratio of observation light speed to emission light speed, but the spatial wavelength (propagation speed divided by frequency) remains strictly unchanged unless local atomic emission scales or diffraction-grating dimensions also co-evolve with light speed5. Because astronomical spectrographs disperse light primarily via spatial diffraction gratings sensitive to wavelength rather than direct optical frequency counting, bare Mechanism E1 (decreasing light speed with static atomic scales) cannot simultaneously explain diffraction-grating cosmological redshifts without an additional physical scaling law—which in turn must satisfy terrestrial optical and nuclear clock-ratio stability limits4.

Mechanism E2: Static Path-Distance Drag (“Tired Light” Deceleration Along Path)

Contrast Mechanism E1 with a static path-dependent slowdown in which photons lose velocity solely as a function of physical distance traveled from the supernova (for example, through continuous scattering or gravitational drag along an intergalactic path). If propagation speed depends strictly on spatial position along the path and not on universal progression T, then every successive pulse from the supernova traverses the identical speed profile and experiences the exact same total transit time. Consequently, the observed arrival-time interval equals the emission interval identically, yielding zero light-curve stretching5. Static path-dependent drag is therefore kinematically ruled out by the DES 1,504-supernova dataset, Blondin et al. (2008) spectroscopic aging, and Lewis and Brewer (2023) quasar variability12.

Mechanism E3: Nonzero Photon Invariant Rest-Mass Vacuum Dispersion (Premise 1 Audit)

Honoring the charter’s strict photon-mass terminology distinction requires separating two physical properties:

  • Uncontested Observed Fact (DOF): Photons carry energy and momentum, exert radiation pressure, and respond to gravitational fields, fulfilling mass-energy equivalence in physical interactions (the broad foundation of ArcSecs Premise 1).
  • Distinct Experimental Claim (OUH): A nonzero invariant photon rest mass modifies the vacuum electromagnetic dispersion relation so that lower-frequency photons travel slower than higher-frequency photons emitted simultaneously from the same source14.

Extragalactic radio pulsars and localized Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs) place extreme empirical upper limits on invariant photon rest mass by separating plasma dispersion from vacuum frequency-dependent arrival delays. Wei and Wu (2018) derived tight constraints from 22 radio pulsars in the Large Magellanic Cloud alongside earlier FRB samples15; Ran, Wang, and Wei (2024) applied artificial neural networks to 32 localized FRBs (including nine Deep Synoptic Array bursts out to redshift 1.016) to obtain cosmology-independent upper bounds14; and a Physical Review D analysis of 104 localized FRBs (drawn from a 115-FRB catalog) combined with supernova, cosmic microwave background, and baryon acoustic oscillation datasets confirmed similarly stringent upper limits across three flat background models while noting residual sensitivity to high-redshift modeling assumptions51 (the 2024 Particle Data Group review maintains reference particle-physics limits70, and future Square Kilometre Array FRB catalogs are projected to push bounds several orders of magnitude tighter71). (Provenance note: Specific negative exponents on mass limits in retrieved text excerpts of Wei & Wu 2018 and the PRD 104-FRB paper were truncated by PDF/HTML math rendering15, so an illustrative order-of-magnitude FRB benchmark of 10⁻⁵¹ kilograms—roughly 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ electron-volts divided by c squared—is used for sensitivity scaling5.)
Evaluating optical dispersion quantitatively demonstrates that invariant photon rest mass cannot explain supernova arrival-time stretching. For an illustrative invariant photon rest mass of 10⁻⁵¹ kilograms over one gigaparsec, the vacuum arrival-time difference between optical g-band photons (475 nanometers) and near-infrared z-band photons (900 nanometers) is only 6.16 parts in 10¹⁵ seconds5. Even if the invariant rest mass were three orders of magnitude larger (10⁻⁴⁸ kilograms, increasing the quadratic delay by one millionfold), the optical delay would remain roughly 6 parts in 10⁹ seconds—more than fourteen orders of magnitude smaller than multi-day Type Ia supernova light-curve stretches, and strongly chromatic rather than achromatic.

8.3 Separating Source-Property Selection and Light-Curve Methodology

Evaluating astrophysical transient timing independently of cosmological assumptions requires isolating survey selection and source evolution. Because intrinsically more luminous Type Ia supernovae display broader intrinsic light curves, flux-limited surveys preferentially detect brighter, slower supernovae at high redshift (Malmquist bias). Conversely, bandpass K-corrections shift fixed observer filters toward bluer rest-frame light at higher redshift, where Type Ia light curves intrinsically rise and decline faster—meaning uncorrected bandpass shifts would reduce rather than mimic stretching. While residual host-galaxy mass correlations and progenitor metallicity evolution warrant continued archival auditing (addressed directly in Prediction P2), the convergence of broadband DES photometry12, non-photometric spectroscopic aging13, and quasar accretion-disk variability44 indicates that pure supernova template bias cannot account for the entirety of observed cosmic arrival stretching.

Challenge 1: Empirical Indistinguishability of Universal Rescaling

Referee Question: If Model 1 (Universal Potential-Coupled Binding Rescaling) alters every electronic, hyperfine, nuclear, and weak-interaction rate identically with gravitational potential, how is constant universal progression T more than a semantic relabeling of standard operational proper time?

Challenge 2: Avoiding Double-Counting in Gravitational Frequency Accounting

Referee Question: Section 6.3 discusses both gravitational binding-energy reduction of the lower clock’s transition frequency and gravitational work done by ascending massive photons. If both occurred additively on the same measurement, the frequency offset would be twice the value observed by Bothwell et al. (2022) and Takamoto et al. (2020). Resolve the energy bookkeeping.
Response: Under constant universal progression T using a single global energy scale, there is zero double-counting. Along a stationary vertical optical fiber linking a lower strontium clock to an upper strontium clock, wave-crest continuity guarantees that the number of optical cycles leaving the lower clock per unit constant T equals the number arriving at the upper receiver per unit constant T. Therefore, the upper detector measures a lower arriving frequency purely because the lower atom’s internal transition energy is reduced by gravitational potential binding-energy loading. The ascending-photon work picture arises only when energies are expressed in locally rescaled frequency units rather than global constant-T units; both account for the exact same single gravitational energy difference without adding together10.

ArcSecs interpretation-versus-observation checkpoint (editorial addition, R26)

  • Observed or calibrated quantities: Clock comparisons record different physical oscillator cycle counts; particle beams record decay/survival distributions.
  • What the observation does not determine by itself: It does not identify a unique fundamental physical ontology or interaction mechanism; experimental limits must remain explicitly labeled as limits.
  • ArcSecs mechanism-building task: Derive gravity- and motion-dependent changes in underlying physical processes using one constant progression parameter, then test differences across electronic, nuclear, and weak processes.
  • Reproducible comparison / sources: NIST clock-comparison results, CERN muon lifetime measurements, thorium-229 nuclear spectroscopy.
  • Falsification rule: Retain every published measured quantity and uncertainty. If a specified ArcSecs mechanism fails that benchmark, record the failing assumption and design a more complete physical mechanism or a discriminating observation; do not erase or recast the measurement as a successful fit.
  • Scientific release status: Research working edition. Source bibliography, numerical calculations, author attribution and any claimed original simulations require independent scientific verification before journal submission.
