4. Literature Review: Classical Bubbles, Vacuum Transitions, Thin Shells, and Cosmic Isotropy Constraints

Primary theoretical literature on cosmic bubbles spans semiclassical field tunneling, relativistic thin-shell matching, numerical collision phenomenology, and observational isotropy tests. Coleman (1977) established the flat-space semiclassical bounce formalism for false-vacuum decay, showing that a metastable scalar field tunnels to a lower-energy configuration via an O(4)-symmetric Euclidean instanton whose critical bubble balances positive surface tension against volume energy release2. Coleman and De Luccia (1980) incorporated gravitational backreaction, calculating that vacuum decay alters the effective cosmological constant across the wall, suppresses tunneling rates relative to flat space, and generates an apparently infinite open interior geometry from an inside observer’s perspective2. Gott subsequently formalized how open universes arise within de Sitter vacuum transitions19, while his later essay (Gott 2017) popularized the “sea of inflating bubbles” imagery—a pedagogical picture that serves as commentary rather than primary empirical evidence16.
Subsequent foundational analyses revealed sharp limitations in standard vacuum-decay dynamics. A critique in arXiv:hep-th/0211160 challenged Coleman–De Luccia decay into flat or anti-de Sitter minima, arguing that generic tunneling transitions with negative true-vacuum energy evolve toward crunching cosmological spacetimes rather than stable static vacua26. A Hamiltonian Wheeler–DeWitt investigation in Physical Review D (10.1103/PhysRevD.104.026013) critiqued Coleman’s reliance on imaginary eigenvalues and Euclidean analytic continuation, showing that a Lorentzian Hamiltonian treatment of false-vacuum transitions can yield closed rather than open universes20. Farhi, Guth, and Guven (1990) and Burda, Gregory, and Moss (2015) further examined how black holes or singular initial conditions complicate or catalyze vacuum nucleation28.
To analyze bubble wall motion independently of Euclidean instantons, Israel (1966) formulated the covariant thin-shell junction formalism, equating the jump in extrinsic curvature across a singular timelike hypersurface to the localized surface energy-momentum tensor and deriving the exact equation of motion for a spherical dust shell separating Minkowski and Schwarzschild domains10. Berezin, Kuzmin, and Tkachev (1987) classified general relativistic thin-wall trajectories across phase transitions and false-vacuum remnants31, while Blau, Guendelman, and Guth (1987) solved the dynamics of a de Sitter false-vacuum bubble embedded in a Schwarzschild true-vacuum exterior1. Blau, Guendelman, and Guth uncovered a decisive geometric paradox: an observer on the false-vacuum side sees the bubble inflate, yet above the critical mass threshold the exterior orientation sign reverses, so the exterior Schwarzschild observer sees a black-hole wormhole throat rather than a wall plowing outward into the surrounding universe1. Ipser and Sikivie calculated that planar and spherical domain walls exhibit repulsive gravitational fields in general relativity28, whereas a nonminimally coupled scalar-field study (arXiv:0710.4599) explored effective negative wall tension18. Extending thin-shell matching to arbitrary gravitational actions depending on curvature invariants and their covariant derivatives, Senovilla (2026) demonstrated via distributional calculus that thin shells arise from jumps in the (m+1)-th normal derivative of the Riemann tensor, that purely quadratic theories uniquely permit gravitational double layers, and that shell-free proper matchings universally require continuity of normal bulk energy-momentum components across every diffeomorphism-invariant theory9.
If neighboring cosmic bubbles collided with our domain, Aguirre, Johnson, and Shomer36 and Aguirre and Johnson (2008, 2009) derived thin-wall collision geometries and survival conditions, showing that causal boundaries map onto circular azimuthally symmetric temperature and polarization features on an interior observer’s sky37. Wainwright, Johnson, Aguirre, and Peiris (2014) carried out full 1+1 relativistic scalar-field simulations directly from Lagrangians, finding power-law collision amplitudes bounded by present spatial curvature15. Observational searches by Feeney, Johnson, Mortlock, and Peiris (2011), McEwen et al. (2012), Osborne, Senatore, and Smith (2013), and subsequent Planck-era forecasts tested WMAP and Planck maps with optimal azimuthal filters, finding no significant bubble-collision detections and bounding expected full-sky collisions to fewer than four at 95 percent confidence4.
Full-sky isotropy and dipole observations provide stringent tests of any large-scale exterior influence. Akrami et al. (Planck Collaboration 2020) documented high statistical isotropy overall alongside persistent low-multipole anomalies including a hemispherical power asymmetry44, confirmed in four foreground-cleaned Planck maps in a 2024 Astronomy & Astrophysics directional study (683, A176)47 and analyzed in a 2025 preprint (arXiv:2509.24712) reporting dipole-modulation amplitude near 0.07 at multipoles below 60 at roughly the 3-sigma level48. Crucially, Saadeh, Feeney, Pontzen, Peiris, and McEwen (2016) used Planck data to constrain global anisotropic Bianchi expansion shear relative to the Hubble rate to below 10⁻¹⁰3. Meanwhile, Secrest et al. (2021) analyzed a flux-limited all-sky sample of CatWISE quasars (arXiv:2009.14826) and reported a number-count dipole exceeding the standard kinematic CMB expectation6, prompting ongoing spectral-index re-evaluations in a February 2026 preprint (arXiv:2602.05700v2)50. Finally, empirical tests of photon invariant rest mass yield severe upper limits: the Particle Data Group (Navas et al. 2024) lists an official upper bound of 2 × 10⁻⁵⁴ kg (on the scale of 10⁻¹⁸ eV) rooted in Ryutov’s (2007) solar-wind magnetohydrodynamic analysis to Pluto’s orbit5, fast radio burst dispersion analyses set 95 percent confidence limits down to 3.9 × 10⁻⁵¹ kg5, and a 2026 Compton-scattering preprint by Nyambuya sets an upper limit below 2.60 × 10⁻³³ kg at 95 percent confidence while citing an earlier unverified gamma-ray burst lag estimate near 10⁻³⁷ kg that stands in multi-order-of-magnitude tension with solar-wind and fast radio burst limits52.

Model A: Classical Material Bubble Baseline

Consider a spherical physical interface of instantaneous radius separating an interior medium at uniform pressure from an exterior environment at another pressure, carrying positive surface-energy density (tension) in joules per square meter and surface inertia per unit area. Under quasistatic assumptions, total potential energy equals the surface-area tension energy cost minus the volume pressure-work gain given by the interior-minus-exterior pressure difference multiplied by enclosed sphere volume. Differentiating energy with respect to radius shows two competing radial forces: an inward restoring surface-tension force proportional to radius and surface-energy density, and a pressure-difference force proportional to surface area and the pressure excess inside over outside. Equilibrium defines the Laplace balance, where pressure difference equals twice surface tension divided by radius, establishing an unstable critical nucleation radius.
Below the critical radius, surface tension dominates and the bubble collapses; above it, positive interior-minus-exterior pressure difference drives runaway radial acceleration. Including wall inertia in the Euler–Lagrange evolution equation ensures conservation of total kinetic plus surface plus volume potential energy3. Notice that if the exterior medium possesses more negative pressure than the interior, then interior pressure minus exterior pressure is strictly positive, producing an outward force across the interface. Calling this outward acceleration “exterior gravitational attraction” is physically mistaken: it is a local stress-gradient force across a contact wall, identical in mechanism to an overpressured vapor bubble expanding in a lower-pressure fluid. Furthermore, the three-dimensional thermal nucleation barrier scales with the cube of surface tension divided by the square of pressure difference, whereas four-dimensional semiclassical quantum tunneling (Coleman 1977) scales with the fourth power of surface tension divided by the cube of vacuum energy difference and cannot be interchanged without altering action dimensionality2.

Model B: First-Order Vacuum Phase Transition (Historical Benchmark)

In standard scalar-field vacuum decay (Coleman 1977; Coleman & De Luccia 1980), two homogeneous field minima have unequal potential energy densities, forming a metastable false vacuum outside and a lower-energy true vacuum inside2. A wall forms where the scalar field interpolates across the potential barrier, giving a positive wall tension set by the integrated field gradient across the barrier23. Semiclassical tunneling nucleates a critical true-vacuum bubble whose growth converts higher-energy false vacuum into lower-energy true vacuum, transferring the liberated bulk energy directly into the kinetic energy of the accelerating wall2. Gravitational backreaction introduces bulk curvature terms that enlarge the bounce action and, if true-vacuum energy is negative, risk a post-tunneling collapse rather than eternal growth23. Crucially, standard vacuum decay does not predict expansion caused by “outside gravity pulling the wall.” Instead, the higher energy density and more negative pressure of the exterior false vacuum relative to the interior true vacuum create a positive outward energy-conversion gradient; gravitational effects of the false vacuum inhibit or curve the background rather than pulling the wall outward like a surrounding attracting shell2.

Model C: Published Relativistic Shell Benchmark Only — Not an ArcSecs Physical Mechanism

Strictly as a historical geometric benchmark and never as an endorsed ArcSecs physical mechanism, Israel’s (1966) thin-shell junction formalism matches distinct interior and exterior spherically symmetric solutions across a three-dimensional wall history parameterized by shell proper time10. Squaring this junction identity yields a one-dimensional effective radial potential governing shell acceleration3.
Benchmarking against Blau, Guendelman, and Guth (1987) reveals why thin-shell geometry cannot validate outward pulling by exterior gravity1. For a high-energy de Sitter bubble embedded in a lower-energy Schwarzschild exterior, the interior pressure is lower (more negative) than the exterior zero-pressure vacuum, so local pressure forces point inward1. Unbound solutions above the critical Schwarzschild mass occur only when the exterior orientation sign reverses across a critical radius, meaning the growing bubble disconnects through a wormhole throat as a separate closed domain while an outside observer sees only a black hole1. Conversely, for a true-vacuum bubble inside a false-vacuum de Sitter exterior (the classical Coleman–De Luccia growth regime), unbounded hyperbolic growth occurs only if dimensionless wall tension stays below unity relative to the exterior Hubble scale, driven by the interior-minus-exterior vacuum energy difference3. Thus, generalized junction rules neither eliminate the requirement for localized wall stress to support a pressure jump nor create an isotropic outward gravitational pull.

The 2,000-point Fibonacci quadrature verifies Newton’s shell theorem: for a uniform exterior shell, net internal force magnitude is 1.876 × 10⁻⁷ against a baseline exterior gravitational pull scale of 4.000 × 10⁻², with mean isotropic wall stretching -3.229 × 10⁻¹⁰ (pure discretization floor)3. Adding a 10 percent exterior mass dipole shifts the bubble’s entire center of mass by bulk acceleration +1.3331 × 10⁻³ while leaving mean isotropic wall expansion at -7.429 × 10⁻¹⁰ and tidal shear at +4.4777 × 10⁻⁸3. Conversely, a 10 percent exterior mass quadrupole produces zero bulk center-of-mass shift (-2.1117 × 10⁻⁷) and zero isotropic expansion (-2.642 × 10⁻¹⁰), but generates an anisotropic tidal shear of +2.4004 × 10⁻⁴ along the symmetry axis3.
In the triaxial Model D integrations, pure isotropic pressure growth preserves zero shear to machine precision3. Combining pressure or a dimensionless relational gradient of 1.2 with a weak tidal field of 0.05 drives radial growth from 1.200 to 2.124 and 2.908 respectively while accumulating large anisotropic shape distortions (+8.3194 × 10⁻² and +6.6925 × 10⁻²)3.
