# Abstract

ArcSecs investigates a physical-process alternative framework in which time is a universal ordering parameter, location is relational, photons have positive mass, and the propagation of light may slow following emission. The program asks whether physical motion, environmental interactions and collective cosmic inertia can account for observations commonly described using expanding spacetime. This consolidated review preserves the twelve investigator-specified foundations, reconciles the original report archive with the subsequent analytical and numerical work, and develops the clarified C-01 hypothesis: the matter distribution of our cosmic domain may determine the maximum initial speed of light, with different effective conditions possible in hypothetical other domains. The approach treats a light-speed ceiling, photon group velocity, rate of separation, and speed of information as distinct quantities. We summarize finite-body screening, gauge-invariant massive-vector electrodynamics, atomic reference standards, autonomous matter–field dynamics, optical reciprocity and cosmological distance tests. The strongest results are conditional constraints: simple common-field models encounter spectroscopy and lensing conflicts; reduced autonomous models produce temporary slowing and redshift but are not physically calibrated; straight-ray distance laws can increase rather than decrease inferred source sizes; reciprocal focusing cannot independently repair a luminosity-distance discrepancy; and a static mass-sourced regulator can have a nonzero field in an empty shell while permitting ray reacceleration beyond it. A new massive-packet drag benchmark shows that redshift from passive momentum loss is compatible with decreasing group velocity, but a very small fixed photon rest energy implies negligible speed reduction at moderate redshift. Stationary age-dependent loss alone also fails to produce redshift-dependent stretching of successive packets. No candidate has yet reproduced all astronomical and local constraints simultaneously. The paper provides an evidence ledger and a falsifiable research plan rather than claiming a validated replacement for conventional cosmology.

**Keywords:** ArcSecs; photon rest mass; photon aging; relational gravity; Machian inertia; cosmic bubbles; optical reciprocity; distance duality; cosmological redshift; physical clocks.

**Manuscript status.** Complete consolidated *review draft*, not peer-reviewed, not an observational likelihood analysis, and not a completed unified theory. The original investigator's premises are preserved as research requirements. Reproducible results from prior reports are identified as reported results; only the new Part XXVIII calculations are rerun for this edition. External references selected for this review were checked against publication or abstract records. The complete historical reports and original citations remain in the companion archive. No remote repository has been modified.

# Research charter and scope

The project is organized around the following twelve working foundations, supplied by the investigator. They are retained as research premises, not restated as experimental findings. Historical source reports use an eleven-item charter in places; those sources are preserved verbatim in the archive rather than silently renumbered.

1. Photons have mass.
2. Time is constant.
3. Light slows down over time.
4. Time is not physical; it is the “When.”
5. Space is not physical; it is the “Where.”
6. Everything physically existing at any moment occupies area or extent and has mass.
7. Spacetime is not physical.
8. Light is not the fastest in the universe.
9. Objects can separate faster than causality; nonphysical time, space and spacetime cannot physically stretch.
10. The size of the universe is unknown.
11. The age of the universe is unknown.
12. Distant objects are smaller and closer than they appear.

These requirements govern the search for candidate mechanisms. They do not exempt any model from empirical testing. In particular, Foundation 9 is interpreted operationally as permitting separation rates exceeding a specified signal-speed ceiling; it does not establish locally transmitted information outside that ceiling. Foundation 12 requires comparison with independently specified conventional distance estimates and a joint fit to angles, brightness, spectra and timing. Its scope cannot be established by assuming the desired distance correction in advance. Foundation 8 is compatible with a distinction between old photons and a faster field mode below a local causal maximum, but an all-sector limit and mode ordering must be derived. “Mass” requires an explicit distinction between invariant rest mass and energy, momentum or gravitational response. A localized quantum state is not necessarily a rigid body with a classical radius.

## What, where and when

Physical entities and their interactions comprise the proposed ontology; position labels $\mathbf{x}$ and ordering parameter $T$ describe where and when events occur. A clock is a physical system with measurable phase or transition count. The choice not to regard mathematical spacetime as a substance does not itself invalidate relativistic predictions: the alternative must predict those same observed clock, motion and gravity measurements wherever they have been verified. A preferred ordering parameter alone does not give a laboratory an operational measurement of absolute uniform velocity. The present SI numerical value $c_0=299\,792\,458$ m/s is a unit-defined local reference, not an independent derivation of a cosmic constant. Differences in nature must appear in dimensionless ratios or comparisons of distinct physical processes.

# C-01: the origin of a local emission-speed ceiling

## The two-spacecraft thought experiment

Imagine two spacecraft surviving from different long-disappeared cosmic bubbles. Each retains a constant inherited trajectory from an earlier period of physical separation. Neither crew feels an acceleration; in the absence of external reference objects it cannot infer its uniform motion solely from the fact that its accelerometer reads zero. The ships eventually approach one another. If a chosen common bookkeeping frame assigns velocities $+u$ and $-u$, the coordinate separation decreases at rate $2u$. In a hypothetical Galilean emission model in which each ship emits forward at $c_0$ relative to itself, the two opposing wavefronts would close at $2(u+c_0)$, yielding $4c_0$ for $u=c_0$. This latter calculation presupposes additive photon velocities; it is not a physical prediction of special relativity and cannot be combined with a common-frame individual photon ceiling of $c_0$. Even in ordinary relativity, opposing signals can have a coordinate closing rate of $2c_0$ while neither transmits a signal faster than $c_0$. No material ship can travel at exactly $c_0$ in special relativity. The thought experiment is a motivation, not a derivation of different transformation laws.

**Investigator's clarified intention.** The central question is not whether light goes at $4c_0$, but what physically establishes the maximum speed at which newly emitted light begins in our present cosmic environment, and why it should begin at that measured maximum rather than begin faster and subsequently slow to it. The proposed answer is a collective gravitational/inertial influence of the cosmic domain's matter distribution. A hollow domain, material boundary, or exterior might have different constitutive conditions. This clarification supersedes interpretations that use the thought experiment as evidence of single-photon superluminal propagation.

The hypothesis is related in spirit to Machian inertia, including Sciama's proposal that inertia could arise from inductive effects of distant matter [1]. This is historical motivation only. A constitutive field must supply actual equations, conservation laws, and observable predictions; ordinary gravitational attraction is not a strict speed ceiling. In Newtonian gravity an exactly spherical, uniform shell produces zero net force at every interior point, although the gravitational potential relative to infinity is nonzero. Invoking a universal potential as a locally measurable field additionally requires fixing the potential offset physically. C-01 therefore investigates a physical environmental variable, not merely a choice of gravitational potential zero.

## Distinct speeds and the monotonicity requirement

Let $C_{\rm em}$ be the local initial emission ceiling, $C_{\rm f}$ the electromagnetic characteristic or front speed, $C_{\rm causal}$ the fastest causal characteristic of all interacting sectors, $v_g$ the group velocity of a specified photon packet, and $V_{\rm sep}=d|\mathbf{x}_2-\mathbf{x}_1|/dT$ a coordinate separation rate. A candidate must state the frame, physical clocks, signal definitions and transformation rules used for these quantities. A proposed strict aging requirement is

$$v_g(T_e+\tau)\leq v_g(T_e)\leq C_{\rm em}(\mathbf{x}_e,T_e),\qquad \frac{dv_g}{d\tau}\leq 0,$$

with $v_g\leq C_{\rm causal}$ along the path. The exact equality $v_g(T_e)=C_{\rm em}=c_0$ for a massive finite-energy packet is not automatically realized by the dispersion laws used below. We preserve the equality as an idealized research target, while explicitly identifying the front-speed or high-frequency-limit interpretation as a distinct candidate. In a changing environment a monotonic history is an additional physical requirement, not implied by a local ceiling.

# Physical fields, finite-body screening and photon mass

## Common-field candidate and atomic standards

The original A-05c report (Part IX) explores a scalar field $\psi$ coupled to electromagnetism and gravitational propagation. Its representative quadratic screened equation has the schematic form

$$K\partial_T^2\psi-S\nabla^2\psi+(m_s^2+g\rho)\psi=f,$$

with positive kinetic and gradient coefficients $K,S$. The local equilibrium response and screening length are not sufficient to determine an actual finite object solution. One must solve the boundary problem, with the relevant range $\ell=\sqrt{S/(m_s^2+g\rho)}$ and size ratio $q=R/\ell$. Small $q$ can mean weak screening even for a high local density. This distinction is a central finding of Parts XI–XII. A-05c did not find a stable parameter set satisfying its combined redshift, spectroscopy, lensing and multimessenger constraints [A1].

Part XII examined a chameleon-like inverse-power potential and a density-driven symmetry-transition potential. Under the specified linear-density coupling and fixed gradient coefficient, its comparison bound limited the solar central field depletion to about $9.03\times10^{-6}$, despite an effective local-minimum estimate $q_\odot\approx2.07\times10^5$. The estimated minimum was not actually attained in the finite object. The leading scalar optical bending could nevertheless be about 1.66 arcseconds, opposite the desired attractive deflection for the chosen coupling. These findings exclude particular parameterized implementations, not every nonlinear scalar theory.

Part XIII investigated screening the electromagnetic coupling instead of the scalar. For a homogeneous, fixed-$e,m_e,\hbar$ response with the chosen sign of permittivity coupling, generating positive propagation redshift entails a logarithmic fine-structure change at least as large as that redshift. A gated medium additionally produces optical boundaries. Part XIV smoothed the density profile and solved a continuous environmental gate; at amplitude $10^{-5}$ the example ray at half a 100-kpc halo radius had approximately 1.814 arcseconds of extra deflection. Changing a step to a smooth gradient does not eliminate refraction. A cosmological model cannot independently tune intergalactic optical evolution, atomic frequencies, and gravitational lensing without justifying each interaction.

## Positive-mass electromagnetic sector

A positive rest-mass hypothesis must distinguish the measurable photon energy $E=\hbar\omega$ from a positive mass gap. A local massive-vector candidate can be represented by a gauge-invariant Stueckelberg construction (Part XIX); a scalar compensator transforms with the vector potential so that a combination of vector potential and scalar derivative is gauge invariant. For the matched transverse and longitudinal coefficients used there, the modes have local dispersion

$$\omega^2=C^2 k^2+\Omega^2,\qquad \Omega>0,$$

and, in a homogeneous background,

$$v_g=\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial k}=C\sqrt{1-\frac{\Omega^2}{\omega^2}},\quad v_{\rm phase}=\frac{\omega}{k},\quad v_{\rm front}=C.$$

The phase velocity is not the information speed. A finite-frequency positive-gap group packet never reaches $C$ exactly in this dispersion. When the local dispersion has the usual relativistic form with characteristic speed $C$, the rest-energy proxy is $\hbar\Omega=m_\gamma C^2$. Across domains, fixed gap and fixed mass are different assumptions. Photon rest mass is not established merely by radiation pressure, gravitational bending, localization or energy transfer. Proca-like electrodynamics is subject to strong model-dependent upper limits [2]; alternative mass-generation mechanisms require distinct tests [3].

The matched vector theory also gives a static Yukawa potential, proportional to $e^{-r/\ell_Y}/r$, with $\ell_Y=C/\Omega$. In Part XX, expansion of this potential gives the leading fractional hydrogen 1S–2S correction $-6(a_B/\ell_Y)^2$. A small positive gap can therefore leave ordinary atomic transition frequencies essentially unchanged while environmental changes in electron mass or electromagnetic coefficients have separate effects. A dynamical theory must preserve charge continuity, positive mode energies and observed longitudinal and static-electric limits. The earlier reduced-mode energy exchange checks do not establish a complete interacting quantum theory.

## Constant ordering and physical clocks

Let the phase rate of a particular clock species $j$ be $\nu_j(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v},T)$. Its observed count is $N_j=\int\nu_j\,dT$. A clock-rate change can then be described as a physical-process change in the proposed ontology without treating the ordering parameter itself as material. This is a representation, not an empirical alternative until its dependence on environment and velocity is derived. A successful model must account for optical, microwave, nuclear and unstable-particle clocks, including moving muon decay, rather than only perturb electronic orbitals. The original constant-time report proposes differential thorium-229 versus strontium-87 clock tests as a way to break universal rescaling degeneracy. Equal fractional changes across every process may merely reproduce conventional measurements; sector-specific changes would give testable ratios. Atomic emission and detection must be solved in the same model as photon propagation.

# Physical recession and energy transport

## Redshift and moving sources

For a local eikonal phase $S$ with frequency $\omega=-\partial_T S$ and wavevector $\mathbf{k}=\nabla S$, a receiver on trajectory $\mathbf{x}_r(T)$ samples phase at rate $\omega-\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{v}_r$. Comparing this with the receiver's physical clock standard gives an operational spectroscopic redshift; the emitter must obey its own phase-matching condition. This decomposition separates source motion, propagation, receiver motion and changes to the atomic reference. In a static environment, Hamiltonian ray dynamics preserves coordinate frequency because $d\omega/dT=\partial_T\omega=0$. Spatially varying group speed does not by itself imply a cumulative spectral shift. A time-varying medium can transfer energy to or from waves, but a common matter and field energy ledger is needed.

Part XV proposed a physical recession law with constant $H$ and a homogeneous, globally time-dependent speed with rate $\gamma$. In its high-frequency, stable-clock, straight-ray construction, $z=(H+\gamma)D_e/c_0$ and the observed low-redshift slope fixes $H+\gamma$. The emission distance $D_e$ at given $z$ is therefore independent of the chosen split. This is not the same model as per-photon aging since emission. The simple distance law gives larger angular sizes than the desired Foundation 12 correction. Part XVI engineered a different separation-dependent recession law, but it developed a finite-distance pole; one illustrative configuration reached that singularity before photons from sources seen above $z\approx0.418$ could arrive. Part XVII replaced the pole with a bounded conservative two-body potential. It gives smaller distances over a limited redshift interval but larger distances at high redshift. Its illustrative dimensionless redshift drift at $z=1$ is 2.05038 versus 0.23932 for the selected reference cosmology. A good low-redshift slope alone is not sufficient to establish a cosmology.

## Autonomous reduced dynamics

Parts XVIII–XXIV investigated physical wave–scalar energy exchange, gauge coupling, atomic emission and moving receivers. Part XVIII derives a time-independent reduced Lagrangian in which a wave exchanges energy with a scalar while total energy is conserved. Part XIX extended the electromagnetic degrees of freedom. Part XXI solved a one-dimensional autonomous system of two matter clumps and a scalar: separation 3 to 12.27837, midpoint field 0.085338 to 1.155239, and center fields near 0.0095–0.0115 in dimensionless units. Halving the time step reduced reported relative energy drift from $4.64\times10^{-6}$ to $1.16\times10^{-6}$. A weak fixed-coordinate probe accumulated $z_{\rm prop}=0.358319535$; a static-profile control conserved its frequency. These reduced dense clumps are not calibrated galaxies.

Part XXII moved the source and receiver with the clumps. In one example $z_{\rm spec}=0.760886$ whereas $z_{\rm prop}=0.059662$; endpoint motion was the predominant contributor. Non-arrival by the end of a finite simulation did not establish a permanent horizon. Part XXIII analytically distinguished a finite packet reach, a front reach, and a separate scalar-signal reach under an assumed eternal speed decline. Part XXIV then proved a positive lower bound on speed in a particular finite-energy closed scalar model: a bounded field cannot make $e^{-\lambda\psi}$ tend to zero indefinitely. The numerical background exhibited temporary slowing and later variation, not a strictly monotone eternal law. These limitations are important for a physically credible aging mechanism.

## Spatial dynamics and ray bundles (Part XXV)

Part XXV uses two finite-width Gaussian concentrations in a two-dimensional periodic field, with an explicit repulsive pair interaction and scalar backreaction from a common action. For $U_0=5$, the concentration separation decreases from 3 to 2.097; for $U_0=10$, it grows to 5.764 by $T=7$. In the latter case the midpoint scalar rises from 0.05472 to 0.50503 while the centers remain near 0.0067. A positive-gap photon emitted at $T_e=1.5$ reaches the moving receiver at $T_a=5.59396$ with $z_{\rm spec}=0.64058$ and propagation-only redshift 0.04436. Transverse spreading of the selected planar ray bundle was about 22%. The matter–scalar sector conserves energy numerically; the photons are weak probes, so this is not a finite-amplitude radiation energy ledger. The demonstration neither gives a 3D luminosity distance nor establishes physical galaxy separation driven by the chosen interaction.

# Astronomical distances, sizes and brightness

## Distance definitions and a 1 Mpc control

An arcsecond measures angle; a parsec is a geometric parallax distance convention (approximately 3.26 light-years). A light-year is a defined length, not a statement that every photon travels that length each year. Distinguish present physical separation, emission separation, path length, a constant-speed travel-time estimate, angular diameter distance $D_A$, and luminosity distance $D_L$. These are different observables or inferences. If a stationary source and receiver are actually 1 Mpc apart and the photon's *independently measured* average travel speed is $\bar v$, then

$$D_{\rm inferred}=c_0\tau=D_{\rm actual}\frac{c_0}{\bar v}.$$

A photon that starts at $c_0$ and slows exponentially in age, $v=c_0e^{-\gamma\tau}$, traverses $D=(c_0/\gamma)(1-e^{-\gamma\tau})$. If its arrival speed is half the initial speed, $D_{\rm inferred}/D=2\ln 2=1.38629$. Thus a true 1 Mpc distance would be inferred as 1.386 Mpc from that measured travel time. A globally changing characteristic speed which was faster in the past than today instead gives the opposite sign in a direct time-of-flight estimate. Real astronomical travel ages are usually unknown. An actual redshift-distance prediction requires calculating observed frequency and the chosen conventional inference procedure; a travel-time example is not a distance measurement.

For geometric anchors, the new theory must model propagation through parallax and binary-distance experiments, not merely multiply distances by a universal correction.

## Two-direction optical geometry (Part XXVI)

Define $D_A$ from source transverse area and observer solid angle and $D_G$ from receiver area and emitter solid angle. Let $A=dT_a/dT_e$ be the pulse-arrival stretch, $Z_E=E_e/E_a$ the single-photon energy ratio, and $\mathcal T$ the surviving fraction of photons in a defined ray bundle. For isotropic emission, photon counting gives

$$F=\frac{L_e\mathcal T}{4\pi D_G^2 Z_E A},\qquad D_L=D_G\sqrt{\frac{Z_E A}{\mathcal T}}.$$

The energy, rate, and geometric factors must each follow from a physical model. When identical endpoint standards give $Z_E=A=1+z$, and the chosen reciprocal static optical transport gives $D_G=D_A$, the ratio to the conventional distance-duality combination is

$$\eta=\frac{D_L}{(1+z)^2D_A}=\frac{1}{(1+z)\sqrt{\mathcal T}}.$$

The usual conventional reciprocity result in metric geometrical optics has a different redshift relation between $D_G$ and $D_A$; we must not silently import it into an alternative Euclidean ray model. In the Part XXVI reciprocal test, a smooth refractive lens changes both beam-area distances together. The example needs a refractive contrast of about 28.7% to reduce angular distance by 20%. If $D_A$ is 0.8 times the reference angular distance at $z=1$, matching the reference $D_L$ under the displayed assumptions requires $\mathcal T=(0.8/2)^2=0.16$ and gives $\eta=1.25$. Loss alone cannot match both independent distances and the same reference duality; it would also require a physical account of deposited energy and any radiation background. No such fitted attenuation law is derived here.

# Cosmic bubbles and the environmental regulator

## Physical boundary versus geometric convention

The original cosmic-bubble investigations (Z01, Z11, Z12, Z22, Z23) explore a physical domain, possible shell structures, exterior forces, and birth/death of domains in a larger cosmos. A boundary in a density or field distribution is different from a survey horizon or a mathematical surface. The archive reports no statistically validated detection of a neighboring bubble. The following geometries are hypothetical alternatives: distributed matter within a domain, low-density cavity enclosed by a shell, nonuniform exterior mass, or no material boundary at all. The total age and size of a larger physical cosmos are not inferred from the radius or history of an observable region. Stellar ages and chronometer data still impose constraints on local physical history. Formation, stability and evolution of any wall require a matter dynamics model and energy budget; a static shell alone proves none of these.

## Finite-range collective response (Part XXVII)

Use a scalar environmental variable $I$ with a positive kinetic term and finite range $\lambda$. In a static limit,

$$(-\nabla^2+\lambda^{-2})I=g\rho,$$

$$I(\mathbf{x})=\frac{g}{4\pi}\int \rho(\mathbf{x}')\frac{e^{-|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'|/\lambda}}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}'|}\,d^3x'.$$

The second expression is a static equilibrium Green function, not instantaneous signaling. A dynamical completion may use $C_I^{-2}\partial_T^2 I-\nabla^2 I+\lambda^{-2}I=g\rho$ with retarded conditions. A reduced positive electromagnetic mode Lagrangian with a kinetic coefficient proportional to $1+\alpha I^2$ gives a characteristic speed of the form

$$C(I)=\frac{C_*}{\sqrt{1+\alpha I^2}},\qquad \alpha\geq0.$$

The mass-gap coefficient must be chosen consistently with the kinetic normalization. For example, for a real reduced mode $a$, $\mathcal L_a=\tfrac12(1+\alpha I^2)[(\partial_T a)^2-\Omega^2a^2]-\tfrac12 C_*^2|\nabla a|^2$ gives the stated fixed-gap dispersion in a constant background. This demonstrates a hyperbolic mode with positive quadratic energy in that background; it is not a proof of stability of a complete coupled vector, matter and gravitational theory. In particular, the regulator field has its own characteristic $C_I$. If it is faster, $C(I)$ is not the all-sector causal ceiling. The constitutive form and reference $C_*$ are assumptions; normalizing the model to $c_0$ does not derive the observed numerical value from cosmic mass. A mechanical inertia law must also be independently specified and tested.

For an infinitesimally thin shell of mass $M$ and radius $R$, let $q=R/\lambda$ and $x=r/R$. Direct angular integration gives

$$I(0)=\frac{gM}{4\pi R}e^{-q},$$

$$\frac{I(r)}{I(0)}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sinh(qx)}{qx},&x\leq1,\\[4pt]\dfrac{\sinh(q)}{q}\dfrac{e^{-q(x-1)}}{x},&x\geq1.\end{cases}$$

The interior response is nonzero even where the Newtonian shell force vanishes. The finite-range scalar gradient need not vanish except at the center or in the long-range symmetric limit; ordinary shell theorem does not apply to every modified force. ## Void benchmark and experimental interpretation

For an otherwise uniform infinite density with the finite-range static kernel, $I_{\rm ref}=g\rho_0\lambda^2$. Removing matter in a sphere of radius $b$ removes a fraction of the center response

$$f(s)=1-(1+s)e^{-s},\qquad s=b/\lambda.$$

At $\alpha I_{\rm ref}^2=1$, the center speed changes by $\sqrt{2/[1+(1-f)^2]}-1$. For $s=0.01,0.1,1$, this is approximately $2.4834\times10^{-5}$, $0.00234215$ and $0.13910987$. The infinite uniform distribution is a mathematical benchmark with a convergent kernel, not a statement about finite total cosmic mass. Real voids contain matter, move and have structure; no observed speed variation is claimed. A comparison must predict changes in clock ratios, signal travel times, phase, and gravity versus electromagnetic propagation. If every sector and standard changes together, the coordinate-speed change may not be independently observable.

# Part XXVIII: irreversible photon aging — new bounded test

## Motivation and model

The previous shell result separates the local emission ceiling from the post-emission slowing mechanism. Can the simplest passive momentum-loss mechanism yield the desired aging, redshift and arrival behavior? Consider a homogeneous stationary environment with fixed $C$, positive rest energy $E_m=\hbar\Omega=m_\gamma C^2$ and a single forward packet with momentum $p>0$. Adopt an explicit effective drag law, not a microscopic interaction derivation:

$$E(p)=\sqrt{C^2p^2+E_m^2},\qquad v=\frac{\partial E}{\partial p}=\frac{C^2p}{E},\qquad \dot p=-\gamma p,\ \gamma>0.$$

This is an open packet description in a selected environmental rest frame; it is not Lorentz invariant as written. Define the dimensionless momentum $u=Cp/E_m$, initial $u_e>0$, and age $\tau=T-T_e$. Then $u=u_e e^{-\gamma\tau}$ and

$$\frac{v}{C}=\frac{u}{\sqrt{1+u^2}},\qquad \frac{d v}{dT}=-\frac{\gamma v}{1+u^2}<0.$$

The derivative demonstrates strict monotonicity in the stationary medium. The energy and momentum lost by the packet are

$$\dot E=-\gamma pv=-\gamma E\left(1-\frac{E_m^2}{E^2}\right),\qquad \dot E_{\rm bath}=+\gamma pv,\qquad \dot p_{\rm bath}=+\gamma p.$$

The bath ledger balances energy and momentum formally but does not supply physical bath degrees of freedom, their recoil, entropy production, noise or stress. A full conservative completion must derive $\gamma$, implement local exchange, and test whether this collinear continuous-loss approximation can avoid scattering, line broadening and excess heat. A passive bath at nonzero temperature may return energy to packets; deterministic irreversible loss is a macroscopic effective assumption. The calculated wave retains a positive rest energy at late time, not a demonstrated dark-matter population.

## Exact distance and redshift relations

Integrating the speed yields

$$D(\tau)=\frac{C}{\gamma}\left[\operatorname{arsinh}u_e-\operatorname{arsinh}(u_e e^{-\gamma\tau})\right].$$

The packet approaches the finite travel distance $D_{\max}=(C/\gamma)\operatorname{arsinh}u_e$ as its momentum tends to zero. This is a packet-range result under eternal drag, not an all-sector causal horizon. For stable identical atomic references and no endpoint motion, $1+z=E_e/E_a$. Set $\epsilon=E_m/E_e$, with $0<\epsilon<1$. Then

$$\boxed{\frac{v_a}{C}=\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2(1+z)^2}}$$

for $\epsilon(1+z)<1$. The allowed redshift has upper limiting value $1/\epsilon-1$. The fractional speed deficit can be evaluated accurately without subtractive cancellation as

$$1-\frac{v_a}{C}=\frac{\epsilon^2(1+z)^2}{1+\sqrt{1-\epsilon^2(1+z)^2}}.$$

For small $\epsilon$, its leading value is $\epsilon^2(1+z)^2/2$. This is a new conditional obstruction: a tiny fixed gap plus modest energy loss does not yield substantial speed loss. Large speed reduction requires the packet energy to approach the rest-energy gap. At $v_a=C/2$, $E_a=2E_m/\sqrt3$ and $1+z=\sqrt3/(2\epsilon)$. For $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ this would require a frequency ratio of about $8.66\times10^7$, not an ordinary optical redshift of order unity. Positive mass alone is therefore not sufficient to derive substantial tired-light deceleration. Conversely, a large photon gap would create strong chromatic effects and conflict with existing mass tests in the corresponding dispersion model.

## A stationary aging law does not reproduce supernova time dilation

For fixed stationary endpoints separated by $D<D_{\max}$, equal emission energies and identical environmental conditions, the travel age $\tau_D$ follows from the distance equation. It has no dependence on the absolute emission time. Therefore $T_a=T_e+\tau_D$, and

$$\frac{dT_a}{dT_e}=1.$$

Even when each packet loses half its energy ($z=1$), successive identical packet intervals are not stretched. A light curve may broaden through frequency-dependent dispersion, but this is not a general achromatic factor $1+z$ multiplying its intrinsic time axis. A moving source or a medium that evolves in absolute time can change this conclusion; so can a physical source clock response, if independently justified. The Dark Energy Survey measured a stretching exponent $b=1.003\pm0.005$ (statistical) $\pm0.010$ (systematic), using a model proportional to $(1+z)^b$ for 1,504 Type Ia supernovae [5]. This places a strong observational burden on the stationary loss-only subclass, not on every model including recession and time evolution.

## Can drag prevent exterior reacceleration?

In a slowly varying medium with dispersion $\omega^2=C^2k^2+\Omega^2$, set $u=Ck/\Omega$. Along the ray the algebraic group velocity satisfies

$$\frac{d\ln v_g}{dT}=\frac{d\ln C}{dT}+\frac{1}{1+u^2}\frac{d\ln u}{dT}.$$

Here the total derivatives include environmental change and the Hamiltonian momentum equation plus any drag; they cannot be chosen independently. To enforce monotonic slowing during a rise of $C$, the second term must be sufficiently negative. In the high-frequency regime $u\gg1$, the factor $1/(1+u^2)$ strongly suppresses momentum-loss effects on speed. Moderate drag cannot generally compensate a substantial rise in the local characteristic while leaving the spectrum in the same band. An additional history-dependent constitutive mechanism, a dynamic common field, or a revision of the idealized universal monotonicity law is needed. No such mechanism has been independently derived by imposing a minimum of previous and current speeds. The C-01 result and the new drag benchmark jointly constrain a viable theory.

## New calculation verification

The included Python program compares the closed distance equation with adaptive numerical integration at 19 combinations of positive gap fraction and redshift. Maximum absolute discrepancy in units $C/\gamma$ is $5.32907\times10^{-15}$. It checks monotone packet energy, the arithmetic energy ledger and equal stationary pulse intervals. The CSV reports the stable speed-deficit formula. The bath is not simulated, so the bookkeeping test is not proof of a microscopic conservation law. This benchmark is an analytical and numerical test of a deliberately restricted ansatz, not a claim of a derived universal photon-aging interaction.

# Relational gravity, horizons and the wider program

The original gravity reports investigate a relational physical-force alternative to interpreting curvature as a substance. A full theory must reproduce solar-system light bending, Shapiro delay, orbit precession, gravitational clock shifts, binary pulsar losses, equivalence-principle tests and gravitational radiation. Treating Newtonian attraction or an arbitrary Machian kernel as an all-purpose substitute does not supply those results. The positive-mass photon proposal also requires a consistent description of polarization, gravitational deflection and black-hole capture. A packet horizon caused by accumulated slowing or a particular detector trajectory is not automatically a universal information horizon. Conventional black-hole observations involve stellar/gas dynamics, accretion, lensing and ring structures; a proposed capture law must predict those independently.

The dark-sector and propulsion reports (Z15, Z21, L06, L11) explore whether massive-photon remnants or collective inertia can account for missing gravitational effects and enable physical propulsion. They remain a separate research branch. A loss mechanism that deposits energy must predict where it goes, how its momentum is transferred, the resulting equation of state, and the resulting lensing and clustering. Galaxy rotation curves and merging clusters constrain whether that energy can be identified with the inferred dark component. The present packet's finite rest energy does not establish a stable population, its density, or a workable propulsion source. Any vehicle model needs a mass intake, force, reaction momentum and energy ledger. No propulsion performance is established by the simulations in this review.

# Observational discrimination and a unified-model specification

## Measurement tests

The model should be constructed from a local dynamical matter, field and electromagnetic action or a precisely defined effective open-system limit. It must specify physical source spectra, detector standards, a causal initial-value problem, boundary conditions, conserved energy and momentum including any bath, and enough independent measurements to determine parameters. Recession and aging must not be tuned separately for every source. A joint inference should use the same measured raw observables and calibrated nuisance parameters for the ArcSecs candidate and a conventional comparison model. Predeclare selection, systematics, likelihoods and rejection criteria; do not rely on outliers discovered after searching many datasets.

| Observable | Required model output | Decision criterion |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Atomic/nuclear clocks | Species-specific frequency ratios, motion and environment | Reproduce laboratory and astrophysical precision comparisons |
| Photon mass / radio dispersion | Longitudinal response, static range, band-dependent group delays | Consistent with plasma-corrected and laboratory limits in the specified mass mechanism |
| Geometric distances | Actual angular mapping, eclipsing-binary and parallax inference | Recover independent anchors without redefining distance units |
| Spectral redshift / SN timing | Moving endpoint spectra and broadband light curves | Fit frequency shifts and observed arrival-time stretch jointly |
| Flux / angular size | Forward and reverse beam maps, loss and emitted luminosity | Fit $D_L$, $D_A$, and surface brightness using one transport model |
| Redshift drift / structure | Evolution of recession and the environmental field | Predict time dependence and clustering, not just a static redshift law |
| Gravitation / multimessenger | Mode characteristics, lensing, clocks and arrival lags | Reproduce measured constraints including GW170817 |
| Bubble search | Specified wall/exterior templates and predicted correlations | Blinded held-out tests, trials-corrected significance, null controls |

GW170817/GRB 170817A places a differential speed constraint of roughly $-3\times10^{-15}$ to $+7\times10^{-16}$ on gravitational versus electromagnetic propagation in the published interpretation [6]. This depends on emission-time and propagation assumptions. A common environmental dependence can evade a *differential* test, but must be reconciled with clocks, matter and other modes. A photon-only slowdown cannot simply ignore the comparison. The laboratory orientation test of Nagel et al. [7] likewise constrains anisotropic changes; it does not directly bound an isotropic coordinate-speed parameter without a matter and clock model. These established benchmarks should be used as quantitative likelihood terms, not rhetorical endorsements of any ontology.

## Minimum completion criteria

A unified candidate must simultaneously solve (i) cosmic mass distribution and field response; (ii) actual inertial dynamics and momentum transfer; (iii) positive-mass gauge and causal mode stability; (iv) emission, aging and detection with consistent atomic standards; (v) dynamical physical recession and lensing; (vi) 3D radiative transfer with energy deposition; and (vii) observation-level distances, time dilation and growth. Calibration must establish dimensionless combinations from independent measurements rather than set the desired answer. Strict emission at the ceiling, positive gap, and strict universal age slowing form a central mathematical tension. Maintaining all three requires a revised physical dispersion or nonlocal-history mechanism with a causal local completion. The research should retain a viable approximation if exact equality is not demonstrated, labeling the departure from the charter requirement.

# Discussion and conclusion

ArcSecs offers a coherent *research agenda* centered on physical processes, nonmaterial location and ordering coordinates, and explicit distinctions between observables. Its most recent conceptual contribution is C-01, the question of whether collective cosmic mass determines the local initial propagation ceiling and whether other environments could differ. The thin-shell solution proves a limited mathematical point: a nonzero environmental response in a cavity is compatible with zero ordinary Newtonian shell force. It does not derive the observed $c_0$, a universal inertia law, or an actual exterior cosmos. The same solution exposes reacceleration in lower-response regions. The new aging benchmark shows that a fixed small mass gap with passive momentum drag changes frequency far more strongly than speed, and cannot produce the observed packet time stretching in a stationary age-only setup. Earlier screening, optical reciprocity and distance-inference results independently narrow the viable parameter space. The extreme-star and quasar examples should be analyzed with independent geometric and dynamical anchors rather than used as confirmation of a distance error.

The most productive next step is a causal, autonomous coupled matter–regulator–radiation model that derives both emission and irreversible energy transfer. First test its homogeneous regime against mass, clock and time-dilation requirements; then a finite 3D source, propagating beam, and receiver; finally a joint cosmological likelihood. If no stable candidate satisfies the required tests, report that negative result and identify which physical assumptions prevent success. The twelve research foundations, the C-01 clarification, and all original source material remain preserved for further investigation. The present consolidated paper is ready for substantive review; the physical theory remains unfinished.
