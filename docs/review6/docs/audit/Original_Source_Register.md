## Reconciliation with ArcSecs Parts I–XXV

1. **Full program breadth:** The earlier reports encompass not only cosmological propagation, but also physical extent and mass ontology, non-geometric gravity, clock behavior across matter species, black-hole capture, Machian inertia, speculative propulsion, and exterior bubble/greater-cosmos hypotheses. Parts XII–XXV concentrated on field screening, photon speed and moving rays; that narrower path must not silently be represented as the whole original ArcSecs program.
2. **Rest mass versus carried energy:** The photon-mass reports insist on a positive-rest-mass research premise but explicitly separate observed energy/momentum transfer from experimental detection of an intrinsic *nonzero rest mass*. Parts XIX–XXV use a positive dispersion gap as a candidate mathematical implementation. Those are connected but not logically identical without a calibrated mass-generation mechanism.
3. **Cosmic-distance claims are object- and model-dependent:** `ArcSecs Astronomical Distance Audit.md` documents both photometric candidates reclassified as nearby objects and independent spectroscopic high-redshift controls. It also reports that several simple static/slowing models infer *larger* angular-size distances at high redshift. Any Foundation #12 application must identify the precise objects, independent evidence, and physical lensing/distance law; reclassifying some sources does not reclassify all.
4. **Time and clocks:** `Constant-Time Physical Clock Processes.md` gives a multi-species clock agenda, including nuclear vs optical clock comparisons and unstable-particle decay. Parts XX–XXV mostly use simplified atomic frequency laws, so direct consistency across nuclear, atomic, and particle rates remains open.
5. **Tired-light transport:** `Physical Redshift Mechanisms Evaluation.md` reports a strong supernova light-curve time-dilation challenge to a pure constant-speed photon-loss model and discusses physical recession as a hybrid. Parts XV, XXII and XXV explored that hybrid but have not reproduced the complete original observational test matrix.
6. **Relational gravity and propulsion:** Original materials propose Weber–Mach inertia, gravity alternatives, dark-substrate/fuel capture, and spacecraft dynamics. None is empirically established by the subsequent scalar/photon simulations, and their predicted forces and energy budgets must be compared before combining them.
7. **Cosmic bubbles and the wider cosmos:** Nine full-depth and related observational-test reports address putative other cosmic domains, boundary forces and search protocols. These are a separate, explicitly provisional branch; they were largely absent from Parts XII–XXV and should have a separate model/evidence ledger.
8. **Physical extent:** `ArcSecs Mass Extent Location Ontology.md` distinguishes compact object localization, quantum scattering extent, electromagnetic field extent, and a rigid mechanical radius. That distinction matters for Foundation #6 and for numerical particle models.
9. **What/Where/When and units:** `Arcseconds vs. Light-Years_ Variable Speed(1).md` and `Parsecs for Speed and Area(1).md` develop angle-based and parsec-centric measurement. An *arcsecond* measures an angle, while a *parsec* measures distance through a geometric parallax convention. Changing photon propagation must not change these definitions by fiat; it changes how distances are inferred from light travel and other observations.
10. **Ontology versus tests:** The editorials argue that mathematical descriptions need not be physical substances; notably, they also caution ArcSecs against shielding its own models from falsification. Fixed project premises should not be mislabeled as independent experimental results.

## Immediate follow-on: a revised Part XXVI agenda

**Recommended title:** *Physically Calibrated Three-Dimensional Screening, Geometric Distance Standards, and Cross-Sector Observational Holdouts*.

**Scope:** (a) preserve the successful Part XXV toy-model equations while measuring how their screening parameters scale from atoms through galaxies; (b) derive three-dimensional ray Jacobians and radiative flux instead of applying a free brightness correction; (c) cross-check spectroscopic calibrators and high-redshift object identification using the original distance-audit cohorts; (d) incorporate supernova duration, precision clock, photon-mass dispersion and multimessenger constraints as independent holdouts; (e) maintain a separate research branch for bubble/greater-cosmos observational tests and another for Machian inertia/propulsion, without treating those hypotheses as established physics.

**Promotion rule:** A source-stated theoretical claim is not an empirically supported result unless its numerical calculation is reproducible, the relevant measurement is independently verified and the comparison uncertainty is reported. One successful toy simulation cannot establish the entire physical hypothesis.
