## Abstract

The preceding ArcSecs R39 study constructed reciprocal photon–source momentum exchange inside a translation-invariant effective interaction. Its synthetic numerical ledger conserves source-plus-photon energy and momentum, but its force is instantaneous and therefore does not explain how an influence travels. Here we construct a first finite-speed carrier benchmark with a moving physical emitter, a physical receiver, and an optional moving hypothetical exterior-bubble source. A mass-bearing messenger exists at the emitting source prior to launch, is accelerated by spending that source's internal energy, travels at a specified finite speed through relational locations, and transfers energy and momentum through an elastic local collision with a receiver. Source recoil occurs at emission, the messenger retains momentum in flight, and the receiver cannot respond before the carrier arrives. An executable Python standard-library model verifies this event-by-event accounting without describing coordinates or progression as physical media. In the chosen dimensionless benchmark, the internal source's carrier reaches the receiver at universal progression T of 5.000; an additional inward-traveling messenger emitted from an exterior source reaches it near T of 8.262 after the receiver's first interaction. Independent tests verify the total momentum and kinetic-plus-reservoir energy ledgers to floating-point tolerance. The external contact modifies the receiver's subsequent motion and therefore establishes a controllable example of delayed source influence. It does not establish an attractive exterior gravitational pull: ordinary positive-momentum incoming particles tend to push the receiver away from their emitter. The central unresolved task is to replace this mechanical exchange demonstration with a field action and stress law able to generate attractive interaction, lensing, and measurable cosmological predictions while satisfying independent observational limits.

**Keywords:** ArcSecs, physical-first cosmology, finite propagation, source recoil, conservation, messenger exchange, constant universal progression, exterior cosmic bubble, relational coordinates, non-substrate field, distinguishable experiment.

## 1. Binding ArcSecs charter and the physical question

The eleven premises are stated here in the direct form of the ArcSecs research program. They set the project's authorial direction, not a license to invent observational confirmation:

1. **Photons have mass.**
2. **Time is constant.**
3. **Light slows down over time.**
4. **Time does not physically exist; it is the “When.”**
5. **Space does not physically exist; it is the “Where.”**
6. **All that physically exists at any moment occupies area and has mass at that moment.**
7. **Spacetime does not physically exist.**
8. **Light is not the fastest thing in the universe.**
9. **Things move apart from each other faster than causality; time, space, or spacetime cannot be physically stretched because they are not physical substances to stretch.**
10. **The size of the universe is unknown.**
11. **Objects in the universe are smaller and closer than they appear.**

This assignment does not revisit a philosophical vote on whether to adopt those premises. It asks what additional physics is necessary if they are to become a measurable system. The exact causal question is whether an influence that changes the motion of a receiver can be traced continuously through identifiable physical participants. The emitter must pay the energy cost; the carrier must possess momentum during travel; the receiver must not react before contact; and an external participant, if one is postulated, must be assigned a physical position, motion, coupling and energy source. A diagram of a stretched location grid would answer none of those operational questions.

A second question concerns the word “pull.” A moving object beyond our observable domain might exert an influence, but that influence need not increase the entire universe's scale or draw every interior object in the same direction. At one boundary, incoming momentum can press inward. An exterior mass distribution could produce a directional gradient, center-of-mass acceleration or tides under a different interaction law. A pressure difference across a physical wall could move a wall without an exterior attractive field. Distinguishing those mechanisms is required before a proposed greater-cosmos effect becomes predictive.

In R39, moving the source can change the force on a distant photon without an intermediary delay. R40 isolates only that missing step. If a receiving AI treats the messenger's adjustable speed as a measured universal limiting speed, it has confused a model parameter with an observation.

R07 through R09 and R33 through R34 supply the greater-universe research context. This paper does not silently assume that a cosmic wall has been seen, that a parent universe is finite, or that a greater universe has the same known matter coupling as our local experiments. It uses an exterior participant only as a counterfactual test source. Its central achievement is to demonstrate which additional physical ledger entries would be necessary if a real external signal existed.

## 3. Explicit physical ontology and independence from material coordinates

The program's world state contains three types of physical record. A *body* is a source or receiver with a position relative to the chosen reference equipment, a velocity, an inertial mass parameter and an optional reservoir of physical internal energy. A *carrier* is a separately counted physical packet with mass, position, velocity, origin, intended first receiver and interaction status. An *event* is a launch, flight update or local collision recorded in constant progression T. Position and T provide relational descriptions of where and when these physical objects act; they do not exert the force themselves.

At launch, the messenger is already present in a stored state at the emitter. This design avoids the misleading accounting shortcut of creating carrier mass out of nothing at emission. The emitter expends stored physical energy to accelerate the carrier and acquires an opposite recoil. The carrier retains its energy and momentum while flying. At the receiver, the two objects undergo a specified local elastic encounter, with the total energy and momentum before and after that encounter unchanged. The model includes every modeled participant in its momentum total, including a candidate exterior source even if it only moves freely.

No aether, metric fabric, time-fluid or stretchable coordinate is a participant. A proposal that relies on an actual physical field must name that field as an interaction-bearing entity and provide its energy and momentum separately. That is not the same as granting physical agency to the location labels where field measurements occur. The present carrier is finite and localized, making it an intentionally simple realization of this distinction.

The use of one reference progression T is an ArcSecs premise. The model does not show that absolute simultaneity has been measured or that physical clocks with different oscillation rates can already be connected without an additional rate law. Those claims belong to R26, R29 and the unified process-model program. Here T is only the ordered event parameter, and carrier arrival is a distinct physical event in that order.

## 4. Numerical method and what is kept out of the manuscript

The executable source is in `tools/causal_messenger_v1.py`; its reproducibility guide is `docs/long-term-memory/research/causal-messenger-methods.md`. The program uses the Python standard library and deterministic event scheduling. It does not fit telescope data, solve a continuous field equation, evaluate arbitrary gravitational paths or calculate the event horizon of a black hole. All symbolic equations, parameter values, collision formulas, numerical output tables and executable code reside in the companion rather than this export-oriented research manuscript.

A launch event updates both the moving emitter and the stored carrier at the same progression label. The source's internal energy reservoir pays for the combined change in kinetic energy, including recoil. This model deliberately freezes the participants' inertial masses over a short process. If a mass–energy equivalence treatment is later introduced, the tiny conversion between reservoir energy and inertial mass must be included explicitly rather than silently assumed away. This is a transparent nonrelativistic approximation, not a completed particle theory.

After emission, every participant moves at its current finite velocity between interactions. The solver determines the first future meeting between each active carrier and its intended receiver from their relative positions and velocities. The receiver does not receive a predictive force from the carrier while the carrier is still elsewhere. The collision changes both velocities by the exact one-dimensional elastic-contact solution. The current computation records only each messenger's first contact and stops before any reflected messenger could collide with its source again; beyond that horizon, a production simulator must add all further collisions and scattering paths.

The first experimental-control scenario has one internal source and receiver. The second introduces a moving exterior source and a separate inward-going packet. The body representing that exterior source has no special cosmological status in the code: its “bubble” name is a hypothesis tag. A third paper would need to define what material surface, mass source, or field boundary generates that packet in a real cosmology.

To prevent false precision, the benchmark's positions, speeds and masses are dimensionless. The simulated travel times are progression units, not seconds inferred from an actual neighboring universe. The model does not assign a size, location or age to the whole physical universe. Neither is the messenger speed asserted to exceed or equal the observed speed of electromagnetic light. The numerical findings are computational consequences of stated arbitrary conditions.

## 5. Results: finite arrival, recoil and conservation

The internal control shows a carrier launched from a source at one physical location and delivered to a receiver at another. Immediately following launch, the emitting source has opposite recoil; before the carrier arrives, the receiver's velocity remains unchanged. The first contact occurs at T equal to 5.0 in the selected dimensionless scenario. The carrier changes direction and speed at the local collision, and the receiver receives a finite impulse. Momentum has not appeared at the receiver earlier merely because it was allocated to the carrier's future target.

In the exterior-source scenario, the independent moving source starts farther away on the opposite side of the receiver. Its launch changes its own motion and spends its energy reservoir. The externally emitted packet approaches the receiver from the other direction. The receiver first responds to the internal carrier, then later reacts to the exterior packet. That later contact occurs at approximately T equal to 8.262 because the receiver has already changed its motion after the first impact. This dependence on the receiver's actual position and velocity is important: a finite-speed interaction cannot be implemented merely by attaching a fixed delay to every source instruction.

For both scenarios, the modeled total momentum remains invariant to floating-point precision when all sources, carrier packets and the receiver are included. The combined kinetic and stored internal energy likewise remains invariant across launch, flight and first contact. The program tests those statements at every recorded event and every chosen checkpoint, rather than at the end alone. Separate carried momentum can be inspected while the messenger is en route, so conservation is not achieved by pretending a distant source and receiver exchange impulse simultaneously.

The external messenger does alter the receiver's subsequent motion. However, the direction matters. A packet arriving from the right carries leftward momentum and tends to transfer leftward impulse to the receiver. That is an inward *push away from the right-hand external source*, not a pull toward that source. If the incoming carrier model were presented as direct proof that external bubble universes gravitationally attract our boundary, the sign of its own momentum exchange would contradict that interpretation. The model thus supplies a genuine constructive constraint for the next attractive-field hypothesis.

The results are fully synthetic. There is no external-bubble detection, no photon rest-mass measurement, no recovered astronomical timing signal, and no observationally fixed coupling constant. Exact conservation in a controlled mechanical toy is necessary for a serious interaction story but far from sufficient to establish the source of gravity. It confirms that one specific kind of physically transported impulse can be implemented without adding a material space or time to the ontology.

## 6. What an actual attractive exterior field still requires

To convert a mechanical impulse into a directional attraction, an interaction model must distinguish momentum moving toward the receiver from mechanical stress or field momentum that can cause the receiver to accelerate toward the distant source. A real mediator may have a potential energy and stress tensor, may be absorbed or scattered in different ways, and may store negative interaction energy while maintaining a consistent total-energy accounting. Some field theories describe attractive interactions through exchange processes rather than absorption of ordinary positive-momentum projectiles. Those mechanisms cannot be simulated simply by changing the sign of an observed momentum impulse without specifying the corresponding energy and source response.

The next continuous-field construction therefore needs a real dynamical action or another independently justified physical conservation rule. Source coupling, field propagation speed, field energy and momentum density, receiver coupling and boundary flux must all be defined. Moving a physical source should emit or redistribute a disturbance that subsequently affects another body. All participants, including any exterior environment, must balance energy and momentum. If a field changes a photon's trajectory, it must account for its own momentum transfer and for the massive source's reaction. It should then reproduce the R31/R35 solar deflection and radio-delay constraints using a parameter frozen before a holdout dataset is opened.

In cosmic-bubble work, a physical boundary adds an additional issue. An exterior field could apply a traction to the boundary, a pressure gradient could support outward wall acceleration, and a neighboring localized mass could create a directional tidal response. These outcomes are not interchangeable. A spherical distribution of ordinary inverse-square attractions is not automatically an isotropic expansion machine. The extra coupling introduced in earlier ArcSecs external-domain studies should be tested for directional dependence and source energy, not inferred from the visual intuition of a bubble being surrounded by other bubbles.

A model that grows a boundary also does not automatically increase separations between internal galaxies or generate their spectral redshifts. The dynamics of individual interior objects, photon emission and propagation, receiver clocks and boundary forces must be connected through an actual shared interaction. No observational requirement is satisfied simply because a drawing shows a larger bubble at the next event label. The new R40 result therefore belongs alongside R39 as a computational building block, not as a substitute for UPM-0 or the original technical source reports.

## 7. Observational requirements and usable primary evidence

**Solar light deflection and spacecraft radio timing.** Bertotti, Iess and Tortora's 2003 Cassini experiment used physical radio links passing near the Sun to constrain gravitational signal behavior. A model claiming a new photon–gravity interaction must reproduce the telemetry-derived delay and its treatment of plasma and spacecraft motion; it cannot cite the conventional interpretation as the directly measured object, nor dismiss the measurement because of that interpretation. Use the primary publication and NASA's radio-science documentation rather than untraceable textbook numbers. The next ArcSecs comparison should freeze its coupling on one subset and test an untouched subset with exactly the same instrument corrections.

**Optical-clock observations.** Bothwell and colleagues' 2022 strontium experiment recorded frequency differences across a millimeter-scale atomic sample. ArcSecs can investigate physical state-dependent oscillator processes under a constant universal When. But the R40 carrier does not couple to atomic binding states; therefore it does not yet predict that frequency gradient. Presenting the toy's constant event parameter as a replacement clock mechanism would overstate the result. A separate source-coupled oscillator Hamiltonian is needed, with independent multi-species or held-out comparisons.

**Multimessenger arrival observations.** The observed gamma-ray and gravitational-wave signals associated with GW170817 arrived about 1.74 seconds apart. The exact relationship to their propagation speeds depends on source-emission timing and distance assumptions. The R40 toy has freely adjustable carrier speed; it must not be interpreted as evidence that a faster-than-light carrier has been detected or that all astrophysical signals share a universal speed. Instead, this observation gives an important condition for any carrier proposed to transmit gravitational effects. A viable physical source model must account for the jointly measured arrival times and their astrophysical uncertainties.

**Short-range force searches.** Precision torsion-balance studies by Kapner and collaborators probe departures from inverse-square attraction at submillimeter distances. An additional attractive field, if constructed, must include its finite range, effective source coupling and environmental dependence. A cosmic-scale mechanism need not have the same unscreened laboratory coupling, but screening must be an explicit independently testable property—not an adjustable escape hatch added after the experiment.

**Neighboring bubbles.** Existing bubble-search work in R09 and R33 reviews full-sky temperature and polarization observations and candidate collision morphologies. No verified neighboring-bubble interaction has been established in those records. The most constructive path is to define an independently predicted spatial or temporal signal pattern, pre-register an analysis and compare blind held-out data. Candidate astronomical anomalies must first be separated from foreground, calibration and survey-selection effects before a physical exterior source is inferred.

These measurements are not proofs of a particular ontology; they are hard numerical targets for a physical alternative. Scientific validity requires the ArcSecs program to reproduce the measurements without redefining away their uncertainties or hiding inconvenient constraints in a separate, unsearchable appendix.

## 8. Distinctive experiments and preregistered failure conditions

**Experiment A — Dynamic source and receiver.** Build a physical laboratory analog with an independently tracked emitter, finite-speed messenger, and recoil-sensitive receiver. Record source recoil, carrier flight, arrival and receiver impulse using physically synchronized instrumentation. Freeze the carrier speed and interaction cross section from calibration trials; predict the withheld receiver event times before opening the comparison data. A failure occurs if signals appear before the admissible carrier arrival or if the full source-plus-carrier-plus-receiver impulse ledger is inconsistent after measurement uncertainties are included.

**Experiment B — Attractive continuous-field candidate.** Replace the event messenger with a finite-speed classical or quantum field candidate and publish a complete action, boundary conditions, field energy and momentum, source feedback and receiver interaction. The key new test is whether the field creates an attractive response with a correctly recorded stress flux, rather than the repulsive contact response observed in this mechanical toy. The field must pass an analytic static-force control and a moving-source retarded-response control. If the field cannot supply its own momentum ledger, the claim that it mediates gravity fails at the model-construction stage.

**Experiment C — Solar light propagation holdout.** Evaluate primary radio or VLBI data with the usual instrument calibrations applied. Use one predetermined sample to fix at most one leading coupling, then publish the independent residual structure predicted for frequency, impact parameter or source-motion asymmetry. A null result bounds the extra interaction; do not re-tune the same coefficient to each source. This experiment is separate from the R40 synthetic timing exercise and should not report synthetic residuals as astronomical measurements.

**Experiment D — Exterior-domain signature.** Define a hypothetical boundary location, source motion and carrier field and propagate its influence into a population of interior receiver bodies. Predict both a directional motion pattern and a radiation pattern; state whether the interaction should be isotropic, dipolar or quadrupolar. Freeze the candidate before comparisons with sky maps or survey catalogs. An inward push, pressure difference or tide must not be renamed an attractive outward pull simply because the simulation eventually shows outward motion after additional unrelated forces.

**Experiment E — Clock, redshift and photon-speed coupling.** If the same physical field is proposed to affect oscillator rates or cumulative photon propagation, derive those couplings from the same governing model rather than appending unexplained speed factors. Separate the effects on emission frequency, propagation time, photon energy, receiver instrument response and radiation arrival-interval widths. Preserve Type Ia supernova timing, multi-frequency dispersion, and gamma-ray arrival limits as real observations. These tests are essential for connecting R40 to the larger ArcSecs book but are not complete in the present benchmark.

## 9. Limitations, uncertainty and what the study cannot support

This paper contains no telescope observations newly reduced from raw instrument archives, no laboratory work and no independent empirical detection. Its exactness is mathematical exactness of a narrow collision rule, not a statement about the microscopic world. The carrier is deliberately massive and nonrelativistic; this does not constitute evidence of nonzero intrinsic photon rest mass. Its source has a finite stored energy reservoir but is approximated as having a fixed inertial mass during each emission. The model has only one spatial coordinate, local elastic impacts and predetermined first-target assignments; it has no polarization, field radiation reaction, wave interference, quantum uncertainty, three-dimensional divergence, boundary stress, distributed-source screening or continuous gravitational potential. Multiple collisions after the first and carrier–carrier scattering lie outside the simulated window.

The phrase “finite-speed” refers only to the explicitly assigned carrier velocity. R40 has not discovered the speed of gravitational influence. Its chosen event ordering is a permitted project assumption, not a measurement of a unique cosmic synchronization. Likewise, the external body in the program is a synthetic counterfactual. Even a perfectly conserved transaction with such a body cannot establish the physical existence of its cosmological counterpart.

The model preserves observation-versus-interpretation boundaries. A published bound obtained through a specific model must retain its assumptions. An observation of photon deflection is not an intrinsic-rest-mass detection. A frequency gradient between clocks is not a direct detection of physical time as an elastic medium. A redshift measurement is not a direct measurement of a galaxy's geometric distance. Conversely, proposing a new physical mechanism does not make contrary instrument readings vanish. This is affirmative research, not selective erasure of evidence.

The next scientific papers need explicit uncertainties for inferred masses, distances, source emission delays, telescope calibration, radiative energy transfer and signal velocities. Synthetic conservation tolerances should be reported separately from those empirical uncertainties. A future continuous-field algorithm must demonstrate numerical convergence, bounded boundary flux, stability and the degree to which discrete sampling respects translational momentum. An unsuccessful numerical field scheme is a failed implementation test, not evidence that nature cannot have a physical field carrier.

## 10. Research contribution and next decision

R40 establishes one careful milestone: a physical source can change the motion of another physical body through a separately tracked carrier whose energy and momentum travel between them and whose contact happens at a later event. That construction does not require space or time to act as stretchable materials. In the paired synthetic scenarios, a moving exterior source is demonstrably distinguishable from the internal-only control after its physical messenger arrives. The effect is conditional on the hypothesized launch and collision rules and therefore cannot be counted as an observational detection of cosmic neighbors.

The scientifically important negative result is equally constructive: a conventional incoming material messenger produces the momentum-transfer sign of a push, not automatically the attractive gravitational pull sought in the greater-universe thesis. The next ArcSecs physical model must specify the interaction stress responsible for attraction, show where its energy and momentum reside at every stage, and demonstrate both a retarded source response and a reproducible weak-field deflection law. That is a precise engineering requirement rather than a return to abstract metaphysical argument.

**Recommended next report:** R41, a source-anchored continuous-field carrier with a field-energy ledger, a verified field-momentum ledger, moving exterior source, three-dimensional force pattern and an independently frozen observational discriminator. Do not relabel a force potential as a proven physical field; do not claim any external cosmic boundary has been observed. Integrate R41 into the book only after its mathematical and numerical tests pass and its actual scope is stated accurately.
