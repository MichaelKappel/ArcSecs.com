### **3\. Critical Literature Review of Relational and Mechanism-Based Gravitational Frameworks**

Evaluating mechanism-based gravity requires auditing the major classes of non-fabric proposals according to their metric ontology, causal propagation, conservation properties, and empirical equivalence to general relativity.

#### **3.1 Action-at-a-Distance and Weber-Type Relational Mechanics**

Relational particle frameworks—ranging from Wilhelm Weber's velocity-dependent direct-interaction potentials to the intrinsic dynamics of Barbour and Bertotti (1982)—eliminate absolute space and independent background time by building actions exclusively from inter-particle distances and their relational rates of change17. Barbour and Bertotti (1982) demonstrated that enforcing invariance under time-dependent Euclidean translations, rotations, and arbitrary time reparametrizations (the Leibniz group) recovers Newtonian mechanics for a zero-total-momentum, zero-total-angular-momentum universe and mirrors the Hamiltonian constraint structure of spatially closed general relativity18. Nevertheless, instantaneous direct-interaction potentials possess no independent propagating field degrees of freedom. Without retarded field mediation traveling at the speed of light, static or instantaneous relational potentials fail to explain the observed quadrupole orbital decay of the Double Pulsar (Kramer et al., 2021\)10 or the multi-messenger propagation of gravitational waves across tens of megaparsecs arriving within 1.74 seconds of gamma rays in GW170817 (Abbott et al., 2017\)21. Instantaneous action-at-a-distance models are therefore excluded by relativistic radiative observations.

#### **3.2 Retarded-Field, Scalar, and Vector Mechanisms**

Relativistic field theories on flat Minkowski space originally explored scalar and vector mediators to guarantee finite causal propagation. Gunnar Nordström's 1913 conformally coupled scalar theory respects the Weak Equivalence Principle and propagates disturbances at light speed using an effective conformally flat metric. However, because the trace of the electromagnetic stress–energy tensor vanishes identically in four dimensions, a conformally coupled scalar field does not interact with vacuum electromagnetic radiation; it predicts zero solar-limb light deflection and a retrograde Mercury perihelion shift equal to negative one-sixth of Einstein's prediction5. Conversely, Sciama (1953) proposed a Maxwell-like retarded vector model of gravity and inertia that yielded an attractive inverse-square force and an inductive inertial drag proportional to cosmic mass24. Yet in Lorentz-invariant field theory, a spin-one vector mediator produces repulsion between like mass-charges unless its kinetic energy carries the wrong sign (introducing catastrophic negative-energy ghosts), and it fails to couple consistently to the full symmetric stress–energy tensor of relativistic matter and light.

#### **3.3 Flat-Space Self-Interacting Massless Spin-Two Field Theory**

Weinberg (1965) and Deser (1970) established a foundational result directly relevant to the ArcSecs position: curved Riemannian geometry need not be assumed at the outset1. Starting strictly on flat Minkowski space with a Lorentz-invariant, massless spin-two tensor field coupled universally to the matter stress–energy tensor—and enforcing consistency with energy–momentum conservation so that the gravitational field's own energy–momentum acts as a source for itself—uniquely resums into the full nonlinear Einstein field equations (Weinberg, 1965; Deser, 1970\)1. Here, flat space is the background stage and gravity acts as a causal physical force field; curved geometry appears purely as an **effective metric** measured by rods and clocks because the spin-two mediator couples universally to all Standard Model Lagrangians1. Crucially, because its classical field equations are mathematically identical to Einstein's locally, flat-space spin-two gravity represents an **equivalent reinterpretation**, not an experimental refutation of general relativity.

#### **3.4 Teleparallel Gravity (TEGR, STEGR, and Nonlinear Extensions)**

Einstein later explored teleparallelism, replacing Riemannian curvature with spacetime torsion encoded via a tetrad frame field and a flat spin connection (Bahamonde et al., 2023\)3. As reviewed comprehensively by Bahamonde et al. (2023), the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR) frames gravity as a gauge theory of the translation group, closely analogous to Yang–Mills gauge fields, where gravitational acceleration appears as a Lorentz-like force mediated by torsion rather than geodesic motion in curved space3. A sibling construction, the Symmetric Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (STEGR), attributes gravitational dynamics to non-metricity with vanishing curvature and torsion28. Because the actions of TEGR and STEGR differ from the Einstein–Hilbert action strictly by a boundary divergence, their classical equations of motion are strictly identical to general relativity3. Consequently, TEGR is **not** experimental evidence against geometric formulations. Attempting to break equivalence via nonlinear functions of the torsion scalar introduces serious theoretical hazards: Li, Sotiriou, and Barrow (2011) proved that generic nonlinear torsion theories violate local Lorentz invariance and activate extra degrees of freedom prone to strong coupling or ghost instabilities29.

#### **3.5 Scalar–Tensor Field Theories and Brans–Dicke Machian Gravity**

Explicitly seeking a relativistic realization of Mach's principle, Brans and Dicke (1961) and Brans (1962) coupled a long-range massless scalar field sourced by cosmic matter alongside the spin-two gravitational field, promoting the local gravitational constant to a dynamical field31. In the Einstein-frame formulation, matter couples universally to an **effective metric** conformally rescaled by the scalar field; generalized nonlinear scalar–tensor models allow the scalar coupling strength to vary with field amplitude5. These frameworks remain causal and ghost-free while producing genuine, testable departures from general relativity in Shapiro time delay, solar light bending, and dipolar gravitational radiation from asymmetric compact binaries (Will, 2014; Kramer et al., 2021\)5.

#### **3.6 Emergent and Entropic Gravity Paradigms**

Jacobson (1995) showed that Einstein's field equations emerge as a thermodynamic equation of state when the Clausius heat–entropy relation is applied to local Rindler causal horizons assuming area-proportional horizon entropy and Unruh temperature35. This treats the metric as an **effective macroscopic hydrodynamic variable** rather than a fundamental quantum field, reproducing Einstein's classical equations identically without generating distinct weak-field observables35. Extending thermodynamic analogies to de Sitter space, Verlinde (2017) proposed that competing area-law and volume-law entanglement entropies generate an elastic-medium-like dark gravitational force at low accelerations near the Hubble acceleration scale37. Although designed to reproduce flat galaxy rotation curves without cold dark matter, empirical evaluations against the 175-galaxy SPARC sample by Lelli, McGaugh, and Schombert (2017) reveal systematic tension between Verlinde's predicted acceleration profiles and observed galactic kinematics5.
