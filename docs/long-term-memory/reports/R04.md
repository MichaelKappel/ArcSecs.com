## **1\. Introduction**

Determining the physical cause of cosmological redshift sits at the foundation of extragalactic astrophysics. Under the standard Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) relativistic framework, the systematic reddening of distant spectral lines stems from metric expansion of space-time, which simultaneously stretches photon wavelengths, dilates observed durations of transient astrophysical events, reduces bolometric surface brightness by the fourth power of one plus redshift, and preserves the Planckian blackbody spectrum of the cosmic microwave background7. Nevertheless, persistent empirical anomalies in modern precision cosmology—including the 4.5–5σ Hubble constant tension between local distance ladders and early-universe CMB/BAO inferences4, hints of evolving dark energy in Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument Data Release 2 (DESI DR2) analyses4, and unexpectedly bright, compact high-redshift galaxies revealed by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)7—warrant periodic, disciplined re-examination of foundational transport physics.

## **2\. Definitions and Epistemic Rubric**

To separate raw astronomical measurements from model-dependent cosmological interpretations, we fix the operational definitions and classification standards applied throughout this report.  
**Cosmological Redshift (Observable):** The dimensionless fractional wavelength shift of an identified atomic, molecular, or hyperfine spectral transition, defined directly as the ratio of observed wavelength to laboratory rest-frame emitted wavelength minus unity. This quantity is a direct spectroscopic measurement independent of expansion assumptions.  
**Luminosity Distance and Angular-Diameter Distance (Model-Linked Observables):** Luminosity distance maps intrinsic bolometric source power to observed bolometric flux through an inverse-square spherical surface area, whereas angular-diameter distance maps an intrinsic transverse physical size to its observed angular subtense7. In any metric gravity theory conserving photon number along null geodesics (the Etherington distance-duality relation), luminosity distance exceeds angular-diameter distance by the square of one plus redshift18. In a static Euclidean universe with photon energy loss and no arrival-time stretching, luminosity distance exceeds angular-diameter distance only by the square root of one plus redshift1.  
**Hybrid Physical-Redshift Fraction:** In our joint hybrid framework, one plus total observed redshift equals the product of an expanding-metric scale-factor ratio and a non-expanding physical energy-loss factor1. The hybrid fraction parameter measures the share of the local, low-redshift Hubble slope generated by physical photon energy loss rather than spatial expansion, bounded between zero (pure FLRW metric expansion) and unity (pure static tired light)1.

## **3\. Literature Review**

The hypothesis that extragalactic redshifts reflect gradual photon energy loss in static space was introduced by Fritz Zwicky (1929), who proposed a gravitational-drag mechanism shortly after Hubble's distance–redshift correlation22. To test expansion directly against static energy loss, Richard Tolman (1930) and Edwin Hubble & Richard Tolman (1935) formulated the surface-brightness dimming test, showing that bolometric surface brightness declines as the inverse fourth power of one plus redshift in an expanding universe versus the inverse first power in a static tired-light universe7.  
Subsequent multi-probe observations steadily narrowed the viable space for non-expanding models. Peebles, Schramm, Turner, & Kron (1991) synthesized light-element nucleosynthesis, CMB isotropy, and thermal spectrum constraints supporting relativistic hot big bang cosmology10. Analyzing the full COBE/FIRAS spectrophotometric dataset, Fixsen et al. (1996) established that root-mean-square departures from a pure blackbody across millimeter and submillimeter bands are below 50 parts per million of peak brightness3, and Fixsen (2009) refined the CMB monopole temperature to 2.72548 ± 0.00057 K3. Early Type Ia supernova light-curve stretching measurements by Goldhaber et al. (2001) and spectroscopic aging tests by Blondin et al. (2008) detected redshift-proportional time dilation34, recently brought to sub-percent precision by the Dark Energy Survey five-year supernova analysis (White et al. 2024\)2 and independently corroborated across 190 high-redshift quasars monitored for over two decades by Lewis & Brewer (2023)38.  
Genuine alternative transport mechanisms and empirical challenges have nevertheless remained active in the literature. Ari Brynjolfsson (2004) advanced a "plasma-redshift" interaction in hot ionized intergalactic gas designed to explain cosmological redshift without angular image blur40. More recently, a stimulated-transfer redshift model invoking ponderomotive electron recoil was compared against 124 localized Fast Radio Burst (FRB) dispersion measures in a December 2025 preprint43, while Rajendra Gupta (2023) and Gupta & Samaras (2026) developed a Covarying Coupling Constants plus Tired Light (CCC+TL) framework addressing early JWST galaxy formation ages and fitting the 175-galaxy SPARC rotation-curve catalog competitively with MOND16. On the Tolman front, whereas Lubin & Sandage (2001) concluded that passive luminosity evolution reconciled Hubble Space Telescope elliptical galaxies out to redshift 0.9 with metric expansion35, Lerner, Falomo, & Scarpa (2014) contended that rest-frame ultraviolet surface brightness to redshift 5 favored a static Euclidean universe35. Deep JWST observations from the ASTRODEEP-JWST catalog (Merlin et al. 2024\) revitalized this debate: analyses of 6,860 spectroscopically confirmed JWST galaxies out to redshift 14 reported a shallow broadband dimming exponent between minus 1.0 and minus 1.37. Crucially, however, Desmond, Yasin, Stiskalek, & von Hausegger (2026) forward-modeled the exact ASTRODEEP-JWST Kron-aperture corrections, point-spread functions, and empirical spectroscopic selection function within the IllustrisTNG-100 hydrodynamical simulation, demonstrating that intrinsic galaxy luminosity-density evolution fully accounts for the shallow JWST scaling inside standard Lambda-CDM18.  
Concurrently, landmark observational datasets published between 2017 and 2026 now enable stringent joint testing across all five benchmarks simultaneously: Planck 2018 legacy power spectra and parameters (Aghanim et al. 2020\)11, Atacama Cosmology Telescope DR6 high-multipole spectra (Calabrese et al. 2025; Louis et al. 2025\)50, South Pole Telescope SPT-3G polarization and lensing datasets (Balkenhol et al. 2023; Camphuis et al. 2025, 2026\)53, DESI DR2 baryon acoustic oscillations from over 30 million galaxies and quasars (Abdul-Karim et al. 2025\)13, DES-SN5YR and Pantheon+ supernova distance compilations (Brout et al. 2022; Abbott et al. 2024\)5, localized FRB intergalactic baryon censuses (Macquart et al. 2020\)59, high-redshift CMB temperature measurements via molecular water absorption at redshift 6.34 (Riechers et al. 2022\)21, milliarcsecond VLBI imaging of quasars above redshift 5 and 6 (Spingola et al. 2020\)20, and multimessenger gravitational-wave and gamma-ray arrival timing from GW170817 and GRB 170817A (Abbott et al. 2017\)17.

## **5\. Theoretical Derivations (Verbal Formalism)**

### **5.1 Minimal Exponential Energy-Loss Toy Law and Meaning of the Decay Coefficient**

Consider a minimal phenomenological transport law in which the rate of photon energy loss per unit proper path length is proportional to local photon energy multiplied by an attenuation coefficient that may depend on path position, photon energy, and ambient environment. For a spatially uniform, achromatic static medium, integrating this decay law yields an exponential decline of photon energy with distance. Matching the emitted-to-observed energy ratio to one plus observed redshift yields a strictly logarithmic distance–redshift relation: physical propagation distance equals the reciprocal attenuation coefficient multiplied by the natural logarithm of one plus redshift1.  
Calibrating this coefficient to the local Hubble slope fixes its numerical value to the Hubble constant divided by the speed of light: **7.28 × 10⁻²⁷ m⁻¹** (0.0002247 Mpc⁻¹, corresponding to a Hubble length of 4,450.6 Mpc) for the Planck 2018 Hubble constant of 67.36 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹1, or **7.90 × 10⁻²⁷ m⁻¹** (0.0002436 Mpc⁻¹, Hubble length 4,104.5 Mpc) for the SH0ES local distance-ladder constant of 73.04 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹1.  
Physically, the attenuation coefficient equals the product of the ambient target number density, the microscopic interaction cross section, and the average fractional energy transferred from a photon to the receiving medium per interaction. Unless derived independently from a field-theoretic Lagrangian or plasma dispersion relation, this coefficient is **strictly a phenomenological curve fit**—merely rewriting the Hubble constant as an inverse length without explaining why fractional energy loss remains identical across more than ten decades of electromagnetic frequency. Grounding this coefficient in the measured intergalactic baryon budget demonstrates the severity of the microphysical demand: localized FRB dispersion measures combined with CMB and Big Bang Nucleosynthesis baryon constraints establish a diffuse ionized intergalactic free-electron density of **0.1835 m⁻³** (using an 83% diffuse ionized baryon fraction and 0.88 electrons per baryon)1. Dividing the Planck-calibrated attenuation coefficient by this electron density demands an effective energy-loss cross section of **3.97 × 10⁻²⁶ m²**—nearly **596.6 times larger than the classical Thomson scattering cross section**, whose optical depth across an entire Hubble length is only 0.001681.

### **5.2 Microphysical Mechanism 1: Dynamical Scalar or Axion-Like Particle Field Coupling**

Our first specified microphysical candidate evaluates gauge-invariant interactions between photons and a light, dynamical dark-sector scalar or pseudoscalar field permeating intergalactic space without introducing a photon mass. Two distinct interaction channels arise in effective field theory.  
In **Mechanism 1a (Pseudoscalar Chern–Simons Coupling)**, the interaction Lagrangian couples a rolling pseudoscalar or axion-like field linearly to the contraction of the electromagnetic field tensor with its dual, governed by a dimensionful inverse-energy coupling constant. Solving the modified Maxwell wave equations in a slowly varying homogeneous scalar background shows that the field's time derivative shifts the photon dispersion relation with **opposite signs** for left-handed and right-handed circular polarizations66. Instead of producing an achromatic, polarization-independent redshift, a rolling pseudoscalar rotates the plane of linear polarization—generating **cosmic birefringence**—while shifting circular polarization frequencies symmetrically upward and downward in inverse proportion to photon frequency1. Using the tightest joint WMAP and Planck isotropic cosmic birefringence constraint of 0.342° (+0.094° / −0.091° at 68% confidence) to cap the total scalar field excursion over a Hubble time67, the maximum fractional frequency shift is **1.46 × 10⁻³⁰** at 21-cm radio wavelengths, **1.30 × 10⁻³²** at the 160 GHz CMB peak, and **3.46 × 10⁻³⁶** at 500 nm optical light1. This mechanism fails by more than 30 orders of magnitude, splits spectral lines by polarization, and is strongly chromatic.  
In **Mechanism 1b (Inelastic Photon–Scalar Dark-Matter Scattering)**, photons lose energy through tree-level two-to-two scattering off a cosmic background of ultralight scalar or axion-like dark-matter quanta, transferring four-momentum directly to recoiling dark-sector particles. Dimensional analysis of the dimension-five vertex shows that the tree-level inelastic scattering cross section scales with the fourth power of the photon–scalar coupling constant multiplied by the square of the photon energy (divided by sixteen pi in natural units)1. Adopting the most restrictive direct experimental helioscope limit from the 2024 extended CAST Xenon-Micromegas run—coupling below **5.8 × 10⁻¹¹ GeV⁻¹** at 95% confidence for masses below 0.02 eV6 (and noting the even tighter astrophysical bound below **4.0 × 10⁻¹² GeV⁻¹** at 99% confidence from 1,038 days of Markarian 421 very-high-energy gamma-ray observations69)—together with the cosmic mean dark-matter density of 1.27 × 10⁻⁶ GeV cm⁻³ at a benchmark ultralight mass of 10⁻²² eV yields an optical scattering cross section of **5.39 × 10⁻⁹² m²**1. Even at maximal ultralight occupation density, the resulting optical interaction rate is only **9.38 × 10⁻³⁵** of the required Hubble attenuation coefficient—a 34-order-of-magnitude shortfall1. Furthermore, quadratic energy scaling induces extreme chromaticity, while recoil off ultralight targets imparts large energy losses and deflection angles per collision, obliterating narrow spectral lines and sharp images.  
A third scalar variant—a CP-even dilaton coupled to the squared electromagnetic field strength—effectively alters the fine-structure constant across cosmic time rather than draining photon energy along a static path. High-resolution comparisons between hyperfine 21-cm radio lines and ultraviolet metal lines in cosmological damped Lyman-alpha absorbers strictly constrain differential shifts across five decades of photon energy (Tzanavaris et al. 2007\)70, excluding a dilaton-driven static redshift.

### **5.3 Microphysical Mechanism 2: Collective Intergalactic Plasma (Plasmon) Excitations**

Our second specified microphysical candidate analyzes photon propagation through the diffuse, ionized intergalactic medium (IGM), testing whether collective plasma excitations (Langmuir plasmons) or ponderomotive stimulated-transfer scatterings can mediate continuous achromatic photon energy loss—a physical class encompassing plasma-redshift proposals (Brynjolfsson 200440) and stimulated-transfer redshift models43.  
At the FRB-calibrated diffuse IGM free-electron density of 0.1835 m⁻³ and canonical photoionized temperature of 10,000 K, the intergalactic plasma possesses an angular plasma frequency of 24.17 rad s⁻¹ (linear frequency 3.85 Hz), a plasmon quantum energy of **1.591 × 10⁻¹⁴ eV**, an electron thermal speed of 389.3 km s⁻¹ (0.130% of light speed), and a Debye screening length of 16.11 km1. Applying four-momentum conservation, radiative-transfer kinetics, and global energy conservation yields two decisive barriers.  
First, in a homogeneous, isotropic plasma, three-wave decay of a transverse photon into a lower-energy photon plus a longitudinal Langmuir plasmon must simultaneously conserve energy and three-momentum. Because longitudinal modes suffer strong Landau damping near the inverse Debye length, undamped thermal plasmons carry typical wavenumbers near one-third of the ratio of plasma frequency to thermal speed (roughly 2.07 × 10⁻⁵ m⁻¹)1. Reaching redshift unity (50% photon energy loss) demands **1.85 × 10⁸** discrete plasmon emissions for a 21-cm radio photon, **2.08 × 10¹⁰** for a 160 GHz CMB photon, **7.79 × 10¹³** for a 500 nm optical photon, and **3.14 × 10¹⁸** for a 100 keV gamma-ray photon1. Solving the Boltzmann–Fokker–Planck radiative-transfer equation for photon occupation shows two unavoidable stochastic consequences: Poisson shot-noise energy diffusion broadens 21-cm lines by a fractional width of 7.36 × 10⁻⁵ (roughly 22 km s⁻¹), directly contradicting observed high-redshift 21-cm absorption profiles exhibiting velocity dispersions down to **1.5 km s⁻¹**1; and cumulative transverse momentum kicks drive a two-dimensional angular random walk that blurs point sources at redshift unity to a root-mean-square radius of **1,950 arcseconds** (over half a degree) at 21-cm radio, **184 arcseconds** at 160 GHz, and **3.00 arcseconds** at 500 nm optical1. Invoking stimulated forward emission to suppress angular recoil (as proposed in ponderomotive stimulated-transfer models43) runs into a kinematic wall: longitudinal modes collinear with the photon light cone require phase velocity equal to the speed of light, where thermal plasma coupling vanishes, while the required cross section (2.735 × 10⁻²⁶ to 3.97 × 10⁻²⁶ m²1) still exceeds the classical Thomson cross section by factors of 400 to 600\.  
Second, energy conservation dictates that every joule drained from redshifting photons enters the receiving plasma electrons, generating entropy as ordered radiation degrades into heat. Today, the CMB carries an energy density of **0.2606 eV cm⁻³**, and the integrated extragalactic background light (EBL, roughly 65 nW m⁻² sr⁻¹ across optical and infrared wavelengths, consistent with the 32.0 ± 7.6 nW m⁻² sr⁻¹ infrared peak alone1) contributes another **0.0170 eV cm⁻³**1. By contrast, the entire thermal energy density of diffuse IGM plasma at 10,000 K is merely **4.74 × 10⁻⁷ eV cm⁻³**1. The CMB radiation reservoir outweighs the IGM thermal reservoir by a factor of **549,000**, and the EBL alone outweighs it by **35,900**1. Consequently, depositing lost CMB energy into IGM electrons at the Hubble rate doubles IGM thermal energy in just **26,000 years (0.026 Myr)**, driving uncooled gas temperatures toward 5.5 billion Kelvin (or 360 million Kelvin within 0.40 Myr from starlight EBL alone)1. Such runaway heating would ionize away the neutral hydrogen Lyman-alpha forest and flood the sky with hard X-ray bremsstrahlung radiation far brighter than observed cosmic X-ray backgrounds.

### **5.4 Radiative-Transfer Analysis of Transient Non-Stretching in Static Media**

A persistent misconception in non-expanding cosmology is that lowering individual photon frequencies automatically stretches macroscopic supernova light curves. Wave-crest conservation in stationary radiative transfer demonstrates mathematically why this fails under any time-translation-invariant medium. Consider two discrete light pulses marking the rise and decline of a distant supernova emitted at fixed coordinate distance separated by an intrinsic emission duration. If the intervening medium—whether vacuum, stationary scalar field, or steady-state plasma—is time-translation invariant, then every infinitesimal segment along the path has a time-independent refractive index and energy-loss rate.  
Consequently, the second pulse traverses the exact same optical path length and accumulates the exact same total group travel time as the first pulse, regardless of how much energy individual photons lose en route. Subtracting the first arrival time from the second cancels the identical transit time: **observed duration equals emitted duration**, fixing the time-dilation power-law exponent strictly to zero. Stretching macroscopic transient intervals in a non-expanding space would require either a monotonically increasing cosmic refractive index over time—violating staticity and forcing light speed or plasma density to drift by order unity over a Hubble time in conflict with GW170817 multimessenger timing17—or severe chromatic group-velocity dispersion, which would stretch supernovae differently across filter wavelengths in conflict with the achromaticity measured across four DECam photometric bands by the Dark Energy Survey37.

### **5.5 Bolometric and Broadband Tolman Surface-Brightness Scaling**

In any relativistic expanding universe conserving photon number, bolometric surface brightness declines as the **inverse fourth power** of one plus redshift: one power from photon energy loss, one from time dilation of the photon arrival rate, and two from the geometric divergence between angular-diameter and luminosity distance7. For fixed observer-frame broadband specific flux (per unit frequency) observing a galaxy whose rest-frame spectral energy distribution follows a power law with spectral index around 0.30, bandwidth compression removes one power of one plus redshift, leaving a non-evolving expanding-universe broadband exponent of minus 3.301. Including physically evolving galaxy projected luminosity density scaling positively with one plus redshift to an evolution exponent shifts the predicted broadband slope upward by that exact evolution exponent18.  
In a static Euclidean tired-light universe without time dilation, angular-diameter distance equals physical distance and photon arrival rate is unchanged, so bolometric surface brightness falls only as the **inverse first power** of one plus redshift1. Applying photon-number conservation to a static beam per unit frequency shows that the ratio of frequency bandwidth to photon energy remains invariant under exponential energy decay: in fixed observer-frame specific intensity per unit frequency, non-evolving static tired light scales only as one plus redshift raised to minus the spectral index (roughly minus 0.30), and adding positive galaxy luminosity-density evolution causes high-redshift galaxies to brighten rapidly in fixed-band specific surface brightness1.

### **5.6 Radiative Transfer of the CMB Blackbody Spectrum Under Non-Expanding Energy Loss**

The Planckian blackbody shape of the CMB offers an exact radiative-transfer test of non-expanding photon energy loss. In an expanding universe, spatial volume grows as the cube of one plus the expansion redshift, diluting photon number density by the exact cubic factor needed to keep phase-space occupation invariant at the redshifted temperature and preserve a pure blackbody.  
If instead a portion of the CMB's redshift arises from a **non-expanding, photon-number-conserving energy-loss mechanism**, comoving photon number density is not diluted by the cube of one plus the tired-light redshift. Every photon's frequency shifts downward by one plus the tired-light redshift and frequency bandwidth compresses by the same factor, keeping photon number per fractional frequency interval constant1. Substituting the shifted frequency into the Planck distribution implies strictly under photon-number conservation that observed specific intensity becomes a **greybody spectrum multiplied by the cube of one plus the tired-light redshift** relative to a true blackbody at the shifted effective temperature1. Because photon number density now exceeds the equilibrium Planckian density corresponding to the lowered photon energies, the spectrum acquires an unavoidable amplitude excess—or, if redistributed by scattering without photon-destroying processes, a severe negative Bose–Einstein chemical-potential distortion1.

Three completely independent observational datasets were withheld from all parameter optimization and used strictly for out-of-sample predictive validation. **Holdout 1 (DES Y6 BAO)** provides an independent transverse comoving distance ratio at effective redshift 0.851 measured at **19.74 ± 0.60**19. **Holdout 2 (Lewis & Brewer 2023 Quasar Time Dilation)** analyzes 190 quasars spanning redshifts 0.2 to 4.0 monitored for over two decades, measuring a time-dilation exponent of **1.28 (+0.28 / −0.29)** (evaluated with symmetric 0.285 uncertainty) and an independent Bayes factor of **1.08 × 10⁵** (log-evidence difference \+11.59) favoring metric dilation over zero dilation1. **Holdout 3 (Riechers et al. 2022 High-Redshift CMB Temperature)** measures a CMB temperature of **23.3 ± 6.9 K** at redshift 6.34 from molecular water absorption against a dusty starburst galaxy (primary detection and citation verified in peer-reviewed sources9, with the exact 23.3 ± 6.9 K value transcribed from a secondary summary citing Riechers et al. 202221).

### **6.3 Uncertainty Propagation, Statistical Model, and Verification Protocol**

All three competing cosmologies (Flat Lambda-CDM with 2 free parameters, Static Tired Light with 1 free parameter, and Hybrid Cosmology with 3 free parameters) were forward-modeled using identical shared parameter vectors across all probes1. In the hybrid model, the local expansion rate is scaled by one minus the hybrid fraction so that the low-redshift Hubble law remains invariant across all hybrid values; for each observed redshift, numerical root-finding inverts the coupled proper-path integral to obtain the underlying expansion redshift, which then deterministically predicts transverse and line-of-sight BAO ratios, luminosity distance, effective transient time-dilation slope, broadband Tolman slope, and CMB greybody distortion1.  
**Statistical Approximation Disclosure:** Because the raw 13×13 DESI DR2 covariance matrix file was not directly ingested during this calculation, our BAO likelihood uses the published diagonal measurement uncertainties1. Official DESI DR2 documentation reports off-diagonal transverse–radial cross-correlations of 0.3 to 0.5 within each redshift bin19. Validating our diagonal BAO fit for Flat Lambda-CDM recovers a matter density of 0.2995, closely matching DESI's official full-covariance value of 0.2975 ± 0.00861; thus, while exact AIC/BIC shifts of 1–2 units require full-covariance re-verification, multi-hundred-sigma exclusions are entirely unaffected.  
To audit estimator bias, we executed a 200-realization Monte Carlo mock injection-and-recovery test (fixed random seed 20261122): 100 synthetic DESI BAO plus DES time-dilation datasets generated at zero true hybrid fraction and 100 generated at 3.0% true hybrid fraction1. The pipeline recovered mean values of **0.0011 ± 0.0111** (bias \+0.0011) for the zero injection and **0.0295 ± 0.0132** (bias −0.0005) for the 3.0% injection, confirming unbiased parameter recovery1.
