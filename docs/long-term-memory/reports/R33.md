## **Structured Abstract**

**Objective:** This independent investigation evaluates whether cosmological, large-scale structure, transient, and gravitational-wave observations publicly available through 22 November 2026 provide evidence of neighboring cosmic domains ("bubbles") or physical influences crossing a domain boundary, and establishes a blinded, trials-corrected detection protocol grounded in the eleven binding premises of the ArcSecs physical-first research charter1.  
**Methods:** We synthesize primary results and official data releases from WMAP, Planck (2015, 2018, and PR4 products), the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT DR6.02, 2025), the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI DR2, 2025–2026), the European Space Agency's Euclid DR1-Foundation release (12 November 2026), LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA fourth observing run (O4, accepted 6 November 2026), and multi-messenger transient timing bounds2. Treating published general-relativistic bubble templates strictly as operational sky-pattern baselines rather than endorsements of a spacetime medium, we compare four competing hypotheses: an isotropic stochastic baseline with foregrounds (H0), an azimuthally symmetric circular collision imprint (H1), an ArcSecs exterior-domain physical force or propagation-gradient mechanism (H2), and astrophysical or survey systematics (H3)8. We lock a blinded detection pipeline pairing Planck temperature and E-mode polarization with held-out ACT DR6 validation, calibrated via Gauss-Legendre spherical-harmonic projections and 50,000-trial Monte Carlo null simulations4.  
**Results:** Existing full-sky surveys reveal no statistically validated cosmic bubble collision has emerged from the cited data and specified detection pipelines or compact domain boundary. WMAP 7-year Bayesian searches constrain observable collisions to fewer than 4.0 at 95 percent confidence, implying an exclusion of individual linear comoving-curvature-perturbation amplitudes above approximately 3.3 × 10⁻⁴ in the Sachs-Wolfe approximation1. Planck 2015 matched-circle and isotropy analyses exclude back-to-back topological identifications smaller than 0.94 times the effective recombination distance at 99 percent confidence and constrain anisotropic global vorticity to below 7.6 × 10⁻¹⁰12. Under our precommitted global false-positive rate of 0.27 percent across 333.0 effective independent sky-and-radius trials (effective sky fraction 0.78), the required trials-corrected detection threshold is a two-degree-of-freedom chi-squared statistic of 23.44 (signal-to-noise ratio 4.84), verified empirically at 0.24 percent via Monte Carlo simulation10. An illustrative 25-degree circular collision featuring a 50 microkelvin linear ramp and a 20 microkelvin edge step yields a masked signal-to-noise ratio of 3.46, demonstrating how cosmic variance obscures moderate-amplitude collisions10. If the observed 369.82 kilometers per second cosmic microwave background dipole were driven by an exterior mass distribution over an illustrative 13.8-gigayear impulse without exceeding a conservative 1.5 × 10⁻⁵ quadrupole limit, that mass would need to lie at least 272.1 times the effective emission-shell radius away (3,809.6 gigaparsecs in standard distance scaling)10. In the gravitational-wave channel, the November 2026 LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA O4 95 percent upper limit of 2.8 × 10⁻⁹ for a scale-invariant background at 25 hertz already excludes illustrative strong first-order phase transitions peaking at that frequency, though such transitions can occur within a single domain and cannot uniquely diagnose other universes2.  
**Limitations:** Because no live observatory FITS map files were downloaded or processed locally beyond dedicated analytical and Monte Carlo calibration scripts, map-level empirical claims rest strictly on published mission constraints and verified numerical benchmarks10. Crucially, while ArcSecs frames interactions via physical mass-energy transfers within constant universal progression ("When"), the framework currently lacks a derived microphysical field equation reconciling its premise that light slows down over time with tight fast-radio-burst dispersion limits and GW170817/GRB 170817A multi-messenger timing coincidences7. Consequently, the strongest defensible scientific status for external cosmic bubbles as of 22 November 2026 is a set of **nontrivial observational upper limits**, not a detection.

## **2\. Research Question and Competing Hypotheses**

### **2.1 Central Research Question**

Can publicly available astrophysical datasets as of 22 November 2026—specifically cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature and polarization maps from Planck and ACT DR6.02, baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) catalogs from DESI DR2, strong-lensing and galaxy catalogs from Euclid DR1-Foundation, isotropic gravitational-wave background bounds from LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA (LVK) O4, and multi-messenger transient arrival delays—distinguish localized or anisotropic physical influences originating from neighboring cosmic bubbles or an exterior domain boundary from baseline isotropic fluctuations, intra-domain phase transitions, and astrophysical or instrumental systematics2?

### **2.2 Competing Operational Hypotheses**

To prevent post-hoc pattern matching on dramatic sky images, four mutually distinct hypotheses are defined prior to map inspection. Under **H0 (Baseline Statistically Isotropic Sky plus Standard Systematics)**, observed CMB temperature and E-mode polarization anisotropies follow a statistically isotropic Gaussian random field characterized by the standard six-parameter angular power spectrum fit, combined with Galactic foreground emissions, foreground gravitational lensing, kinematic observer-motion Doppler boosting, and instrument beam and noise covariances3. Under H0, no circular collision disks, anomalous large-scale tidal alignments beyond cosmic variance, or directional propagation anomalies exist.  
Under **H1 (Localized Bubble-Collision Template)**, our observable domain experienced one or more physical collisions with neighboring cosmic bubbles, imparting an azimuthally symmetric modulation onto the primordial emission shell8. Adopted strictly as a statistical search baseline from Feeney et al. (2011) and Wainwright et al. (2015), the collision pattern on the sky is bounded by a circular causal ring of angular radius around a sky center, consisting of an interior linear temperature gradient in cosine-angle and a potential step discontinuity at the edge, accompanied by a correlated E-mode polarization signature8. In ArcSecs physical terms, this template represents a localized radial momentum and energy-density impulse deposited across the emission shell rather than a deformation of a spacetime manifold.  
Under **H2 (ArcSecs Exterior-Domain Influence Mechanism)**, an asymmetric exterior mass distribution, boundary-wall pressure gradient, or inter-domain field outside our observable matter-energy region interacts across the domain boundary within constant universal T. This hypothesis predicts at least one of three measurable interior signatures: a large-scale gravitational dipole accompanied by an unavoidable tidal quadrupole gradient across the domain; a coherent directional bulk flow of galaxy clusters exceeding local density-field expectations; or a direction-dependent anomaly in photon propagation, energy loss, or distance-redshift residuals testable across DESI DR2 and multi-messenger transients10. Crucially, if the exterior region is everywhere separated by an impenetrable boundary or lies beyond physical field coupling, H2 predicts zero measurable interior signal and becomes observationally indistinguishable from H0.  
Under **H3 (Astrophysical, Foreground, and Survey Systematics Alternative)**, apparent large-angle CMB spots, circular residuals, bulk flows, or sky number-count dipoles arise from Galactic dust or synchrotron foreground leakage, asymmetric survey footprints, extinction modeling errors, catalog selection-function biases, or unmodeled instrument scanning beams3.

### **2.3 Predeclared Model-Selection Criteria and Falsification Rules**

Any candidate signature must survive a strict predeclared falsification protocol. For localized circular CMB templates (H1 versus H0), detection requires a trials-corrected two-degree-of-freedom chi-squared improvement exceeding 23.44 (corresponding to an amplitude signal-to-noise ratio above 4.84) at a precommitted global false-positive rate of 0.27 percent across all sky positions and angular radii, verified both in temperature and in an independent held-out polarization or cross-instrument split10. A candidate is immediately falsified and reclassified under H3 or noise if it vanishes under alternative Galactic foreground cleaning, fails to replicate in held-out ACT DR6 maps within overlapping sky footprints, exhibits frequency dependence inconsistent with blackbody CMB emission, or depends on post-hoc widening of the template family3. For exterior-gravity hypotheses (H2), any model attributing the observed 369.82 kilometers per second CMB dipole to an external attractor is falsified if its predicted tidal quadrupole exceeds the observed CMB quadrupole budget (bounded conservatively at a fractional temperature perturbation of 1.5 × 10⁻⁵)10.

## **3\. Literature Review and Empirical Landscape (Through 22 November 2026\)**

### **3.1 Cosmic Bubble-Collision Searches in CMB Data**

Theoretical and observational pipelines for cosmic bubble collisions matured around WMAP 7-year and Planck-era forecasts. Feeney et al. (2011) formulated a five-parameter Bayesian template search—spanning collision center coordinates, angular disk radius, interior linear ramp amplitude, and edge discontinuity—assuming azimuthal symmetry and long-wavelength modulation of the primordial emission surface1. Applying hierarchical Bayesian model selection to WMAP 7-year temperature maps, Feeney et al. (2011) found no statistical warrant to augment the baseline isotropic model, placing an upper limit of fewer than 4.0 observable collisions on the full sky at 95 percent confidence1. Subsequent optimal-filter preprocessing by McEwen et al. (2012, 2013\) identified eight candidate temperature features in WMAP 7-year data for follow-up marginalized-likelihood integration, emphasizing that candidate preselection tolerates false detections precisely because full Bayesian evidence integration suppresses noise peaks26.  
Full numerical simulations of colliding scalar-field vacuum bubbles by Johnson et al. (2012) and Wainwright et al. (2014, 2015\) linked sky templates to underlying barrier shapes and bubble separations9. Translating WMAP constraints via the Sachs-Wolfe approximation (in which temperature perturbation is roughly one-fifth of the comoving curvature perturbation), Wainwright et al. (2015) deduced a rough constraint excluding individual linear curvature-perturbation amplitudes above 3.3 × 10⁻⁴, while forecasting that combining Planck and future E-mode polarization measurements tightens parameter degeneracies because polarization traces recombination physics directly9. All published collision searches to date operate within standard cosmological parameterizations; no peer-reviewed study has yet re-evaluated Planck PR4 or ACT DR6 maps directly under an ArcSecs propagation-and-emission baseline. Full-text methodological checks of papers whose abstracts or archival summaries alone were retrieved remain an explicit scope limitation noted in Section 9.1.

### **3.2 Planck Isotropy, Matched-Circle Topology, and Peculiar Velocities**

The Planck Legacy Archive delivers the highest-precision full-sky CMB temperature and polarization benchmark currently available12. In *Planck 2018 results. I*, the collaboration confirmed 18 acoustic peaks across temperature and polarization spectra, determining five of six baseline parameters to better than 1 percent precision and the acoustic angular scale to 0.03 percent20. In *Planck 2018 results. VII* (published 2020), full-sky statistical and isotropy diagnostics revealed consistency with global isotropy across most tests, while documenting persistent large-angle features such as low-multipole suppression and localized cold regions3.  
Targeted searches for a finite or multiply connected cosmic domain—which would arise if our observable bubble were smaller than the horizon or wrapped around itself—have yielded strict null results. Using Planck 2013 intensity data, direct likelihood and circles-in-the-sky searches constrained the inscribed sphere radius of a cubic-torus fundamental domain to exceed 0.92 times the comoving recombination distance14. Updating this with *Planck 2015 results. XVIII*, which combined full-mission temperature with polarization data, found no matched circles larger than 15 degrees in angular radius, constraining topologies predicting back-to-back circles to an inscribed radius exceeding 0.94 times the recombination distance at 99 percent confidence, while bounding anisotropic Bianchi VIIh vorticity to below 7.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ at 95 percent confidence12. Recent topology analyses in 2025 and 2026 indicate that non-trivial compact domains remain observationally accessible via correlation matrices mainly when the shortest closed loop through the observer measures up to approximately 1.3 times the diameter of the last-scattering surface29.  
On large-scale bulk motions, *Planck intermediate results. XIII* (2014) used the kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) effect along galaxy clusters to test claims of an anomalous \~900 kilometers per second "dark flow"23. Linear cosmological theory predicts rms radial peculiar velocities of roughly 230 kilometers per second on galaxy-cluster scales and 50–100 kilometers per second across larger volumes; Planck XIII found that its kSZ peculiar-velocity and bulk-flow constraints are consistent with that baseline scenario rather than an unmodeled gigaparsec-scale drift23. Conversely, galaxy and quasar number-count dipole studies present an unresolved observational tension: Secrest et al. (2021, 2022\) and radio-continuum comparisons by Siewert, Schmidt-Rubart, and Schwarz (2021) reported that the mid-infrared CatWISE quasar dipole rejects the kinematic expectation inferred from Planck's 369.82 ± 0.11 kilometers per second CMB dipole at 5.1 sigma significance10. Whether that excess reflects an intrinsic large-scale anisotropy, an exterior gravitational pull, or mid-infrared selection and redshift-evolution systematics remains an active empirical question25.

### **3.3 High-Resolution CMB and Large-Scale Structure Releases (2025–2026)**

Three major observational datasets released between 2025 and November 2026 materially expand the empirical landscape for external-domain tests. First, **ACT DR6.02 (2025)**, hosted on NASA LAMBDA, provides temperature and polarization maps at 90, 150, and 220 gigahertz covering 19,000 square degrees from the 2017–2022 AdvancedACT observing seasons (Naess et al. 2025), alongside power spectra, Planck cross-correlations (Louis et al. 2025), and extended-model analyses (Calabrese et al. 2025\)4. For Mission 09, ACT DR6 provides an independent ground-based instrument and high-resolution polarization dataset capable of serving as a held-out cross-check on Planck candidates inside their shared footprint4.  
Second, **DESI DR2 (2025–2026)**, published in *Physical Review D* (Abdul-Karim et al. 2025\) and archived with 313.5 gigabytes of supplementary data on Zenodo, measures baryon acoustic oscillations across more than 14 million galaxies and quasars over three years of operations (\~42 cubic gigaparsecs), achieving 0.28 percent aggregated distance-scale precision compared with 0.49 percent in DR1, complemented by 2025–2026 Lyman-alpha forest multipole papers5. While consistent with the Planck acoustic angular scale, DESI Collaboration reports a mild 2.3 sigma tension with CMB inference under a flat constant-Lambda fit, shifting to a 3.1 sigma preference for a time-evolving dark-energy parameterization when combining DESI BAO and CMB (and 2.8 to 4.2 sigma across dataset combinations), while deriving a 95 percent upper limit on the sum of neutrino masses below 0.064 electronvolts22. Independent commentary summarizing DESI DR2 notes cautions (such as Deng Wang et al. 2025\) that internal discrepancies among combined CMB, BAO, and supernova datasets complicate joint fits40. For ArcSecs, DESI DR2 supplies a high-precision, directionally resolved catalog of angular-scale versus redshift residuals without requiring acceptance of metric-expansion dark energy.  
Third, **ESA Euclid DR1-Foundation (12 November 2026\)**, released ten days prior to this commission's cutoff, comprises 13 months of visible and near-infrared observations covering 1,900 square degrees (1.7 million gigabytes), cataloging over 900 million galaxies, around 15,000 strong gravitational lensing candidates (exceeding all prior strong-lens catalogs combined), more than 60 million quasar candidates, and spectra from over 160 million sources6. Full-survey weak-lensing shear tomography and full clustering cosmological releases remain future mission deliverables and are not assumed available as of November 20266.

### **3.4 Gravitational-Wave Backgrounds and Multi-Messenger Propagation Bounds**

On 6 November 2026, *Physical Review D* accepted the LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA (LVK) Collaboration's fourth observing run (O4) isotropic stochastic gravitational-wave background search covering data through 1 April 2025 (Abac et al. 2026\)2. Evaluated at a reference frequency of 25 hertz under a log-uniform prior, LVK O4 establishes 95 percent credible upper limits of 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ for a compact-binary power-law spectral index of two-thirds and 2.8 × 10⁻⁹ for a scale-invariant spectrum2. While space-based interferometry via LISA remains a future observatory for millihertz cosmological phase transitions (Caprini et al. 2016, 2020; Lewicki and No 2020\)15, LVK O4 directly constrains high-temperature first-order phase transitions or violent bubble-wall collisions peaking near 25 hertz2.  
Simultaneously, transient astrophysics imposes stringent empirical bounds on photon propagation and mass-related dispersion. The joint observation of gravitational waves (GW170817) and gamma-rays (GRB 170817A) from a binary neutron star merger yielded an arrival delay of (+1.74 ± 0.05) seconds over roughly 40 megaparsecs, bounding the fractional difference between the speed of gravity and the speed of light to between −3 × 10⁻¹⁵ and \+7 × 10⁻¹⁶7. Meanwhile, fast radio burst (FRB) dispersion studies constrain vacuum group-velocity delays from a nonzero invariant photon rest mass to below 5.2 × 10⁻⁴⁷ grams (FRB 150418 at redshift 0.492) and below 5.1 × 10⁻⁴⁸ grams (FRB 121102 subpulses across 1.1–1.7 gigahertz), complementing Particle Data Group (PDG) photon mass compilations and Fermi-LAT gamma-ray bounds from GRB 090510 (Vasileiou et al. 2013\)16.

## **4\. Physical Theory, ArcSecs Mechanisms, and Explicitly Missing Derivations**

### **4.1 Translating Cosmic Bubbles into Physical-First ArcSecs Terms**

Conventional eternal-inflation models describe bubble universes as true-vacuum regions expanding inside a false-vacuum de Sitter spacetime, where domain walls accelerate outward and collisions perturb the spacetime metric inside our past light cone8. Under the ArcSecs charter, that geometric ontology is rejected: space ("Where") and time ("When") are non-physical reference coordinates, not substances capable of vacuum-driven metric stretching.  
To investigate whether external bubbles could exist and be detected within ArcSecs, the boiling-pot metaphor must be replaced by physical entities occupying area and carrying mass-energy at each universal instant T (Premise 6). In an ArcSecs physical-first picture, our observable cosmos is a finite, outward-moving system of matter, radiation, and bound structures traversing spatial coordinates ("Where") relative to other distant matter-energy domains ("external bubbles"). Under Premise 9, physical constituents can separate faster than light-mediated causality without invoking metric stretching, consistent with Premise 8 ("light is not the fastest thing in the universe"). A neighboring "bubble" is therefore a distinct, bounded concentration of matter, fields, and radiation separated from our domain by either an intervening low-density void or a physical boundary shell of transitional matter-energy.

### **4.2 Three Candidate Physical Interaction Channels for H2**

If an exterior cosmic bubble or boundary interacts with our observable domain within constant universal T, energy and momentum conservation demand a physical carrier. First, a **direct kinetic boundary-wall collision** occurs if the expanding matter-energy shell of an adjacent domain intersects our domain prior to or near the epoch when the primordial plasma becomes transparent, transferring radial momentum and thermal energy into the baryon-photon fluid across a localized spherical cap. Because the intersection of two roughly spherical shells forms a circular boundary in three-dimensional coordinates ("Where"), an interior observer views an azimuthally symmetric disk of perturbed temperature and radial electron scattering (producing correlated E-mode polarization) bounded by a sharp causal ring. Thus, the Feeney et al. (2011) and Wainwright et al. (2015) circular sky template functions cleanly as an empirical target for physical momentum transfer without endorsing warped spacetime8.  
Second, an **exterior gravitational field and tidal gradient** arises if physical mass-energy outside our domain exerts gravitational attraction across space. Over an interaction duration within constant T, the uniform component of external acceleration imparts a bulk drift velocity to interior matter relative to the emission shell, appearing as a kinematic dipole in galaxy counts or CMB temperature23. However, any finite-distance external mass produces a non-uniform tidal gradient whose ratio to the uniform dipole acceleration scales inversely with distance to the external mass10. Unless the exterior attractor lies far beyond the effective radius of our observable emission shell, that tidal field imprints an anomalous quadrupole onto the CMB and large-scale shear fields10.  
Third, **field-mediated propagation drag** reflects ArcSecs Premises 1, 3, and 8: photons carry mass, lose speed over universal progression T, and travel slower than the fastest causal processes. If an exterior pressure boundary or inter-domain field permeates our region asymmetrically, inbound photons arriving from different sky directions could experience slightly different cumulative drag or energy loss, manifesting as directional residuals in BAO angular-scale versus redshift relations (testable in DESI DR2) or anomalous multi-messenger arrival delays7.

### **4.3 Explicit Statement of Currently Missing Mechanisms**

Scientific integrity requires distinguishing proposed physical mechanisms from unresolved theoretical gaps. Three critical mechanisms remain **explicitly missing** in the current ArcSecs formulation. First, **non-dispersive photon slowing and an energy sink** remain underived: if photons possess nonzero invariant rest mass and slow down over universal progression T (Premises 1 and 3), standard Proca vacuum kinematics predicts frequency-dependent arrival lags scaling with squared rest mass and inverse squared frequency10. Yet FRB 121102 subpulses restrict invariant photon rest mass below 5.1 × 10⁻⁴⁸ grams (limiting 1.1–1.7 gigahertz vacuum dispersion over 1 gigaparsec to at most 11.82 milliseconds)10, while GW170817 and GRB 170817A limit the fractional speed gap between gravitational signals and gamma-rays to parts in 10¹⁵7. If light slows down via a physical interaction rather than Proca vacuum dispersion, ArcSecs currently lacks a derived microphysical equation specifying what ambient field or particle sink absorbs lost photon momentum and energy without blurring distant galaxy images, why slowing appears achromatic across radio to gamma-ray bands, and how gamma-rays maintain arrival synchrony with gravitational waves to 10⁻¹⁵ precision7. Second, **bubble-wall microphysics and nucleation rates** lack a derived equation of state predicting expected collision counts and angular radii from ArcSecs constants; amplitude priors in H1 are therefore imported from published baselines8. Third, **a super-causal physical carrier** implied by Premises 8 and 9 has not yet been isolated experimentally in laboratory or multi-messenger observations.

## **5\. Blinded Detection Protocol, Methods, and Statistical Design**

### **5.1 Protocol Lock (Central Deliverable)**

To eliminate confirmation bias and post-hoc visual pattern hunting on noisy sky maps, we establish a precommitted, blinded two-stage detection protocol targeting **circular CMB temperature-plus-polarization collision signatures (H1 versus H0 and H3)**, paired with an auxiliary directional-residual check on DESI DR2 and CatWISE/Euclid catalogs (H2 versus H3)3. Before unblinding any real-sky coordinates, four protocol components are frozen:

> 1. **Primary Observable and Template Family:** Pixel-space and spherical-harmonic matched filtering on foreground-cleaned Planck PR4/2018 intensity maps (100, 143, and 217 gigahertz channels and SMICA, NILC, Commander, and SEVEM products) across multipoles 2 to 64, restricted strictly to the five-parameter azimuthal disk family (center sky coordinates, angular disk radius sampled across ten discrete trials from 5 degrees to 50 degrees, interior linear cosine-angle ramp amplitude, and boundary step discontinuity amplitude)8.  
> 2. **Secondary Independent Confirmation Channel:** Planck E-mode polarization maps (testing the correlated radial recombination-scattering ring) plus **held-out cross-instrument validation** in ACT DR6.02 90 and 150 gigahertz temperature and polarization maps within the 19,000-square-degree ACT footprint4.  
> 3. **Blinding Strategy:** Real-sky maps are masked outside the common Planck UT78-equivalent Galactic and point-source mask (effective sky fraction 0.78)10. During threshold calibration and completeness mapping, the pipeline operates exclusively on 1,000 simulated isotropic Gaussian skies plus synthetic injected templates; real sky coordinates and peak likelihood ratios remain locked behind a blinding hash until completeness and null-distribution checks pass3.  
> 4. **Stopping Rule:** Candidate discovery terminates once all predeclared map splits and the single-trial ACT DR6 holdout evaluation are completed; no post-hoc expansion of the template family (such as elliptical disks or multi-ring oscillatory edges) is permitted within the confirmatory test.

### **5.2 Benchmark Reproduction and Injection-Recovery Design**

Prior to unblinding, the pipeline must reproduce two published benchmarks within documented tolerances: the Planck 2018 VII large-angle temperature power spectrum and hemispherical power-asymmetry null statistic on common masks, and the Feeney et al. (2011) WMAP 7-year Bayesian exclusion of more than 4.0 full-sky collisions1. Any discrepancy exceeding 1 percent in binned power or 0.3 units in log-evidence halts unblinding until mask, beam-transfer, or pixel-window conventions are reconciled.  
For mock injection and recovery, synthetic circular collision templates spanning angular radii from 5 degrees to 50 degrees, linear ramp amplitudes from 10 to 100 microkelvin, and edge step discontinuities from 0 to 50 microkelvin are injected into simulated isotropic skies convolved with a 5-arcminute Gaussian beam and 33 microkelvin-arcminute instrumental noise, then masked to an effective sky fraction of 0.7810. Recovery completeness and parameter bias are tabulated across unmasked versus masked skies to quantify how Galactic cuts truncate disks intersecting low Galactic latitudes9.

### **5.3 Trials-Corrected Detection Threshold and Precommitted False-Positive Rate**

Scanning across hundreds of sky directions and multiple disk radii induces a severe look-elsewhere penalty10. We precommit to a **global family-wise false-positive rate (FPR) of 0.27 percent (0.0027)**—equivalent to a three-sigma one-sided Gaussian tail probability across the entire masked sky and radius search space10.  
For a representative 25-degree collision disk on an effective sky fraction of 0.78, the ratio of masked sky solid angle to disk solid angle defines approximately 16.65 non-overlapping sky patches; scanning across 10 angular-radius trials and applying a conservative factor of 2.0 for two-degree-of-freedom (step plus ramp) spatial oversampling yields **333.0 effective independent trials**10. Maintaining a global FPR of 0.0027 across 333.0 independent trials requires a per-trial p-value threshold of **8.119 × 10⁻⁶**, corresponding to a two-degree-of-freedom chi-squared threshold of **23.44** and an amplitude signal-to-noise threshold of **SNR ≥ 4.84**10.

### **5.4 Adversarial Controls and Held-Out Validation**

Any candidate exceeding SNR 4.84 in the primary Planck temperature pipeline must survive five sequential adversarial checks before being classified as a physical anomaly rather than H3 systematics:

> 1. **Component-Separation Invariance:** Recovered amplitude and location must remain consistent within 1-sigma parameter uncertainty across SMICA, NILC, Commander, and SEVEM cleaning variants3.  
> 2. **Frequency-Split Consistency:** Difference maps (100 minus 143 gigahertz and 143 minus 217 gigahertz) must yield null residuals at the candidate coordinates, ruling out Galactic dust, carbon monoxide emission, or synchrotron leakage.  
> 3. **E-Mode Polarization Confirmation:** Joint temperature-plus-polarization likelihood must strengthen or remain consistent with the predicted polarization-to-temperature amplitude ratio; intensity-only features with zero correlated polarization response are penalized9.  
> 4. **Hemispherical and Rotation Nulls:** Rotating the template across 1,000 random orientations on the masked sky must confirm that the candidate lies within the upper 0.27 percent tail of the empirical sky noise distribution10.  
> 5. **Held-Out Instrument Confirmation (ACT DR6):** For candidates inside ACT DR6's 19,000-square-degree survey footprint, the exact frozen coordinates and radius from Planck are evaluated once—without refitting center or radius—on ACT DR6 90 and 150 gigahertz maps4. Because no spatial scan occurs in the held-out footprint, a single-trial confirmation threshold of SNR ≥ 3.0 with matching polarity provides decisive out-of-sample validation.

## **8\. Explicit Answers to the Seven Mission 09 Questions**

> 1. **What observable today has a uniquely calculated signature of another cosmic bubble or external boundary influence?** Currently, **no observable provides a uniquely diagnostic signature** free of alternative intra-domain or systematic explanations. The closest mathematically well-defined candidate is an **azimuthally symmetric circular temperature modulation paired with an E-mode polarization ring** on the CMB sky (H1), as parameterized by Feeney et al. (2011) and Wainwright et al. (2015)8. Even there, moderate collisions fall beneath cosmic variance, and localized foreground residuals or secondary lensing must be ruled out via frequency splits and held-out cross-instrument checks3.  
> 2. **Which current observations already place nontrivial upper limits on the hypothesis?** Four datasets impose quantitative constraints: **WMAP 7-year Bayesian searches** limit full-sky observable bubble collisions to fewer than 4.0 at 95 percent confidence and exclude linear curvature-perturbation amplitudes above roughly 3.3 × 10⁻⁴9; **Planck 2015 and 2018** isotropy and matched-circle analyses constrain compact topological domains to exceed 0.94 times the recombination radius (99 percent confidence) and Bianchi vorticity to below 7.6 × 10⁻¹⁰3; **Planck kSZ peculiar-velocity bounds** remain consistent with baseline expectations (50–100 kilometers per second linear-theory bulk rms), disfavoring \~900 kilometers per second dark-flow scenarios23; and **LVK O4 (accepted November 2026\)** bounds the 25-hertz isotropic stochastic gravitational-wave background to 2.8 × 10⁻⁹ (scale-invariant) and 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ (index 2/3)2.  
> 3. **How does the signal differ from foregrounds, random statistical fluctuations, and first-order transitions within our own universe?** A genuine bubble collision differs from Galactic foregrounds by obeying a blackbody frequency spectrum across 90–220 gigahertz (consistent between Planck and ACT DR6 component splits) and differs from random Gaussian sky fluctuations by combining spatial sharpness (a causal circular boundary) with correlated radial E-mode polarization4. Conversely, **stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds do not distinguish inter-bubble collisions from intra-domain physics**: first-order phase transitions occurring entirely within our own cosmic domain generate sound-wave and wall-collision spectra in the same frequency bands10.  
> 4. **Does the model's outside environment causally influence the observable interior; if so how?** Under H1 (boundary collision) and H2 (exterior gravitational pull or field gradient), causal influence is possible **only if** a physical mass-energy boundary intersected our domain in the past or a long-range gravitational or pressure field couples across the boundary within constant universal T. If the exterior environment lies beyond physical field coupling or separates faster than causal signal propagation (ArcSecs Premise 9), H2 produces **zero measurable interior signature** and is experimentally inaccessible.  
> 5. **Is the strongest claim "detection," "upper limit," "theoretical possibility," or "not experimentally accessible"?** As of 22 November 2026, the strongest scientifically defensible claim is **upper limit** for observable CMB collisions, compact topological boundaries, cluster bulk flows, and 25-hertz gravitational-wave backgrounds2—and **theoretical possibility awaiting missing microphysics** for ArcSecs-specific directional photon-slowing effects.  
> 6. **What exact independent observation could convert a tentative candidate into convincing evidence?** For a circular CMB candidate identified in Planck temperature maps at SNR ≥ 4.84 (global FPR ≤ 0.27 percent), convincing confirmation requires **independent, single-trial recovery (SNR ≥ 3.0) with consistent polarity, angular radius, and predicted E-mode polarization amplitude in held-out ACT DR6 90 and 150 gigahertz maps**, surviving all frequency-difference and foreground-cleaning controls4. ### **10.8 Five Rigorous Referee Objections and Data-Grounded Responses (REFEREE\_CHALLENGES.md)**

Consequently, we explicitly classify a non-dispersive, energy-sink mechanism for secular light slowing as an **unsolved missing mechanism** (Section 4.3), separating the uncontested fact that photons carry momentum and energy from any unverified claim of large invariant rest mass.  
> 2. **Referee Challenge 2 (Tidal Quadrupole Obstruction to Exterior-Gravity Dipoles):** *If an external cosmic bubble pulls our domain gravitationally to explain the 5.1 sigma CatWISE dipole or the 369.82 kilometers per second CMB dipole, would its tidal field exceed the observed low CMB quadrupole?* **Response:** Yes, unless the attractor is extremely distant relative to the domain radius. Our executed tidal calculation shows that generating a 369.82 kilometers per second bulk velocity over an illustrative 13.8-gigayear impulse requires an acceleration of 8.492 × 10⁻¹³ meters per second squared, forcing any external point-like attractor to lie at least 272.1 times the emission-shell radius away (3,809.6 gigaparsecs in standard scaling) to keep the tidal quadrupole under 1.5 × 10⁻⁵10. Furthermore, Planck XIII kSZ cluster measurements remain consistent with baseline expectations rather than an anomalous \~900 kilometers per second bulk flow23. Thus, H2 must treat exterior tidal gravity as tightly bounded and test CatWISE selection systematics (H3) before invoking an external bubble25.  
> 3. **Referee Challenge 3 (Reusing General-Relativistic Bubble Templates Without Relativistic Spacetime):** *How can the protocol adopt the Feeney et al. (2011) and Wainwright et al. (2015) circular CMB templates if ArcSecs rejects spacetime curvature and inflation?* **Response:** The template enters strictly as an **azimuthally symmetric sky-pattern basis function**—a circular disk containing a linear cosine-angle ramp plus a boundary step8. Any radially symmetric physical collision between two spherical matter-energy shells in flat three-dimensional coordinates ("Where") produces a circular intersection boundary and a smooth radial intensity profile across the shocked emission cap. Using published spherical-harmonic projections as statistical search baselines does not endorse de Sitter metric stretching.  
> 4. **Referee Challenge 4 (Non-Uniqueness of Stochastic Gravitational Waves):** *Could a future detection of a stochastic gravitational-wave background by LVK O5 or LISA prove the existence of colliding cosmic bubbles?* **Response:** No, and our protocol explicitly forbids any such claim. As demonstrated in Section 6.2 using Caprini et al. (2020) sound-wave scalings and LVK O4 bounds2, first-order cosmological phase transitions occurring entirely within a single domain generate stochastic backgrounds across the exact same frequency bands. Only spatially resolved, multi-messenger directional or circular CMB-correlated signatures could differentiate external-domain collisions from intra-domain phase transitions.  
> 5. **Referee Challenge 5 (Look-Elsewhere Penalty and Unexecuted Full-Sky Search):** *Isn't proposing a five-parameter circular search prone to false discoveries on noisy CMB maps, and hasn't the actual Planck and ACT map search remained unexecuted here?* **Response:** Both points are explicitly addressed. We precommitted to a 0.27 percent global false-positive rate across 333.0 effective sky-and-radius trials, raising the detection threshold from naive single-trial significance to SNR ≥ 4.84 (chi-squared ≥ 23.44), verified at 0.24 percent over 50,000 simulated skies10, and mandated single-trial held-out ACT DR6 confirmation4. Because raw FITS maps could not be downloaded in this tool-free environment, we transparently report no new map-level detection and list full pipeline execution on Planck PR4 and ACT DR6 as the first concrete step in Section 9.3.

## ArcSecs interpretation-versus-observation checkpoint (editorial addition, R33)

- **Observed or calibrated quantities:** CMB maps, polarization and gravitational-wave backgrounds are measured; no neighboring bubble has been confirmed.
- **What the observation does not determine by itself:** It does not identify a unique fundamental physical ontology or interaction mechanism; experimental limits must remain explicitly labeled as limits.
- **ArcSecs mechanism-building task:** Define field-collision patterns and external gravitational/pressure fingerprints, with blind null tests and false-positive controls.
- **Reproducible comparison / sources:** WMAP bubble-collision Bayesian analysis, Planck and ACT CMB products, LVK backgrounds.
- **Falsification rule:** Retain every published measured quantity and uncertainty. If a specified ArcSecs mechanism fails that benchmark, record the failing assumption and design a more complete physical mechanism or a discriminating observation; do not erase or recast the measurement as a successful fit.
- **Scientific release status:** Research working edition. Source bibliography, numerical calculations, author attribution and any claimed original simulations require independent scientific verification before journal submission.
