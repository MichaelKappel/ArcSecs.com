## Central argument

The dependence of inertia on the cosmic mass distribution is a powerful research question, but the claim of near-zero inertia in void centers must be calculated rather than assumed.

## The proposed coefficient

The source report introduces a dimensionless Weber–Machian inertia coefficient β derived from an integral of distant mass divided by separation. Such a coefficient is a *model assumption*. It does not establish that standard inertial mass disappears or that β approaches zero inside an observed large-scale void.

## Global matter does not vanish at the void center

A local low-density region remains surrounded by galaxies, filaments, clusters, and more distant cosmic matter. An integral over the full mass distribution need not shrink drastically when local density changes. The original idea of canceling scarcity against inertia collapse can only work if an explicit integration and independent limits demonstrate that the two terms have the required scaling.

## The scarcity paradox must stay visible

A field scoop with a large geometric aperture is not guaranteed to capture sparse particles. Effective cross-section depends on real interaction physics. ṁ=ηρAv decreases with density unless other factors compensate; when density tends toward zero and η and A are finite, the intake also tends toward zero.

## What would count as a discovery

Create a realistic cosmic-void matter profile and calculate β at many positions. Separately compute intake density, drag, thermal load, and thrust. Derive the claimed acceleration with proper units and stability conditions, then propose an experiment that tests inertia changes in environments of different gravitational potential.

## Travel phases as simulations

The source report divides travel into departure, void-center, and arrival phases. Retain these as modeled scenarios, not confirmed operational phases. For every phase show energy, mass, force, acceleration, and uncertainty so a dramatic peak in velocity cannot arise from parameter clipping or a singular denominator.

## Proposed decisive tests

1. Integrate β over a nonlocal mass distribution rather than locally approximating zero.
2. Check β remains finite and positive under physical conditions.
3. Demonstrate intake force and capture efficiency with lower density.
4. Validate conservation and numerical stability around the midpoint.
