# Substantive review of the ArcSecs consolidated scientific paper

**Source:** *ArcSecs: A Physical-Process Research Program for Photon Propagation, Cosmic Distance, and Relational Inertia*, Parts I–XXVIII, review edition 1.0, November 23, 2026, 22 pages. The exact uploaded PDF is preserved beside this review as `source-paper.pdf`. This is an independent internal technical review, not external peer review or a completed observational analysis. The book edition is based on the cleaned 30Q master, not an older export.

## Executive judgment

The manuscript succeeds as a research-program synthesis because it separates environmental constitutive response, photon energy transport, local atomic calibration, physical recession, and astronomical inference. It is candid about negative results. The highest-value contribution is C-01, the proposal that matter in a cosmic domain establishes a local emission-speed ceiling. The best-supported new result is the restricted Part XXVIII drag obstruction, rather than the proposed ultimate mechanism. Neither is an observational discovery. The paper is ready for technical discussion after revisions, not ready to be represented as an alternative cosmology that matches observations.

**Recommended editorial decision:** integrate the conceptual distinctions and verified equations into the book, retain the unmodified source PDF, and preserve the empirical failure gates. The previous 30Q program of dynamically coupling a three-dimensional scalar, charges and propagating vector modes remains unresolved. This intake is a cross-scale bridge rather than completion of Candidate A-05j-E.

## Section-by-section review

### Abstract and research charter (pp. 2–4)

The abstract correctly distinguishes premises, candidate equations, conditional analytical results, numerical checks, and empirical observations. Preserve this evidence hierarchy prominently in the book. Retain the authoritative wording in `.uai/totem.uai` rather than importing slight paraphrases from the paper. Foundation 9 must retain separate definitions of separation rate and information propagation. Foundation 12 is a target for independently constrained inference, not permission to multiply all distances by an adjustable factor. The age and size of a hypothetical larger cosmos are distinct from the age and size estimates of the observable region. An ontology in which coordinates are labels is not a mathematical refutation of relativity; reproduce verified observations using any proposed replacement dynamics.

The review reports 38 original source documents (15 standalone and 23 bubble reports), whereas the current book preserves 41 historical reports R01–R41, including derived investigations. These count different lineages and cannot be combined into 79 reports. There is no demonstrated one-to-one ID mapping between paper Parts and book chapters, and no new R42 is invented. The PDF's referenced companion scripts were not part of this upload; only the supplied PDF is newly archived. Earlier reported results remain attributed rather than represented as independently rerun.

### C-01 and the two-spacecraft experiment (pp. 4–5)

Retain the clarified intention. A coordinate closing rate, front speed, group velocity, source emission convention, and maximum characteristic of all interacting sectors are separate. The Galilean 4c example assumes a velocity addition rule incompatible with simultaneously imposing a common-frame photon ceiling of c. It is a thought experiment about the source of the ceiling, not evidence for superluminal messages. A zero accelerometer reading is not evidence of zero velocity; the proposed preferred ordering requires an operational clock and frame model. Sciama's historical proposal provides motivation, not an independent derivation of C-01.

A constitutive law can assign a characteristic to the electromagnetic sector. It cannot explain why every atom emits exactly at that characteristic when a positive fixed mass gap makes every finite-frequency group speed strictly smaller. Preserve the exact target and mark the shortfall rather than quietly replace packet speed with front speed. The local numerical SI value of c is a unit definition; a physical test must predict dimensionless ratios or independently measured delays and clocks. A matter-sourced environmental field differs from a gravitational potential with arbitrary zero. To turn the field into a law of mechanical inertia, specify a matter kinetic term, its coupling and reaction momentum.

### Field theory, screening, and clock standards (pp. 5–7)

The quadratic screened equation has range sqrt(S/(m_s^2+g rho)); a finite body with radius much smaller than that range need not approach its interior equilibrium. The stated solar and halo estimates are inherited model-specific results, not rerun here. The common-field spectroscopy and bending failures should be retained. A field coupled to electromagnetic coefficients generally changes ray trajectories as well as frequencies. A static nonuniform refractive environment conserves frequency for a time-independent Hamiltonian, even when group speed changes.

The local massive-vector dispersion is consistent with Part XIX and chapters 30L–30Q. A nonzero gap does not by itself establish invariant photon rest mass in a variable medium: holding gap constant while C varies changes the proxy hbar Omega/C^2. A physical model must specify which quantity is fundamental. Equal local dispersion does not imply equal transmission, as 30Q's longitudinal boundary result demonstrates. The range link applies only to the matched Stueckelberg coefficients, not every conceivable mass mechanism.

The quoted hydrogen 1S–2S Yukawa leading fractional correction is correct in the nonrelativistic fixed-Coulomb-strength, fixed-reduced-mass, large-range limit. Expanding V=-K exp(-r/ell)/r gives -K/r+K/ell-K r/(2 ell^2)+... . The constant cancels between levels; using <r>_1S=1.5 a_B, <r>_2S=6 a_B, transition energy 3 K/(8 a_B), gives -6(a_B/ell)^2. This is distinct from the 1s–2p example in chapter 30M. It is not a precision bound without nuclear, QED and experimental uncertainties and a common parameter calibration. Clock phase N_j=integral nu_j dT is an operational representation, not a derivation of physical rates. Include nuclear, electronic, cavity, and unstable-particle clocks; the preferred-time proposal must predict their ratios and velocity dependence.

### Recession and autonomous dynamics (pp. 7–9)

The decomposition of phase rate at a moving receiver into omega minus k dot v is useful. Recession, environmental transport, and source/receiver calibration must be evaluated from the same solution. The low-z slope fixing H+gamma does not independently constrain either rate. The paper fairly records the finite-distance pole and the bounded model's high-redshift distance and drift conflict. Part XXI–XXV autonomous clump and scalar examples have not been rerun here; their values must not be confused with observed galaxy properties. In particular, a weak-probe ray does not contribute finite-amplitude radiation backreaction. The 22% planar bundle spreading in Figure 1's source calculation cannot establish a three-dimensional luminosity distance. The book's existing 3D numerical work complements these results but has not solved all these physical tasks together.

### Distances, sizes, brightness and optical geometry (pp. 8–11)

The 1 Mpc example is arithmetically correct: in an exponential speed-aging model ending at half the initial speed, inferred constant-speed distance divided by actual distance is 2 ln 2. A measured travel time is essential to that inference; astronomical photons normally have no known emission timestamp. In a globally evolving medium the sign of direct travel-time bias can reverse. Separate angular, luminosity, geometric and trajectory distances.

The table on pp. 9–10 is an important negative result: the listed static tired-light and bounded-propagation alternatives yield larger angular distances than the selected conventional model at all listed redshifts. In particular at z=3 the quoted factors are 3.74 and 2.02. No evidence for smaller high-z objects follows from these laws. A correction to a distance-sensitive stellar radius or quasar virial estimate is a conditional scaling, not an actual revised estimate. Preserve the independent LMC eclipsing-binary anchor and model its observables before changing distance. The published value is 49.59 +/- 0.09 statistical +/- 0.54 systematic kpc, confirmed against Pietrzynski et al. (2019). The 66-billion-solar-mass TON 618 number is explicitly an illustration, not newly checked catalog evidence.

The two-direction optical geometry is a major improvement. Under consistent endpoint units, photon conservation apart from survival fraction and an isotropic source, forward area distance D_G enters flux; reverse angular diameter distance D_A enters inferred size. The separate factors Z_E (energy), A (arrival stretch), and transmission T must all be predicted. Generalized eta=(D_G/D_A)*sqrt(Z_E A/T)/(1+z)^2. At z=1 and D_G=D_A, Z_E=A=2, T=.16 gives eta=1.25; this agrees with the paper. If D_A is 0.8 of the reference and one insists on reference D_L, duality changes by 25%. That is an algebraic incompatibility with matching all three reference quantities under the stated assumptions, not proof that arbitrary metric or refractive theories cannot fit raw observations. Real nonstationary, dispersive systems may require frequency-dependent geometric maps, bandwidth and clock-unit conversions, and ray-dependent survival. Figure 2 (p. 11) demonstrates reciprocity for its particular static paraxial lens, not a general theorem for the proposed evolving medium. The claimed 28.7% refractive contrast and ppm defocusing are inherited values, not independently recomputed. Plan an independent 3D forward/reverse ray bundle and spectral transport model before any distance fit.

### Cosmic regulator, shell and void (pp. 12–14)

The Yukawa Green function follows the specified elliptic operator and its angularly integrated shell profile is correct. The static solution assumes a fixed source and boundary conditions; it is not instantaneous communication. The center normalization alpha I(0)^2=1 implies C_*=sqrt(2)c_0 (confirmed visually in the source, not 2c_0 as a plain-text extraction might suggest). Different shell range cases use different normalizations; holding alpha, g, M and R fixed would not automatically keep alpha I(0)^2=1 when lambda varies. A causal retarded wave equation with speed C_I is a separate proposed completion; it requires its own source backreaction and energy balance. A nonzero field in a hollow shell is not an observed cosmic boundary, and a finite-range gradient is generally nonzero away from the center.

The six range cases reproduce independently against angular quadrature at five radii each. At q=1, C(R)/c_0=0.916487, C(1.5R)/c_0=1.277330, C(2R)/c_0=1.382287. The plot is conditional and not calibrated. The uniform-background void kernel fraction 1-(1+s)exp(-s) follows from integrating r exp(-r/lambda). A static positive-mass packet of fixed frequency accelerates as it exits into a higher characteristic-speed region. The speed-regulator law therefore fails strict aging when applied alone. Do not infer a universe edge or exterior faster light from a normalized shell example. The mechanical inertia law is still missing.

### Photon aging, timing and irreversible loss (pp. 14–17)

The new Part XXVIII equations pass independent analytical and numerical checks. For C and E_m fixed and dp/dT=-gamma p, the finite-distance limit and exact distance formula are correct. The stable expression for 1-v/C avoids catastrophic floating-point cancellation, so tiny numbers are calculable even when ordinary floating-point speed rounds to C. At epsilon=1e-8 and z=1, the arrival deficit is 2e-16; the energy loss is 50%. For epsilon=1e-16 the deficit is 2e-32. This is deficit from the characteristic, not change relative to emission speed (which is smaller by the initial deficit). Both results make the same physical point. Redshift here denotes energy ratio under identical stable endpoint standards, not the general spectroscopic observable.

For a homogeneous stationary medium, identical equal-energy packets between fixed endpoints have equal flight ages, hence dT_a/dT_e=1. Dispersion may broaden a burst with different energies, but does not by itself create universal achromatic stretching. The measured DES exponent b=1.003 +/- .005 statistical +/- .010 systematic, for 1504 supernovae over about z=0.1–1.2, is verified against the published abstract. The stationary identical-source age-only subclass misses the observed trend. This is not a likelihood rejection of every alternative; source evolution and detector bandpasses must be modeled.

A further caution: an exactly stationary linear wave equation cannot convert an input monochromatic frequency into a different output frequency. Inelastic scattering, time-dependent couplings, nonlinear interactions or a resolved bath is required to make the packet energy-loss ansatz into a coherent spectroscopic model. The ledger dE_bath=-dE_packet and dp_bath=-dp_packet is bookkeeping; it does not prove that such a physical bath exists or that it preserves image sharpness, line widths, polarization, blackbody spectra or background heat limits. The selected frame must be measured rather than hidden in gamma. A finite packet travel range is not an information horizon for the other sectors.

The new book extension derives two additional restrictions. First, a fixed-gap ray entering a region with higher characteristic C_f can avoid speed increase only if E_f/E_m <= 1/sqrt(1-(v_i/C_f)^2). For a near-C_i packet and C_f/C_i=sqrt(2), final energy is at most about sqrt(2) E_m. If E_m/E_i=1e-8, no more than about 1.4142e-8 of the original energy survives. This is an endpoint necessary condition, not a sufficient condition for monotonicity at every point. Second, transferring momentum to a set of bath excitations whose energy always costs at least u_min times net transferred momentum is impossible if u_min exceeds the packet's initial group speed. This follows from the derivative of the dispersion, not from a chosen drag coefficient. It excludes a particular fast-excitation-only bath, not media with recoil, low-phase-speed modes, or other interactions.

For moving endpoints and fixed launch momentum in one preferred frame, differentiating the arrival condition gives A=(v_a-v_s)/(v_a-v_r), on a regular first-arrival branch. Our normalized example has A=1.1145173, independently verified by finite differences. This is an additional mechanism for arrival stretching, but does not force A=1+z or solve the emitter's moving atomic phase-matching law. The book now makes that distinction explicit.

### Gravity, dark sector and observation plan (pp. 17–20)

The paper properly does not identify energy deposited by drag with observed dark matter. A credible inventory must account for stress, stability, spatial clustering, gravitational coupling, background radiation and heat. Mechanical inertia must be derived, including reaction momentum. Relational gravity needs independent predictions of lensing, orbital motion, Shapiro delay, clocks, binary dynamics and gravitational waves. It is not enough to describe curvature as a nonmaterial mathematical representation. The joint GW170817/GRB 170817A speed bound quoted in the paper is correct under the published interpretation; source emission delays and common-mode changes matter. The orientation-dependent laboratory result is not a blanket constraint on isotropic coordinate speed.

The observational matrix and minimum completion criteria are a strong organizing device. Improve it by requiring a frozen set of couplings before holdout tests, explicit uncertainty/selection models, raw observables rather than reusing distances inferred under a competing cosmology, and no per-source drag tuning. Include achromatic pulse timing, broadband spectra, differential time delays, optical focusing, atomic and nuclear clock ratios, and deposition constraints together. Keep an explicit null control for cosmic-bubble searches. The full 3D moving-charge and propagating longitudinal-field integration from the book remains a separate unfinished task.

### Conclusion and source lineage (pp. 20–22)

The conclusion correctly calls this a falsifiable research program. The uploaded paper alone does not include its cited executable companion files. Its source ledger is useful but needs precise cross-links and checksums to make every inherited value auditable. The independent implementation supplied here is not a claim to have rerun the original Part XXVIII script. Historical reports, Part XIX, and the new PDF remain byte-for-byte unchanged. No external submission or remote repository change is made.
